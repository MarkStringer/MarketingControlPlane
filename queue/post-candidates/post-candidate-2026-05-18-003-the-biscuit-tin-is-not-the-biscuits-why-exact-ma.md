## Draft

The biscuit tin is not the biscuits.
The spec is not the software.
The plan is not the project.

And yet we keep treating them as if they are.

Here's the problem with software delivery nobody talks about:

Most people — unconsciously, automatically — think in 'all/same/forever/exact match'.

All features. Same as the idea. Forever — no, actually RIGHT NOW. And it had better match exactly what I imagined.

This isn't stubbornness. It's how most human cognition works by default.

The moment someone pictures a finished product in their head, they treat it as if it already exists. The time, cost, and uncertainty of actually building it feels like a personal affront.

Prioritisation feels like loss.
Partial delivery feels like failure.
Change feels like betrayal.

None of this is the stakeholder being difficult. It's just exact-match fantasy running unchecked.

The fix isn't a better Gantt chart.
It isn't a longer requirements doc.
It's shifting to 'some/different/for now/inexact match' thinking.

Some features. Different from the original idea, but actually useful. Valuable for now, not forever. And close enough to be worth shipping.

An asymmetric project isn't a failed one.
It's a real one.
