## Draft

The moment your stakeholder imagined the project, it was already done in their head. The rest is just you catching up.

This isn't stubbornness. It's a very human mode of thinking — what the book I've been writing calls symmetrical thinking: all, same, forever, exact match.

When someone pictures something, they picture it whole. Finished. Exactly as they imagined it. Delivered immediately.

So when you say 'we need to prioritise,' they hear: 'we're going to give you less than the thing you already have in your head.'

When you say 'we need six more weeks,' they hear: 'the thing you already have is being taken away from you.'

This is why negotiation on software projects feels like loss — because for the stakeholder, it is loss. The complete version already existed. You're handing them something smaller.

The most useful thing I've found: stop arguing about the description and get something working in front of them as fast as possible.

Not because the working thing will match what they imagined. It won't. But it shifts them from imagining the whole biscuit tin to actually tasting a biscuit. And a real biscuit you can eat is worth more than a perfect tin you're still waiting for.

Delivering some value, for some people, in some timeframe — that's not a compromise. That's the job.
