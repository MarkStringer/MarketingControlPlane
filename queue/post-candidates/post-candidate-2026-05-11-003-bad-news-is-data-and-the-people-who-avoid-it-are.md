# Bad news is data — and the people who avoid it are the ones who need it most

## Hook

If your project is really happening, it will produce bad news. The only question is whether you hear it.

## Draft

If your project is really happening, it will produce bad news.

Not because something has gone wrong. Because reality never unfolds exactly as planned. Problems, surprises, and awkward discoveries are signs that the project is alive, not that it's failing.

The leaders who understand this treat bad news as data. It can reveal threats — and opportunities — that weren't visible from the room where the decisions get made.

But here's the hard part: the people who most need that data are often the ones who avoid it.

Because data is confusing. It's threatening. It may require uncomfortable decisions. It may mean admitting that the original plan was wrong, or that what seemed like a certainty was always a bet.

Hierarchy makes this worse. Bad news gets softened as it travels upward. The people at the top — the ones with the most power to act — often hear the most distorted version of what's actually happening.

The antidote isn't a dashboard. It's a trusted advisor and a real conversation.

Not a status report. A genuine discussion about the evidence coming out of the project — what it means, what it implies, what it demands.

Bad news is data. Treated well, it's the most useful thing your project produces.

How does bad news travel in your organisation?
