## Draft

Nobody on your project is seeing the same thing. They think they are. That's the problem.

I've been developing a show for Edinburgh Fringe about writing — and the central idea is this: there are no mirrors, only prisms. You can't record anything without transforming it. Every observation comes through a lens that bends it.

The same thing is true on every software project you've ever worked on.

The stakeholder who funded the idea is seeing a mirror of their original vision — shiny, intact, exactly as imagined. The developer is seeing a prism of technical constraints, dependencies, and things nobody told them. The user is seeing something else entirely: their actual problem, which may barely resemble what the team has been building.

None of them are wrong, exactly. They're just each looking through a different prism.

The mistake isn't having different points of view. The mistake is assuming you all have the same one.

Point of view is worth 80 IQ points. That's not a metaphor to motivate you — it's a navigational warning. If you can see what someone else can't see, you can help them. If you can't see what they're seeing, you'll talk past each other until something expensive goes wrong.

The job of good project management isn't to enforce a single view. It's to understand the prisms in the room — and work with all of them.
