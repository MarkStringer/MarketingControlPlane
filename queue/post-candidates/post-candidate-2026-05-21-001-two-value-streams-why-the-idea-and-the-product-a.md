## Draft

Every software project is actually two projects running at the same time — and they're fighting each other.

The first is the **idea value stream**. Somebody had a vision. They pitched it, got people excited, secured funding. The idea has momentum, champions, and emotional weight behind it.

The second is the **product value stream**. That's the messy, uncertain, user-shaped process of building something that actually works in the real world.

Here's the problem: the product almost always requires changing the idea. Users need something different from what was imagined. Reality doesn't match the pitch deck.

But the people who backed the idea feel threatened by that. They've staked credibility on it. Changing course feels like failure — even when it's the most rational thing to do.

This tension doesn't mean the project is broken. It means the project is real.

Successful delivery means guiding a team through that gap: between the dream that got funded and the product that can genuinely deliver value.

Most project breakdowns I've seen aren't technical failures. They're failures to navigate the gap between those two streams.

What does your organisation do when the product says 'we need to change the idea' — and the idea says 'absolutely not'?
