## Draft

Most software projects are born in conflict — and nobody mentions it at the kick-off meeting.

Here's why.

Before your project started, there was an idea. That idea travelled through meetings, slide decks, budget conversations, and someone's enthusiasm. It gathered political support. It got funded.

Not because it had been rigorously tested. Often because it was exciting, or because the right person believed in it, or because it solved someone's problem — not necessarily a user's problem.

That's the idea value stream.

Then your team starts building. And building means discovering. You talk to users. You find out the original idea doesn't quite match what people actually need. You have to change things.

That's the product value stream.

These two streams are different. They run at different speeds. They serve different masters. And the people who backed the idea may feel personally threatened when reality starts reshaping it.

Successful delivery isn't just a technical problem. It's guiding a project through the gap between dream and reality — and doing it in a way that doesn't destroy the relationships that funded it in the first place.

Most project managers are handed a conflict and called it a brief.

Understanding that early changes everything.
