# The Agile Manifesto Is a Piece of Asymmetrical Thinking. Most Companies Adopt It Symmetrically.

## Draft

The Agile Manifesto is a piece of asymmetrical thinking. Almost nobody reads it that way.

Look at the actual sentences. Individuals and interactions *over* processes and tools. Working software *over* comprehensive documentation. Responding to change *over* following a plan.

Over. Not instead of. Not "the right-hand side is rubbish." Just: when you have to lean, lean this way.

That little word is doing all the work. It refuses to say all, or always, or forever. It favours one thing and leaves the other one standing. It is asymmetry, written down.

Here's the thing. Most of us don't think like that by default.

By default we think in all, same, forever, exact match. All the features. Exactly like the idea. Now, not later. And right forever once it's done. Ignacio Matte Blanco called the machinery underneath this symmetrical thinking: the bit of your mind that treats different things as if they were the same.

So watch what happens when an organisation that runs on "all, same, forever" picks up a manifesto built out of "this over that, for now."

It adopts it symmetrically.

Everyone does all of it. By the book. The same way in every team. Forever, because we've Transformed now. Two-week sprints become law. The stand-up becomes a status meeting with worse chairs. Story points get audited. The framework that was supposed to inject asymmetry gets installed as one more rigid, exact-match process.

And then everyone is confused about why the agile transformation feels exactly like the thing it replaced.

I bet you've been in that room.

The fix is not "be asymmetrical instead." That is the same mistake pointing the other way. Matte Blanco's word for the real skill is bi-logic: holding both at once and knowing which one the moment in front of you actually needs. Some all (we will always test). Some some (we'll ship this slice and learn). The expert joins them up. The amateur picks a side and defends it.

A project lives in the gap between the idea and the thing. Symmetrical thinking pretends there is no gap. Asymmetrical thinking does its work inside it.

Agile was never a set of rules. It was a way of leaning.
