# The Two Streams: Why Your Idea and Your Product Are Already at War

## Hook

Every software project is running two projects at once. Most teams only know about one of them.

## Draft

Every software project is running two projects at once. Most teams only know about one of them.

The first is the one everyone talks about: building the product. The second is the one nobody names: the idea that got the project funded in the first place.

These two streams don't naturally flow in the same direction.

The idea stream is weird. Ideas get funded for emotional and political reasons — not because they've been tested. Someone influential believed in them, or championed them, or tied their identity to them. The idea arrives with momentum it doesn't necessarily deserve.

The product stream is where reality lives. Users don't care about the idea. They care about whether the software actually helps them. Which means the team almost always has to change the original idea to build something genuinely useful.

And that's where the trouble starts.

The people who backed the idea feel threatened. Changing it feels like failure. So teams hide the gap for as long as they can — and the longer they hide it, the worse the collision when reality finally arrives.

Successful delivery means guiding the project through that difficult gap between dream and reality. Not pretending it doesn't exist. Not fighting it. Understanding it — and working with it.

Most project problems aren't technical. They're navigational. You're trying to close the distance between what was imagined and what will actually be useful.

That's the job.
