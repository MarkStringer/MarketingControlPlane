## Draft

Nobody on your project is seeing the same thing. Not because they're being difficult — because nobody can.

I've been developing a show about writing, and there's a line in it that keeps coming back to me when I think about software delivery:

*There are no mirrors, only prisms.*

A mirror would show you reality as it is. A prism bends the light and splits it — every person looking through their own prism sees a different spectrum.

We treat this as a problem. We write specs, hold alignment meetings, draw diagrams, build slide decks — all in the hope that we can force everyone to see the same thing at the same time.

But the prism isn't a bug. It's what everyone has.

The boss sees the original idea, the commitment, the thing that was promised.
The developer sees the constraints, the technical debt, the gap between the diagram and the codebase.
The user sees a workflow that doesn't match how they actually work.
The product manager sees all three and none of them clearly.

Point of view is worth 80 IQ points. That's not just an argument for empathy — it's a practical delivery argument.

The project teams that deliver well are the ones who stop trying to eliminate the prisms and start working with them: user research that takes distortion seriously, working software that makes the gaps visible, and honest conversations that don't pretend everyone is looking at the same thing.

The distortions are different from anybody else's. That's not a flaw. It's the data.
