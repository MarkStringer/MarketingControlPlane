## Wednesday 26th August 2026

## One person came

Monday's show had an audience of one.

If it had an audience of zero, I would have still done it.

The single audience member — an Australian lady, I think — offered me a way out. "Did you want me to go away and you can go home and have a night off?"

No, I said. That's absolutely fine. If you're fine with it, then I'm absolutely fine.

So we did the whole thing.

The way the show works is that the audience picks cards and the cards decide what I talk about, and in what order. Even though there was only two of us, we still picked cards.

I explained that there's audience participation in the show. It says so in the small print. Nobody reads the small print. This audience member hadn't — she'd come in straight from another show. She'd looked up to see what was on next, seen the title and thought "that sounds good."

About fifteen minutes in, everybody gets a notebook and a pen and two minutes to write something. Two minutes is a long time in a room with one other person in it. Silent. Writing. I write as well, so at least there are two of us not looking up.

Then later what we do is we read back what we've written and then we change it. That's the second exercise, and it's the one I find hardest. And it turned out the person sitting in front of me was better at it than I am. She drafts something, leaves it, doesn't try to make it perfect, moves on, finishes the draft, then goes back.

Which is the thing I have spent nine years just about managing to teach myself.

Somewhere in the middle of the show I explain how I got my first Fringe slot, in 2023. A friend caught COVID and gave me his audience! That time I gave them a way out. They were expecting a show of topical comedy — I was offering them project management! Everybody stayed!

I end my show with heckles. I hand out cards with heckles written on them so people can shout them at me. Boo! Why should I listen to you? Writing is just content for AI to steal! There's a limit to how many of those you can hand out to one person, so I shouted some of them at myself on this particular night!

And then it was over, and I thanked her for staying.

I said I hoped it had been worthwhile with just the one person in the audience.

She said it was extremely enjoyable.

Everybody in that room wrote something. They read it back, changed it, and talked about it with a stranger. That's the entire show.

So you could say it was a hundred per cent success.
