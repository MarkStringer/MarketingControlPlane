# Your project has two value streams — and they are fighting each other

## Hook

## Draft

Every software project has two value streams. One of them is trying to kill the other.

The first stream is the idea stream. It's how the idea got funded — the pitch, the enthusiasm, the political backing, the story that made someone say yes. That stream is real. It did important work.

The second stream is the product stream. It's the process of actually figuring out what users need and building something that delivers it.

Here's the problem: they conflict.

The product stream nearly always requires changing the original idea. You talk to users. You build something. You learn that the story you told to get funded wasn't quite right. So you adapt.

But the people who backed the idea — who championed it, whose reputation is tied to it — feel threatened by every change. From their perspective, you're not adapting. You're attacking.

This is not a failure of communication. It's structural. The two streams have different goals.

Successful project management means holding both streams at once: respecting what the idea stream made possible while letting the product stream do its job — which is discovering what's actually useful and building that instead.

Most project failure I've seen isn't technical. It's this collision, playing out invisibly, until the project is too far in to fix.

What does that look like in your team?
