## Draft

Elisabeth Kübler-Ross wrote about the stages of grief. It turns out they're also the stages of not writing your book.

Denial: I'm busy. I'm getting married, having children, just been diagnosed. There's always another passage. The quiet moment never quite arrives.

Anger: The frustrated artist who only gets out sideways — drunk, dancing on the table, buried again the next morning. My book ranks 60,000th on Amazon. Which means it exists. That's further than most people get.

Bargaining: Buying guitars. Expensive notebooks. Writers retreats. Creative writing courses. All of it is running. The hound catches you anyway.

Depression: You've run out of excuses. Money — neither do most writers. Time — 20 minutes a day is enough. Knowledge — read, or make it up. You've stopped running and still haven't started.

Acceptance: Carry water, chop wood. Write something. Read it. Change it. Show it to someone. Keep writing. The arrow releases itself. But only if you keep drawing the bow.

The show I'm developing for Edinburgh Fringe this August is built around exactly this structure. Because I think most people are stuck somewhere between Bargaining and Depression — and that's the most useful place to have an honest conversation about making things.

Where are you?
