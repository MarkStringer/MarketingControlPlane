## Wednesday 26th August 2026

## The mother of all sulks is the reason I'm doing the show

I was walking along the street in Edinburgh on my way back from doing the show — and I had a queasy realisation.

I'm trying to write a mystery story. I've got about 35,000 words. And now I'm kind of stuck.

I'm stuck with writing my mystery story. And I'm doing a show about being stuck with my previous book.

I kid you not, I hadn't understood that these two things might be connected.

Here's the story of the sulk that I talk about in my show.

Back in the olden times, before the pandemic, people used to commute to work. I had a particularly ferocious commute — an hour and three quarters each way. I lived in Limehouse in East London and the job was in Uxbridge, which meant I spent a large part of my life on the Metropolitan Line.

So in May 2016 I decided I was going to write a thousand words every morning for the whole month. I'd read all the books that say it's very important that you write every day. And so that's what I decided to do.

And I did it. At the end of May I had 20,000 words.

And then I had no idea what to do. Just no idea at all. I didn't have a book. I had a thing.

What I realise now is that I'd fallen for a Hollywood idea of what writing a book is. In the films somebody sits down at an old-fashioned typewriter and types frantically, hands flying in the air. Then there's a montage. The seasons change around them and the manuscript on the table gets higher and higher. And at the end of it, in the movies, they've written the book. It's done!

Naively, I thought that's how it worked.

So when I got to 20,000 words and it wasn't a fully formed book, I didn't know what to do next.

So I sulked. For SIX YEARS!

I didn't really go near it again in that time. And I only found out that's what had happened by going through my diaries, which were all in one place. This is another tip that I talk about in the show — put all your writing in one place. Because I managed to follow that great piece of advice, I could read back through them. I was all enthused about writing this book.

And then I wasn't.

This is the main message of my show — you can get stuck. In a way it's fine to get stuck — it's very understandable. But if you actually want to write a book, rather than just write a few things, then a lot of what you're doing is figuring out a way to get past that and not be stuck.

Which brings me back to the street in Edinburgh.

The one thing I found that really helped, and I've not heard many people say this, was talking about it. In 2023 I came to the Fringe without a venue and pretty much without a show. So, I gave the talk on the Meadows — the big park south of Edinburgh centre — instead. I did the "show" two or three times an afternoon, for about a week. Sometimes friends came. Sometimes nobody came. But that's where I started to sort the book out in my head.

And I'm starting to think I'm doing the same thing again. But rather than just sulk, I'm talking about my stuck-ness — in a venue, a poster and a run of tickets.

I only worked that out midway through the run.

Which you might think is a bit late in the proceedings. But it isn't six years.
