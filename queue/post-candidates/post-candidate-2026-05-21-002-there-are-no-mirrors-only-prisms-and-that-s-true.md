# There Are No Mirrors — Only Prisms (And That's True for Your Stakeholders Too)

## Draft

I've been developing a show for the Edinburgh Fringe called *You Can Write a Book*. One of the ideas at its heart is this: there are no mirrors, only prisms.

A mirror gives you back what you put in. A prism transforms it. White light goes in; a spectrum comes out.

Nobody — no person, no team, no organisation — is a mirror. We are all prisms. We don't reflect the world; we refract it. Our backgrounds, our experiences, our fears, and our interests bend what we see into something slightly different from what anyone else sees.

I've been thinking about what that means for software projects.

When a project kicks off, every person in the room is looking at a different version of the same idea. The sponsor sees the business case. The engineer sees the technical constraints. The user sees the gap between what was promised and what they actually need. The project manager sees all three, and also sees that none of them fully sees the others.

We usually call this a 'communication problem'. But it isn't, really. It's just prisms doing what prisms do.

The book I've written — *Delivering the Impossible* — argues that 'point of view is worth 80 IQ points.' The person who learns to work with divergent viewpoints, rather than trying to eliminate them, is the one who actually delivers.

So next time your stakeholders can't agree on what the project means, don't reach for a better slide deck. Ask instead: what is each person's prism showing them that the others can't see?

The spectrum is the signal. Not the noise.
