Two things on that list cannot both be true, and it is worth naming which one wins.

Protecting BAU is essential. Also, open and honest conversations early.

If BAU is protected as a matter of policy, then when the resource runs short the project absorbs all of it. That is not a risk. It is a decision, and it was taken before anyone sat down to be open and honest. The variance has to land somewhere, and you have said in advance where.

So the honest conversation cannot really be about whether the project slips. That was settled at the top of the list. It can only be about who says so, and how early they are willing to.

Which brings me to the gatekeeper line, because I think it is doing something the panel did not intend. A gate is a filter. You cannot be the mechanism that decides what gets through and also the guarantee that things get raised early. People learn what a gate lets through, and they learn it much faster than anybody writes a policy about it. If the same person applies the pressure and receives the issues, then the price of raising one is set by the people who most need it raised.

Bad news is data. But data only moves when the person holding it is not also the person who pays for handing it over.

The useful version of this is duller than a panel. Say out loud that BAU is protected, which means the project timeline is the shock absorber, and then replan the project against that. You will get a later date. It will also be a date somebody can still defend in twelve months, which the current one is not.

Deliver the possible, not the fantasy. The fantasy here is not the deadline. It is the idea that you can protect BAU, hold the date, and close the gap with candour.
