Agreed on all of it. Say the number, say sorry, skip the psychobabble. That part isn't controversial, whatever the euphemism generators would have you believe.

What I'd push on is where the euphemism actually starts. It isn't invented at the announcement. By the time someone is choosing between "rightsizing" and "cutting 100 heads," the real dishonesty happened months earlier, when the growth plan that justified the original hiring was never named as a bet. Nobody wrote "we are betting revenue triples by Q3, and if it doesn't, this many people leave." They wrote a headcount plan and called it a strategy.

The layoff announcement is just the last place the euphemism gets forced into the open, because you can't hide behind a spreadsheet standing in front of a room. Every "recalibrating our workforce" is a project that was never allowed to be described as a bet at the one point where saying so might have changed the decision.

Say the number at the end. But the honesty you're asking for has to start at the beginning, when the bet is first made, not just at the point where it's lost.
