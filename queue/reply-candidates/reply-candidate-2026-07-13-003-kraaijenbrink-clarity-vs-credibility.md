You're right that blaming the resisters is lazy. I'd just watch the sentence that usually replaces it, which is "they didn't understand the strategy." That one is comforting because it keeps the whole problem inside the communications team. Explain it better, add a town hall, run it through the deck one more time, and the resistance is supposed to evaporate.

Sometimes it does. But plenty of resistance survives perfect clarity. People understood the strategy fine. They priced the bet, decided it doesn't pay, and declined to pretend otherwise. That isn't a comprehension gap. It's a disagreement, and it tends to come from the people closest to the work, the ones who watched the last three versions of this land badly.

Clarity is not the scarce ingredient. Credibility is.

So before concluding your people didn't get it, it's worth asking whether they got it completely and simply don't buy it. Point of view is worth 80 IQ points, and the view from the floor has usually seen this film before.
