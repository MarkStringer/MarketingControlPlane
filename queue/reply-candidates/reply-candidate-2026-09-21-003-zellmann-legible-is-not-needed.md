Turning that grant down was the right call, and it's worth naming what actually happened when you did.

The funder wasn't betting on the circular economy working. They were betting on being able to show, in their own report, that they funded something. Workshops and study visits produce evidence a funder can point to. A truck sitting in a depot producing nothing measurable for eighteen months doesn't, even when the truck is the thing that makes the whole system function.

That's not donors being cynical. It's what happens when the person placing the bet and the person who has to make the bet pay off are different people with different scorecards. The funder's risk is looking bad in their own report. Your risk is a training programme with no way to act on what it taught. Those are not the same bet, even though they're both called "funding the project."

Deliver the possible, not the fantasy, applies to funders as much as it applies to delivery teams. The fantasy here is that transformation scales on capacity-building alone. The possible thing, the truck and the salary, doesn't fit the category that gets a donor a clean line in their annual report, so it doesn't get funded, and that's a design choice, not an accident.
