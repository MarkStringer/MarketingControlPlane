Before asking whether you're building velocity, ask whether you actually have a clearly defined end goal.

Most projects don't. They have an approved estimate and a scope document that nobody fully read. What counts as the "end goal" gets renegotiated throughout delivery — scope arrives from above, priorities shift, the thing that mattered at kickoff turns out to matter less now than the thing nobody mentioned at kickoff.

Velocity toward a fixed point is a useful concept. In practice, the point keeps moving. What you find out mid-project is whether the team can tell the difference between moving quickly and moving toward the right thing — and in most organisations, those get rewarded identically.

All projects are swamps. Swamps don't have clearly defined edges.
