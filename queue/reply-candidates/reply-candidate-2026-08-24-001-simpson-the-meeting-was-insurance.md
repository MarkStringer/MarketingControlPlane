The audit is the interesting bit here. Four days out of thirty producing tangible progress sounds damning, until you ask what the other twenty six were actually for.

A status meeting isn't a failed decision meeting. It's a working piece of machinery doing a different job. It spreads knowledge of a problem across enough people that nobody has to carry it alone. That isn't waste. That's insurance.

Which is why deleting them so often doesn't give the time back. The anxiety that filled the calendar has to go somewhere, and it tends to return as one to ones, or a channel nobody is allowed to mute, or a monthly review with forty slides.

Your CEO got twelve hours back because he had the authority to decide things on his own. Most people staring at a full calendar are booking those meetings precisely because they don't.

Redesign the rhythm and you get a tidier calendar. Change who is allowed to decide without a room full of witnesses and you get the twelve hours.
