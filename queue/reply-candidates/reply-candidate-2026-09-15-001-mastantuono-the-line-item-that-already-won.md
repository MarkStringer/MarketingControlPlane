Bringing people into the room before the money moves is right. What's missing is what happens to the trade-off once the room agrees.

A trade-off that got argued over stays contestable in one direction only. The four million that lost keeps getting revisited, because someone remembers losing it and has a reason to bring it back up. The ten million that won just becomes the baseline. Nobody schedules a meeting to ask whether it's still winning.

That isn't a communication gap. It's what happens to a bet once everyone stops treating it like one. All projects are swamps, and an approved line item doesn't get less swampy just because it already survived one meeting.

Making the trade-off visible before the money moves is good discipline. Making it visible again once the money's spent and underperforming, when nobody wants to be the one who reopens it, is the harder job, and the one that actually protects the business.
