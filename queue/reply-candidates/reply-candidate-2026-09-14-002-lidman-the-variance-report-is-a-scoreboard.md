Numbers versus story is the right distinction, but it's still a story told after the fact.

By the time FP&A is mapping an overage against growth initiatives, the money is already spent and someone has already decided which projects get to be called high-ROI. That's not analysis. It's defense with better footnotes.

The real fix sits upstream. If a project is approved as a bet, on record, before the spend happens, 12% over doesn't need investigating. It's just the bet paying out slower or costing more than expected, which bets do sometimes.

Great FP&A shouldn't be the department that explains the overrun well. It should be the department that made sure everyone already knew, going in, which numbers were commitments and which were bets. Then the variance report isn't a confession. It's a scoreboard.
