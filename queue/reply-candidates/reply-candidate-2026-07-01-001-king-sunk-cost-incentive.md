The sunk cost story is the polite version. It says people keep going because they can't do the maths on money that's already gone. Most of them can. That isn't what keeps a dead project walking.

A project is a bet. Killing it means someone has to stand up in a room full of their bosses and say the bet lost. Nobody gets promoted for that. So the thing carries on, not because anyone still believes in it, but because the one person who could stop it would personally pay for stopping it.

Reevaluation frameworks don't touch this. You can run the cleanest quarterly review in the world and it still dies on contact with the fact that the honest answer is career-limiting. The problem was never the analysis. It was that bad news is data nobody is rewarded for delivering.

Fix the incentive to kill things early and you won't need the framework. Leave the incentive alone and the framework just becomes one more green report on the way down.
