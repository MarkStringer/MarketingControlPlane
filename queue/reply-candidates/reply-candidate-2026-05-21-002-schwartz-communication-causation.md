The 56% figure is worth interrogating. PMI collects it by asking teams why their projects failed, after the fact. People who've just watched a project collapse aren't objective witnesses to its causes.

Communication failures are usually visible at the end. The structural problems that caused them arrived much earlier: impossible timelines, unclear sponsors, competing priorities that nobody wanted to resolve. Those are harder to admit to a surveyor.

"We communicated badly" is a safer answer than "the project shouldn't have been approved." Both might be true. Only one requires anyone to accept responsibility.
