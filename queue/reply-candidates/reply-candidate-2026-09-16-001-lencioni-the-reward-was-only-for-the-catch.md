Lencioni is right about the mechanics. Smoke ignored becomes fire, and everyone stops trusting the
person who looked away. But the discomfort isn't the real obstacle.

Look at what actually gets rewarded. The leader who puts out a visible fire gets credit for it
happening in public. The leader who investigates a wisp of smoke and finds nothing gets nothing, not
even a thank you, because from the outside it looks like time spent on a problem that was never
there. Chasing smoke is a bet with an invisible payoff if you're right and a public cost if you're
seen doing it for nothing.

Bad news is data, but only in organisations that pay for the checking, not just the catching. Ask
leaders to run toward every wisp of smoke and you are asking them to absorb a private cost for a
benefit the business rarely sees. Fix the reward first. The courage follows the incentive, not the
other way round.
