Pushing back on unrealistic timelines is good advice. But "push back" treats PM courage as the limiting factor. And usually it isn't.

The person setting the unrealistic timeline typically has more organisational power than the PM objecting to it. The PM pushes back, gets overruled, and then gets blamed when the timeline slips. What's been demonstrated is not that they needed more backbone, but that backbone alone doesn't move the constraint.

Most bad timelines survive not because no one said anything, but because the people saying it were in the wrong position in the hierarchy to be heard.

The structural problem is that the PM owns the schedule but doesn't own the resources or the scope. Bravery doesn't close that gap. What closes it — sometimes — is making the gap visible to someone who does own both. That's a different skill from saying no in a meeting.
