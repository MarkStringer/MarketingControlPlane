Judgment wasn't abundant before AI either.

Jeremy's framing — execution gets cheaper, judgment gets scarcer — is right about the direction. What AI is doing to routine execution is real, and it genuinely shifts what's valuable.

But the judgment bottleneck isn't new, and it isn't primarily a skills problem.

Project managers, delivery leads, and programme directors have been making judgment calls for decades. The ones who got promoted weren't always the ones with the best judgment. They were often the ones who'd learned to deploy it carefully — which usually meant telling people what they wanted to hear while quietly trying to fix things.

If organisations continue to punish honest assessment and reward confident optimism, automating execution won't change anything. You'll just have cheaper optimism generators.

Judgment becomes a scarce resource when you decide to reward it.
