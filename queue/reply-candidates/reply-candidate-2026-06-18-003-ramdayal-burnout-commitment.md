The credit-to-team, blame-to-PM pattern isn't unfair recognition. Given how the role is usually set up, it's structurally correct.

The PM is the person who took the commitment on behalf of the organisation. When the project succeeds, it succeeds because the team did the work. When it fails, it fails because the commitment was wrong — the deadline, the scope, the resourcing, or some combination of the three. The PM is the one who agreed to it.

Before asking how to fix the recognition problem, it's worth asking: were the commitments this PM was making ever achievable? If the deadline was dictated from above, scope was added monthly, and the team was half the size needed — burnout isn't a wellbeing problem. It's the expected output of a structurally impossible job.
