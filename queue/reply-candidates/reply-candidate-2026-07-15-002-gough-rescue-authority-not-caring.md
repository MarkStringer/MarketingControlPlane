You are right that templates don't rescue projects. But "you have to be arsed" is only half of it, and it is the half that gets good people burned.

Caring is what makes you walk onto the troubled project. It is not what turns it around. What turns it around is someone finally being allowed to change the thing that was broken. The scope, the date, the promise made before anyone had looked at the ground. Rescue work is mostly the slow unwinding of a bet that plenty of people could see was bad and nobody had the authority to call.

I have watched people ask the difficult questions, lead through the discomfort, and care right up to the edge of their own health, and still lose, because the answer to every difficult question was "that's already been decided." Deliver the possible, not the fantasy. The rescuer's real job is getting someone senior to admit which fantasy they are still paying for.
