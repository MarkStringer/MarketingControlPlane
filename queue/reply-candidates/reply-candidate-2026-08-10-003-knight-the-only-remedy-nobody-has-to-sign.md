All six of these are real, and I would put them in a different order. Not by size. By who has to sign something.

Look at what each one actually is. The technical debt was taken on to hit a date somebody promised. The cross cutting dependencies are the org chart, drawn by someone, on purpose. The context switching is what happens when nobody upstream is willing to say no to anything. The batch size is downstream of a release process that has a name on it somewhere.

Every item on your list is the residue of a decision made above the people being asked to work harder.

Which means fixing any of them requires somebody senior to unmake a decision they made, announced, and then defended in a meeting. That is expensive, and it is expensive for a specific person.

And then there is the seventh option, which is the one in your title. Work harder. It costs nothing. It requires no budget, no replan, no reorg, and above all nobody has to say they were wrong about anything.

That is why it keeps coming back. Not because people have never heard of technical debt. Because it is the only explanation of the delay that leaves every existing decision standing.

There is a second reason it is so hard to shift, which is that it cannot be tested.

If the team delivers, effort worked. If the team misses, they did not try hard enough. There is no result that counts against the theory. People are not usually attached to it because they are stupid. They are attached to it because it is the only account of the situation that is guaranteed to survive the outcome.

Point of view is worth 80 IQ points. Swap "are they working hard enough" for "how many things is this team allowed to be doing at once, and who added the last one". Same delay, same team, same fortnight. Suddenly the answer is a number, and the number has a person attached to it.

That is precisely why the swap does not get made. The first question has no owner. The second one does.

So I would be careful about arguing that the engineers are working hard, true as it is. It accepts that effort was the subject. It never was. The subject is which explanation of the delay is cheapest for the person doing the explaining, and shouting is only rarely the solution because it was never meant to be one.
