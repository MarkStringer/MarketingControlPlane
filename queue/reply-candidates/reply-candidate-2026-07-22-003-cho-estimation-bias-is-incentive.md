Good list. But I'd be careful calling these biases.

A bias is a bug in one head. Optimism bias, planning fallacy, student syndrome. You can name them all day, but they show up reliably in every organisation, which is a strong hint they aren't accidents of psychology.

The optimistic estimate is the one that gets funded. The honest estimate gets you a reputation as negative and, often, gets you taken off the project. So people produce the number that survives the room, not the number they believe. That isn't a cognitive error. It's a rational response to the incentive.

Fix the incentive and most of the "bias" evaporates. Leave it in place and no amount of debiasing training will help, because you'd be treating a symptom the system keeps regenerating.

Deliver the possible, not the fantasy. But first stop punishing the people who tell you which is which.
