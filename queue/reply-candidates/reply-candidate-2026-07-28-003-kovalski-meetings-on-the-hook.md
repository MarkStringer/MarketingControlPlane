Forcing function is the right phrase. I am not sure it is forcing what you think it is.

The three things on your list, alignment and nuance and brainstorming, are all about moving information around. If that were the whole story then the async people would win the argument, because documents move information around better than meetings do. They are searchable, they do not need six diaries to agree with each other, and nobody has to pretend to be paying attention.

What a meeting does that a document cannot is put a person on the hook in front of witnesses.

Someone has to say the date out loud. Someone else has to be seen not objecting to it. That is a completely different mechanism from communication, and it explains both halves of your argument. The meetings that earn their place are the ones where a named human commits to something. The meetings that make people want to abolish meetings are the ones where fourteen people confirm that things are broadly on track.

The other thing you only get in the room is the stuff nobody has said. The pause before someone answers. The person who agrees to a date while looking at the floor. You cannot get that over Slack, and you certainly cannot get it from a status document, because a status document is written by somebody who has had time to decide what they want you to know.

So I would swap out the test. Not "does this need discussion", because the honest answer to that is nearly always arguably yes. Better: does something need to become true in public. If nothing does, send the document.

The rest of the calendar is theatre.
