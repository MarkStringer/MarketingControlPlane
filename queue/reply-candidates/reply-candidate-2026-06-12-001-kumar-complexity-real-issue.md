Calling complexity "created" is only possible from the other side of it.

Before you've solved a problem, complexity is information. Demand simplicity too early and you're not clarifying — you're assuming. The impulse to cut through the noise and find the real issue is healthy. But it only works when you already know which is which.

All projects are swamps. You don't choose how complicated the terrain is. You find out by moving through it. The project that looks complicated and turns out to have a simple answer is manageable. The one that looks simple and turns out genuinely complex is the one that damages people, because they stopped looking for complications before they found them.

Simplifying a problem you don't yet understand is just a more confident way of being wrong.
