There's a comfort in the five-causes list. It says failure is a hygiene problem. Tighten the comms, sharpen the goals, watch the risks, and you'll be fine.

Some of it is hygiene. But a project is a bet, and some bets lose however clean your five boxes are. You can communicate flawlessly about a thing that was never going to work. All projects are swamps, and a tidy plan does not drain them.

The quiet danger in the preventable-failure framing is what it does to people. If failure is always somebody's hygiene lapse, then admitting a project is in trouble becomes an admission of personal incompetence. So nobody admits it. The bad news stops moving. And bad news is data, so you have just blinded yourself at exactly the moment you needed to see.

Treat some failure as the price of taking real bets and you make it safe to report trouble early. Treat all of it as preventable and you teach everyone to hide it.
