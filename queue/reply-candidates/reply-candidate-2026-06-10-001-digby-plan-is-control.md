The plan isn't treated as reality by accident.

The project manager typically has no authority over the team, the budget decisions, or what the organisation prioritises. They own the plan. That's it. Of course they defend it — it's the only artefact that's actually theirs.

The role is structured to separate accountability from authority. The PM is responsible for outcomes they don't control. Treating the plan as reality isn't a cognitive mistake. It's a rational response to that situation.

When the plan diverges from reality — and it always does — the PM faces a choice: update the plan and explain why it slipped, or defend it and hope reality catches up. Organisations that punish bad news make the second option more attractive. You fix that in the culture, not by reminding PMs that Gantt charts aren't real.
