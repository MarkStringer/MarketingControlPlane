The planning fallacy isn't the cause. It's what you call it after.

At the bid stage, giving a realistic estimate — three months when the client wants one — often means you don't get the work. The team that bids optimistically wins the project. The system selects for optimistic estimates, then calls the outcome a cognitive bias.

This matters because 'planning fallacy' locates the problem in people's heads, where training can fix it. The actual problem is structural. When realistic estimates win as often as optimistic ones, the bias largely disappears.

Projects are late because of how the work was won, not because of what people were thinking when they wrote the estimate.
