This is project reporting's oldest disease, just wearing a legal coat.

A report that flags every theoretical risk isn't communication. It's an alibi. It exists so that when something blows up, the author can point at row 34 and say "I raised it." That's a rational thing to build when the finger gets pointed at you for missing a risk, and nothing bad happens to you for burying the two that mattered under thirty that didn't.

Bad news is data, but only if it reaches someone who can act on it while acting is still cheap. A forty-item register handed to a busy stakeholder is data going straight into a drain. The length isn't laziness. It's self-protection, and self-protection and communication pull in opposite directions.

You don't fix it with a shorter template. You fix it by making it safe to write "these two things, everything else is noise," so the person carrying the risk isn't punished for having a point of view. Point of view is worth 80 IQ points, but only where it's allowed to exist.
