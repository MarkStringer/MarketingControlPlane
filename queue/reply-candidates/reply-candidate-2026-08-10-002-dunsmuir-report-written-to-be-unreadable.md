Your three reasons are all true, and I think they are symptoms rather than causes.

Take the last one first. Documents rather than conversations. Ask why the document reads the way it does. Communication could have been improved. Stakeholder engagement should have started earlier. Nobody wrote that sentence because they could not think of a better one. They wrote it because it is the only version that does not name anybody.

Here is the thing about writing a lesson down. Once it is in the file, it is available. And the next project that walks into the same wall is no longer unlucky. It is negligent, and there is a document proving somebody could have known.

So the report gets drafted in a register that cannot convict anyone, and then it goes unread, and the two facts are the same fact. People are not failing to read it. They already know what is in it, because they know what was allowed to go in it.

Then there is the lesson that never appears at all.

Every project is a bet. Lessons learned reviews only ever ask how well we played the hand. They do not ask whether we should have been at the table, because the people who put us there are the people who commissioned the review, and they are usually still in post.

Which means the whole exercise is scoped to execution by default. That is not an accident of timing. It is the one boundary nobody has to be told about.

On building reflection in throughout, I think that is harder than you are making it sound, not easier.

At the end of a project the finding is embarrassing. Halfway through, the same finding is a threat to something people are currently being paid to defend, with a business case still open and a sponsor who has said the words in public. Bad news is data, but only where the person carrying it does not get charged for the delivery. Moving the conversation earlier moves it to where the charge is higher.

To answer your question. Yes, twice that I can actually point at, and the document was not what did it. In both cases somebody with the authority to change how the work was done had personally been burned by the thing, and the review gave them a piece of paper to wave. The learning was already sitting in the room. The report was ammunition for someone who already wanted to fire.

So I would not spend the effort on the format. I would spend it on making it survivable to write the true sentence, with a name in it, while the project is still running.

An organisation that cannot do that does not have a lessons learned problem. It has a perfectly functioning system for not finding out.
