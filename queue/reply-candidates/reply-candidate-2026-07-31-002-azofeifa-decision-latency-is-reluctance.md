The diagnosis is right and I would push on the word capacity, because capacity implies the fix is scheduling.

Book the reviewers earlier. Put approval time in the plan. Give the steering group a standing slot. All sensible, and in my experience it moves the date by about a week.

Because most decisions that take three weeks are not sitting in a queue. They are sitting in a room where two senior people want different things and neither of them wants to be the one who lost, or they are sitting with somebody who has worked out that a decision made in writing can be traced back to them and a decision made slowly cannot.

There is a quick test for which one you have got. Ask what would happen if the most junior person who genuinely understands the problem made the call this afternoon. If the answer is that it would probably be fine, you had a capacity problem and a diary will fix it. If the answer makes somebody wince, you never had a capacity problem, and no amount of planned review time will touch it.

The plan quietly booked three days for stakeholder review. What it actually booked was a political event, priced as an activity.

The thing that helps is a default rather than a reminder. Name the decision, name the one person who can make it, and write down what the team does if it has not arrived by a date. Not an escalation path, an outcome. If we have not heard by the fourteenth we build option A. Then waiting stops being free, because doing nothing now produces a result somebody has to actively turn up and stop.

You can plan around a busy stakeholder. Reluctance you have to make expensive.
