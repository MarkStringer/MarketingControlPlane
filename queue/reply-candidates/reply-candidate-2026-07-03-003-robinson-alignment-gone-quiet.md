Agreed that alignment beats micromanaging the timeline. The thing worth watching is what alignment actually means on the ground.

Often it means the disagreement went quiet, not that it went away. Real stakeholders have real conflicts of interest. When everyone appears to be working toward identical goals, the more likely explanation is that someone has stopped saying what they actually want, usually the person with the least power in the room.

You don't need consensus. You need the disagreements visible and on the table early, while they are still cheap to deal with. Point of view is worth 80 IQ points, and the point of view you most need is the one from the stakeholder who keeps making the meeting awkward. Alignment that has gone quiet isn't alignment. It's a bill you pay later.
