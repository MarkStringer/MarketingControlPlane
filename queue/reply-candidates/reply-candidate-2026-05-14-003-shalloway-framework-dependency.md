The diagnosis is sharp but the prescription makes the same mistake as the original complaint.

If organisations are damaged by treating Scrum as the only viable option, offering Amplio as the correct replacement restores exactly the belief being critiqued: there's a right framework, you've just been using the wrong one.

The underlying damage isn't framework monoculture. It's the belief that a framework can substitute for judgment about a specific project, with specific constraints, in a specific context. That belief is what makes organisations reach for a guide when what they need is a question: do we understand what we're building, for whom, with what resources and what time?

Any framework that helps you ask that question is useful. Any framework that promises to answer it is selling something the situation can't deliver.
