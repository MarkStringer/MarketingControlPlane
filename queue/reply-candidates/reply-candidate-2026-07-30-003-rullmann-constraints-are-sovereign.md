There is one sentence in here doing a lot of quiet damage. Time, money and scope are real, but they are not sovereign.

They are sovereign. That is the entire thing that makes them constraints. The date does not care how coherent you are.

I take the point that the triangle is thin. Everybody has known it is thin for about sixty years, and it keeps surviving anyway, and that is worth being curious about rather than annoyed by. It survives because it is falsifiable. You can be caught out by it. On the morning when somebody has to choose between the date and the scope, the triangle is the only item on your list of four that turns up and forces the choice.

Goal, value and norm are not hard to have. Projects have them in industrial quantities at kickoff. There are slides. The trouble is that none of the three can be checked by anyone, which means none of them can ever lose an argument, which means all three are gone by the first genuinely painful trade-off and nobody notices they left.

The other problem with coherence is that it is a description of a project that is going well rather than a way of making one go well. Goal, value, norm and constraint line up beautifully on projects that are already succeeding for entirely other reasons. That is an outcome wearing the costume of an input.

All projects are swamps. The reason people cling to the triangle while they are in the swamp is not that they think it explains the project. It is that it is the only instrument they own that still returns an answer once everyone in the room has stopped agreeing with each other.
