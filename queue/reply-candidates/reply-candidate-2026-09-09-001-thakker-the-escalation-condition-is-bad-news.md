The five-part decision list is right, and worth stealing. But it puts the weight on the wrong word. "The condition that requires escalation" sounds like a definition problem. It's actually a disclosure problem.

Escalating means saying, out loud, before the outcome proves it: I've reached the edge of what I can handle inside this boundary. That's not neutral information. It's an admission, and admissions get read as failures long before anyone checks whether the timing was actually right.

Every team I've watched avoid escalating hadn't misunderstood their boundary. They understood it perfectly. What they didn't have was any evidence that naming the boundary early wouldn't be treated the same as missing it. So they wait for the evidence to become undeniable, which is precisely the point where the founder becomes the fastest problem-solver again, because now it really is urgent.

Bad news is data, but only for the person who isn't punished for producing it early. Fix that and the decision-rights framework runs itself. Skip it and you've built an elegant system for routing problems upward exactly one crisis late.
