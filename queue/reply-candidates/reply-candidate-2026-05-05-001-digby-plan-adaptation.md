"Adapting the plan" is the right conclusion, but it assumes you know when adaptation is needed.

The project that sticks to a failing plan isn't usually run by someone committed to defending it. It's run by someone who found out it was failing after the window for course correction had already closed — because the reporting cycles were monthly, the status reviews were surface-level, and the people with ground truth weren't being heard.

The plan doesn't become immovable because PMs are rigid. It becomes immovable because the information that would justify changing it arrives too late and too filtered to act on.

Short feedback loops and an environment where unwelcome signals reach the people who can act on them: that's what makes adaptation possible. Without those, "be flexible" is advice for being a different kind of PM in exactly the same swamp.
