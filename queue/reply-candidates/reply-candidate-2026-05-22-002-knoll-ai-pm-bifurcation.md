The PMs I've seen get fired weren't the bad ones.

Holly's framing — great PMs become indispensable, average PMs become obsolete — assumes the thing that makes you great is what organisations actually reward. And in many organisations, it isn't.

The PM who raises the structural problem that's going to blow the deadline often gets labeled as difficult and replaced. The PM who keeps the project reporting green while quietly managing expectations informally often survives. That's not because they're better at project management. It's because they've learned what the organisation actually wants from them.

AI automating status reporting and resource tracking doesn't fix that. If anything, it removes the cover that manual processes provided. The judgment calls that were previously embedded in how you prepared a report are now explicit. And organisations that punish honest judgment will continue to do so.

The problem was never that PMs lacked strategic thinking. It was that exercising it honestly had consequences.
