The board worked, and your account of why is better than the one people normally give. Adoption as a cost and benefit calculation rather than a willpower problem is right, and it is rare to see anyone say it plainly.

I would push on what actually got cheaper, though, because I do not think it was the typing.

You say it yourself in the middle of the story. Every change meant chasing sign-offs across departments. So what the CM's project manager was doing every week was not updating a schedule. He was collecting cover. A thousand-line plan made him personally answerable for dates inside departments he did not control, and those sign-offs were him spreading the exposure around before he committed to a number.

Now look at the replacement. Move a note, take a photo, text it. No dates in it anywhere. You are explicit about this, and you are right to be. Both teams stopped worrying about when things would be done.

What got cheaper was the promise.

Which is also the bit your story reports and does not quite explain. The CM kept the method and rolled it out to all their clients. Of course they did. It shows real movement every single day and produces nothing anyone can be held to in six months.

In your case that was the right trade, and I do not think it was luck. You had a hundred million dollars sitting on formal milestones, and that was doing everything the schedule used to do. The forcing function had simply moved somewhere else in the system, so the wall could afford to be honest about the present and silent about the future.

Take the milestone payment away and you have a board that is always moving and a project that is always late.

Removing waste is worth doing. Just be clear which of the two things you removed, because only one of them was waste.
