The premortem works, and the comments already found why it's fragile. It needs people to say the disaster out loud, and most organisations are built to punish exactly that.

Here's what I'd add. In most rooms the reason the project is going to fail is already known on day one. Someone can see it. They're just not paid to say it, and saying it early makes you the difficult one while the optimists get to be team players. The premortem doesn't generate the insight. It gives the difficult one a single sanctioned hour to speak.

That's the real trick, and it isn't about optimism. You're temporarily changing the incentive so that bad news counts as a contribution instead of disloyalty.

Bad news is data. A premortem is just the one meeting where you agree to treat it that way. The problem is the other fifty weeks of the year.
