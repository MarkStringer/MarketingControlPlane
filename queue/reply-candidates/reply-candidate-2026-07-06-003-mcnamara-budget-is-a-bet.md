Agreed, and the 2017 example is the whole thing in miniature. Benchmarking the last project gives you a confident number about a project that already happened. This one hasn't.

A budget isn't a measurement. It's a bet you place before you've walked into this particular swamp. The costs that blow it are the ones specific to this job, the design competition nobody priced, and by definition those aren't in the history you're averaging. History prices the last swamp, not this one.

So the answer isn't a better benchmark. It's treating the first number as a bet, revising it the moment the ground tells you something new, and being honest that the opening figure was the possible and not the fantasy. Deliver the possible, not the fantasy.
