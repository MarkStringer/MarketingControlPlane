post_summary: O'Reilly distinguishes project managers from program managers. The project manager delivers the project (scope, time, budget). The program manager delivers the business benefit (strategy, cross-team coordination, sponsor management). Working together they balance delivery with strategy. PM success is measured on delivery, program manager success on benefits.
---

The clean line between delivering the project and delivering the benefit is exactly where most projects quietly die.

I've watched teams hit scope, time and budget and still ship something almost nobody used. Everyone on the delivery side got their bonus. The split is what lets you do that. If one person owns delivery and a different person owns benefit, then no single human is actually on the hook for the only thing that ever mattered.

The project is a bet on a benefit. Drawing a neat boundary halfway through the bet is fine on an org chart, but it tells the project manager their job ends before the bet pays off. That is how you end up with a green status report stapled to a failure.

The day delivery and benefit answer to two different people is the day "on time and on budget" stops meaning very much.
