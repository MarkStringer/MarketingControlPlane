Every item on that list is invisible by construction, and I think that is the actual problem rather than a shortage of appreciation.

Reading the room. Holding accountability without damaging the relationship. Keeping momentum through the messy middle. All real, all skilled, and all of them produce an absence. The argument that never happened. The rework nobody did. The escalation that was not needed because two people talked on a Tuesday.

Nobody has ever been promoted for an absence.

So when the profession is asked to justify itself it reaches for the same sentence every time, which is that things would have been worse without me. That sentence cannot be checked. And unfalsifiable value is not value, it is a claim, and the person setting next year's budget knows the difference even if they are too polite to say so.

I have made that claim myself, in a room, about a project that had gone reasonably well. It went down about as convincingly as you would expect.

Here is the version that does work, and it is uncomfortable because it can go wrong in public. The project is a bet. Before it starts, write down what you think is going to happen, which two or three things are most likely to break it, what you are doing about them, and what would tell you that you were wrong. One page. Give it to the sponsor. Then be in the room when the result lands and read it back.

Do that three times and you have something nobody can wave away, which is a record of a person who called it early while everyone else was being optimistic in writing.

The craft is not undervalued because people fail to understand it. It is undervalued because it is almost always described afterwards, when the counterfactual is safe and the person describing it is the only witness.
