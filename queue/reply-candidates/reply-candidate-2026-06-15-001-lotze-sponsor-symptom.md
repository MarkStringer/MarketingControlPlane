The missing sponsor isn't the root cause. It's a symptom.

When an executive disappears from a project mid-delivery, the question worth asking is: what did they actually understand about it when they sponsored it? In most cases, not much. Someone presented them with a set of promises — scope, timeline, budget — and they approved the presentation, not the reality underneath it.

The project is a bet. The sponsor made it. But bets are only ownable when you know what you're betting and what the odds are. If all you got was a slide deck and a confident PM, you didn't get enough.

When bad news doesn't reach the top, it's rarely because the sponsor is absent. It's because the organisation has spent months learning that the sponsor doesn't want bad news. Active sponsorship won't fix that. A culture where bad news travels upward will.
