Every PMO transformation conversation for the past decade has started with "we need to measure outcomes, not outputs." The diagnosis is right. The prescription keeps not working.

Output metrics dominate not because PMOs lack ambition but because outputs are measurable, attributable, and defensible at the governance meeting. If the feature shipped on time, that fact is hard to dispute. If the benefit materialised at a debatable level six months later, that is a conversation nobody wants to have in a steering committee.

You do not change that by calling the PMO a "value integrator." You change it by making it politically safe to report uncertain or disappointing outcomes. That is not a framework problem. It is a leadership problem that frameworks are being asked to solve.
