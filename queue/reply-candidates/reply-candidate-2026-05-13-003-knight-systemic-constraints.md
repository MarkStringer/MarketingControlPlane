This is right. Systemic impediments are usually the binding constraint, not individual effort.

But it's worth asking where those impediments come from.

Tech debt is the accumulated cost of decisions made when the original architectural assumptions turned out to be wrong. Cross-cutting dependencies are what you get when the team structure was designed around a product roadmap the product has since outgrown. Process overhead accumulates when past uncertainty generates present paperwork.

Most systemic project constraints are the sediment of earlier bets that came back with new information and weren't properly updated. The plan was treated as correct a little longer than it should have been.

Clearing the tech debt and restructuring the teams is necessary. But it won't stop the next layer accumulating unless the organisation changes how it responds when the plan turns out to be wrong.
