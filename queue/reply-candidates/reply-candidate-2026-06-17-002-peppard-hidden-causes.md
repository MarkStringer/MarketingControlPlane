The root causes aren't surprising. They're predictable.

When you commit to delivering something you don't yet know how to deliver, hiding the evidence that it's not going as planned becomes the rational thing for everyone in the chain to do. The team tells the PM what the PM wants to hear. The PM tells the sponsor what the sponsor wants to hear.

The causes look subtle and hidden because the whole system is designed to keep them that way. That's not an unusual organizational failure — it's what happens when a bet gets structured as a commitment. Once the commitment is made, bad news stops being information and starts being a threat.

Bad news is data. But only if the people holding it believe they'll be heard rather than punished for passing it upward.
