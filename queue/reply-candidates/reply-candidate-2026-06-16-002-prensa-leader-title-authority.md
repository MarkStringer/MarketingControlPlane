The difference between a "project leader" and a "project manager" in most organisations is the business card.

If the person in either role still can't move the deadline, change the resourcing, or stop scope arriving from three directions above them, the title is doing no work. They can report. They can escalate. They can frame things in business language. And when the project fails, they're accountable.

What determines whether you can actually make trade-offs and protect value isn't what you're called. It's what authority you've been given. In most organisations, nobody is giving that away.

Calling the role "project leader" rebrands the accountability without changing the power. That's not an upgrade. That's a longer fall.
