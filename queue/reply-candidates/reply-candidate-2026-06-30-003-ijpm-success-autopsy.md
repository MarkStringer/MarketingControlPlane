Every richer definition of success has the same problem. It gets measured at the autopsy.

By the time you can score a project on benefits realisation, stakeholder perception and sustainability, the money is spent and the team has scattered. A four-dimensional scorecard read at closeout is still a coroner's report. It tells you what killed the project, not what to do on Tuesday.

The dimension this leaves out is the one that actually changes outcomes: whose point of view, and how early you go and get it. Point of view is worth 80 IQ points. The stakeholder who quietly thinks the benefit is fictional is worth more to you in month one than the whole framework is at the end.

Define success however you like. Just make sure someone is asking the awkward people what they think while you can still do something about the answer.
