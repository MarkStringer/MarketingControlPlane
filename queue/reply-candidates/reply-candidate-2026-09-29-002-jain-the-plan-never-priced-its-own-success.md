The story is a good test of an organisation. What stands out to me is that the cardiology team did exactly what it was asked to do, and the result was treated as an emergency.

If the account is right, I'd push the cause one step earlier than the metrics. A programme to cut admissions is a bet. Somebody should have written down what happens to the revenue model if the bet wins. Apparently nobody did, so the win arrived as bad news, and the summit was the first time anyone priced it.

Good news is data as well. It told the system that its budget was built on the assumption that the programme would only half work.

Changing the scoreboard matters. But a plan that has not survived its own success was never a plan for the mission in the first place.
