The IKEA effect is real, and it explains something I have watched happen more times than I can count. Hand someone a finished transformation and they treat it the way you would treat assembly instructions for furniture you did not choose. No attachment, because there was no labour.

But watch what happens the first time a team's build does not match the direction leadership actually needed.

That is the moment that decides it, not the moment of consultation. Most transformations that claim to let teams build inside a fixed direction are running real co-creation right up until the first disagreement, and then quietly are not. The team does not need a second announcement to find that out. One overruled decision does the job on its own.

Bad news is data, and the first override is exactly that kind of data. It tells the team what the actual deal was, whatever the launch slide said.

If you mean it, the test is not whether you let people build. It is whether you can point to a decision where their build won and yours lost. Most transformations cannot, and that gap is what your own IKEA effect is quietly measuring.
