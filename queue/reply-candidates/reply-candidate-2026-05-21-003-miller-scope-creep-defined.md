The definition is doing something quietly misleading here. "Unmanaged change" implies there's a category called "managed change" which is fine — not scope creep, just healthy evolution.

But most scope changes aren't labelled "unmanaged" when they arrive. They're discussed, sometimes agreed, considered handled at the time. They become "scope creep" retrospectively, after the project is late and someone needs a word for what went wrong.

The diagnostic value of this definition runs backwards. You find scope creep in the post-mortem, not in the planning session where it was approved.
