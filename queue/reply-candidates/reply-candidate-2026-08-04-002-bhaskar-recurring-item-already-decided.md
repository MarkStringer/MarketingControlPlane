The one on your list I would underline is the same issues reappearing every quarter.

Everybody reads that line as a failure to resolve something. I read it as a resolution that nobody was willing to sign.

An issue that survives four reviews has been decided. The organisation has decided to live with it. That decision got made by attrition rather than in a meeting, but it was made, and every person in that room knows it. What the quarterly review does is quietly re-ratify it in a format that lets everyone keep describing it as still open.

Open is the load bearing word. Open is free. Open means nobody has yet written down the sentence "we are not going to fix this", and the trouble with that sentence is that it has an author.

I watched an item go round a portfolio review for the better part of two years. Same slide, same amber, same wording. Somebody senior would say we really need to get on top of that, and we would all nod, and it would be back in eleven weeks looking exactly the same. When I eventually asked what would happen if we just closed it as accepted, the room went quiet. Closing it meant naming the person who was accepting it. Amber meant nobody had to be that person.

So I would push back gently on the fix. You want leadership systems where ownership is unavoidable, which is true but is also the hardest possible place to start, because the theatre exists precisely to make ownership avoidable.

Start somewhere smaller and ruder. Make the review close things. Every recurring item gets one of exactly two labels. Either a name and a date, or "accepted, we are living with this, and here is who decided that." No third option. No amber, no monitoring, no under review.

About half your register will empty inside a quarter, and people will be quite annoyed about it. The half that is left is your actual portfolio, and it will be the first honest picture anyone has had of it in years.

The theatre is not there because your leaders are lazy. It is there because still open costs nothing, and the alternative sentence needs somebody's name at the bottom of it.
