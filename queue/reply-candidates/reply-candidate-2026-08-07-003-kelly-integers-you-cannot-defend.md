The pairing advice is right. I want to argue with the integers.

A strict order with no duplicates looks like rigour, and at the top of the list it is. Everyone can defend one, two and three. The trouble starts somewhere around seven.

Nobody in that room can honestly tell you why item seven beats item eight. The difference between them sits well inside the noise of what anyone actually knows, which is not much, because the value of most of these things is not knowable until after they have been built. Forcing a strict order does not create the missing knowledge. It requires the group to produce a number it does not have, and then write it down in a format that looks like it did.

That matters later, though not in the way you would expect. It matters because an unearned ordering is trivially easy to attack.

Someone senior turns up wanting their thing moved from eleven to four. The conversation that follows is not about value. It is about whether your ordering survives being questioned. And it does not, because you made it up, and everybody who was in the room when you made it up knows you made it up. So it moves. Do that twice and the list is no longer a priority list. It is a record of who has been in the building recently.

A list with honest ties is much harder to move. If seven through fifteen are all a joint seven, there is nothing to argue a path up through. You have to come at the top six directly, and those are the ones that can actually be defended. The precision you gave up was fictional. The precision you kept is load bearing.

The stuff that drops off the end is the other half of this, and it drops off silently. Nobody tells the person who asked for item twenty three that it is not happening, because the list does not say not happening. It says twenty three. They find out ten months later by asking, and then they escalate, and it comes back near the top with a sponsor attached to it.

Everything on that list is a bet, including the ones you are not taking. Numbering fifty items one to fifty presents them as fifty considered decisions. Six of them were decisions. It is more honest, and considerably more durable, to say we are backing these six, we are not backing the rest of it this year, and here is the list of people I have told that to.

Point of view is worth 80 IQ points, but only where you have actually got one. Manufacturing forty four more of them to fill in the integers just gives everyone else something to pull on.

Share the pain, as you say. Share the accurate amount of it.
