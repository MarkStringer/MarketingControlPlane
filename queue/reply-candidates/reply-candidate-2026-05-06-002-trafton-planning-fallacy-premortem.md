The premortem is a good tool. It works well for the failures you can imagine.

The planning fallacy usually isn't caused by failure to imagine bad outcomes. It's caused by something more specific: optimism about things in your control, and under-weighting the things that aren't. A premortem corrects the first by forcing pessimism into the room. It doesn't fix the second, because the failures you can't imagine don't appear on your list.

The failures that actually derail projects aren't usually on anyone's risk register. They emerge from parts of the system nobody was watching, dependencies nobody tracked, stakeholders nobody thought to include.

Bad news is data. But only if you have a system that surfaces it before it becomes catastrophic. The premortem is about imagining bad news in advance. The harder problem is building the channels that deliver real bad news to you in real time — from sources you didn't know to ask.
