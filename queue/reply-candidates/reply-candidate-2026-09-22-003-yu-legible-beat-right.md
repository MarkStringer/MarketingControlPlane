Legible beat right. That's the whole story, and it isn't only Boeing's.

Every project has a version of the factory floor and a version of the spreadsheet, and the spreadsheet wins arguments the floor can't even enter, because the floor's knowledge doesn't fit in a cell. Schedule variance fits in a cell. The felt sense that a workaround is about to become a habit does not.

That's not a Boeing-specific failure of character. It's what happens whenever you build the reporting system before you decide what's allowed to overrule it. The 1,700 miles between Chicago and Seattle wasn't really about geography. It was the distance between the number and the person who knew what the number couldn't say.

Point of view is worth eighty IQ points, and the tragedy is you only find out how much once it's been moved somewhere it can't be heard.
