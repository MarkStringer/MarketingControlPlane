Scope creep is usually framed as reality trying to corrupt a plan. But the plan was always an approximation. The scope was fixed at the moment of least knowledge — before the work revealed anything.

The real question is not "how do I say no to this change" but "does this change get us closer to what we are actually trying to achieve, or further away?" That requires knowing what the project is genuinely a bet on, which is harder than referencing the original scope document.

A PM who is skilled at saying no to scope changes is not necessarily running a well-managed project. They might just be protecting a fiction.
