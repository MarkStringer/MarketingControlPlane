Good questions. I would ask all ten.

I still think the 70% is pointing somewhere other than where you have aimed it.

Here's the problem with bad requirements as a cause. It is the one diagnosis that is always available afterwards. Every project that failed had a requirement that turned out to be wrong, and the only reliable way to find out which one was to fail. So the post mortem writes itself, and it clears everybody in the room, because nobody has to explain why they did not know something in advance.

Now run your questions in a real room and listen to what happens.

"What's non-negotiable?" The honest answer is very often the date. The person who knows why the date is non-negotiable is not going to say why in front of their director.

"Who cares if it's solved?" Sometimes the honest answer is nobody. This is a response to an audit finding and the point is that it exists.

"Where have similar projects failed?" Everybody in the room knows. Two of them were on it. Writing it down means putting your name next to a prediction about your employer's competence.

So the answers were available. They were in the room, in people's heads, on the day. They did not make it onto the page, and not because nobody asked.

I sat in a requirements workshop for three days once. Good facilitator, right people, whiteboard full. On the last afternoon, in the corridor, somebody mentioned that the department the system was being built for was being restructured in the autumn and probably would not exist in the same shape. That went into nobody's document. It was the only requirement that mattered.

Bad news is data. A requirements workshop is the first moment in a project when all of the bad news is sitting in one room at the same time, and it is usually the last moment anyone is willing to write any of it down. That is not a gathering technique problem. It is a question of what your organisation lets people say out loud with their boss's boss present.

So I would add an eleventh question, and I would ask it about the room rather than about the project. What does the person who answers honestly in here have to lose.

If the answer is nothing, your ten questions will work exactly as advertised. If the answer is something, you will come out with ten well documented answers and one missing one, and the missing one is the project.
