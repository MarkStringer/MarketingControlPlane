The structural observation is worth dwelling on.

Scrum Masters are having a technical argument: you're doing Scrum incorrectly. Practitioners are having a strategic argument: is Scrum the right tool at all? Those aren't the same conversation, and talking louder on either side won't make them converge.

Scrum was a bet that short feedback loops solve the hardest problem in product development. That was a reasonable bet for a specific distribution of uncertainty. If AI has changed what the hardest problem is — and it probably has shifted significantly — then doing Scrum more correctly doesn't address that. You'd need to re-examine the bet.

"Scrum is the anti-pattern" is too blunt. But "this framework was designed for a different distribution of difficulty" is exactly what practitioners seem to be trying to say.
