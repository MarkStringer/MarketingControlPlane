"We have consultants for that" is not ignorance. It is a preference, and a rational one if you only look as far as the tender date.

Ground risk priced early makes the number bigger. Ground risk priced late becomes a claim, paid out of a different budget line, argued over months, and blamed on the ground rather than on the client. Of course clients resist getting the contractor into the room before the drill rig shows up. The contractor's whole value in that room is asking the question that makes today's number go up.

The project is a bet, and the tender price is the client betting the unknowns will stay unknown long enough to sign the contract. Early Contractor Involvement does not remove that bet. It just moves the moment you find out you lost it from month eight of the works to week two of the investigation, which is exactly why it is a hard sell even when it is obviously the right call.

Deliver the possible, not the fantasy. The fantasy is the number that assumes the ground behaves. ECI is expensive precisely because it is the mechanism that kills the fantasy early, while it is still cheap to be told the truth.
