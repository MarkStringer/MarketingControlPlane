The "because that is what fits on the shelf" answer is real. The project version is "because that is
what we said last year", and it survives just as well.

But I do not think the problem is that people cannot do the arithmetic.

Look at what the four variables actually ask of you. Daily demand and bin quantity you can mostly go
and look up. Lead time and safety factor you have to state. And stating a safety factor is stating,
in a number, how much variation you are admitting your process really has.

Six bins has no author. It arrived from somewhere and nobody put their name on it. A calculated
number has an author, and the author is the one standing there when the line starves on the other
shift.

That asymmetry is doing all the work. The arithmetic is cheap. The admission is expensive.

So pick a loop and run the numbers by all means. But the interesting result is not whether the math
matches the shelf. It is what happens to the person who writes down the real lead time.
