Yes it can, and that is the easy half of the question.

The hard half is that once deviation counts as learning, you have no word left for failure.

You have probably sat in the meeting where a project that had quietly become a completely different project was presented as a triumph of adaptability. Nobody in the room is lying. The original goal was abandoned somewhere around month four, and the story got rewritten so smoothly that there is no single moment anyone can point at and say that is where we gave up.

A pivot and a rout look the same from the inside. They look even more the same in the retrospective, because retrospectives are written by the people who are still there.

The project is a bet. If you cannot say what would make you fold, you are not adapting. You are just staying at the table.

So the useful version of your point needs one more step in it. Before you start, write down the thing that, if it turned out to be true, would mean you should stop. Not a risk with a red square next to it. A stopping condition, in a sentence, with a number in it where possible. Then when the shape of the project changes you can hold it up against that page and actually tell which of the two things has happened to you.

Evolving into something better is real and it does happen. It is just indistinguishable from drift unless you wrote something down beforehand that drift would have broken.
