The diagnosis is hard to argue with. Fixing a number at the moment of maximum ignorance and then managing against it for a decade is not a plan, and everyone involved knows it is not a plan.

But here's the thing. The baseline is bad at forecasting because forecasting was never its job.

It is the record of what somebody promised in order to get the money released. That is all it is. Its usefulness has nothing to do with whether the number was any good, and everything to do with the fact that it stopped moving, so that ten years later you can put the outturn next to it and see the size of the gap.

The gap is the only honest measurement in the whole system. It is certainly the only one produced by nobody's judgement.

A baseline that evolves with new data cannot produce a gap. The target ends up wherever the programme ends up. Every programme lands on target, forever, and the estimate that was optimistic in the first place is never again attached to the person who made it.

So it is worth asking who a fixed baseline actually inconveniences. Not really the programme manager, who has been openly working around a stale number for years and has plenty of company. It inconveniences whoever set that number low to win the funding, because the variance is the one piece of paper that leads back to them.

And I would look hard at the last recommendation. Adherence to an early estimate is brutally falsifiable. It has a date on it, and a stranger with a calculator can check it. Mission impact is assessed by the people running the programme, against criteria they helped write, on no particular date in particular.

That is not a shift to a more adaptive measure. It is a shift to a measure that can no longer come back and say no.

None of which is an argument for keeping the ten year old number as the plan. Re-plan as often as the world changes. Just write each new number down next to the old one, with the date and the name of whoever moved it, and never delete the first one.

A bet you are allowed to re-price after the race has started is not a bet. It is a subscription.
