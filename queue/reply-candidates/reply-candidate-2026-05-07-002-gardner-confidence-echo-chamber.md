"Keep a contrarian around" is correct as far as it goes.

But it frames information suppression as a personality problem. The leader gets overconfident, stops listening, and needs to consciously hire someone who'll push back.

The structural version is harder. Bad news doesn't reach the top because the organisation's incentives run against it. The project manager who reports amber when everyone else is reporting green gets told to recalibrate. The team that flags a problem early gets handed an unrealistic fix deadline. Good news travels fast. Bad news arrives late and filtered, if it arrives at all.

You can't solve that by asking for volunteers to be contrarians. The person who tells the room the strategy is wrong needs protection, not just an invitation.

Bad news is data. But first you have to build an organisation where data like that can actually get through.
