Experience teaches you the shape of the swamps you've already been in.

That's valuable — if you keep going back to the same kind of swamp. But most projects don't work that way. Each one is different terrain. The PM with thirty years in infrastructure projects isn't automatically equipped for a software transformation. They've just developed a very detailed mental model of a different problem.

What actually develops you isn't doing the job. It's doing the job, making predictions, being wrong, and working out why. Experience without that loop just makes you more confident in patterns from your past. Which is fine until the new project doesn't fit those patterns.

All projects are swamps. The course won't save you. But neither will experience alone. What saves you is the habit of noticing when your mental model is wrong — and being curious rather than defensive about it.
