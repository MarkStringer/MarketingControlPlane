A good communicator running a bad plan delivers the bad news clearly and on time. That is actually valuable — it is how you course-correct.

But "communication wins, not plans" assumes the two are in tension. They are not. The plan is what you communicate — the shared understanding of what you are trying to do, in what sequence, under what constraints. Without that, effective communication just circulates ambiguity in a more engaging way.

Most plan failures happen not because there was too much process, but because the plan never reflected what anyone actually believed was possible.
