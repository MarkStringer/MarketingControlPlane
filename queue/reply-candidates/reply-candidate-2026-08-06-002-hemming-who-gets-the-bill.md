There is a test worth running on any explanation for why projects fail. Ask who has to change if it is true.

"We lacked strategy alignment" sends the bill upstairs. Somebody senior has to decide something, drop something, or admit out loud that two of their priorities were never compatible.

"We lacked accountability" sends the bill downwards and sideways. Everybody else has to own more, try harder, and be somewhat less like the people in your list.

Only one of those can be adopted without anybody powerful doing anything at all. Which is why I would gently push back on it being the unpopular opinion. Every organisation I have worked in was thrilled to discover it had an accountability problem.

But look at your own examples, because they are better evidence than the conclusion you have drawn from them.

Sponsors missing key decisions. Priorities shifting with little notice. Critical feedback arriving too late.

Every one of those is a person with more power than the project manager choosing where to spend their attention. That is not a character defect. That is what having more power consists of.

And the script you want is a good script. "That gap was ours, we will have a solution by next week." I have used it. It works beautifully sideways, and it works downwards.

Try it upwards. "That decision was yours, you missed it, I need it Thursday." That is not accountability. That is a career event, and everybody in the room knows which one it is before you get to the end of the sentence.

Here's the thing. Accountability is not a virtue that people either possess or lack. It is a bet on the organisation. Saying "that was mine and I got it wrong" in public only makes sense if the last three people who did it are still here and still trusted.

So when you hear "not my fault, I was waiting on so-and-so", you are not listening to a moral failure. You are listening to an accurate weather report. Bad news is data, and so is the shape people twist it into to make it safe to say.

I would stop asking how to drive accountability among sponsors and stakeholders, and go and find out what happened to the last person in your organisation who owned a serious miss in front of a steering group. Whatever happened to them is your accountability policy. Everything else is a poster.
