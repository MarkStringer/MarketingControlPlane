The satirical read is right, but the target is too narrow.

Whether the Scrum Guide is prescriptive or flexible, the underlying promise is the same: that there is a correct procedure for managing uncertain work, and applying it produces better outcomes.

Twenty-odd years of evidence suggests this isn't quite right. Uncertainty doesn't yield to procedure. It yields to judgment about the specific situation you're in.

A guide that says "customise to your context" is slightly more honest than one that says "do exactly this." But it is still a guide. All projects are swamps. A guide can describe swamps in general terms. It cannot tell you about the one you're standing in now, with this team, these constraints, and this particular mess of unknowns.

The problem isn't whether the procedure is flexible. The problem is treating procedure as the answer to questions that only the project can answer.
