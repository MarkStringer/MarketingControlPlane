The ambiguity here isn't a failure of process. It's the government declining to place a bet it
isn't ready to defend.

Committing a number means defending that number against every other claim on the budget, in
public, for years. Staying vague costs nothing today. So the state does what any actor facing a bet
it doesn't want to price does: it waits for more information, and calls the waiting "process."

The unfairness you're describing isn't the ambiguity itself. It's that someone still has to move
first while the state delays, and it isn't the state. SMEs are being asked to commit capital, lock
suppliers and hire against a bet nobody with the authority to place it has actually placed. The
risk of an undecided bet doesn't evaporate while it's undecided. It just moves downstream, to
whoever can least afford to be wrong.

That's not a funding gap. That's a bet with the wrong person holding it.
