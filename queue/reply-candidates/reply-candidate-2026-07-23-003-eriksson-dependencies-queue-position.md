The six teams and three months bit is exactly right. It's the diagnosis I'd push back on.

Most of that three months isn't coordination. It's queue time. Your request is sitting in the platform team's backlog behind work that somebody more senior than you already told them to do. The handoffs aren't slow because handoffs are inherently slow. They're slow because nobody with the authority to do it has said your thing matters more than their thing, and until someone does, every polite chase is you asking a team to disobey their own boss.

That's a priority problem wearing a coordination costume. Reorganising doesn't delete it, it relocates it. Give six teams the independence to make payments changes themselves and you get six implementations that disagree about what a refund is, and you pay the coordination cost two years later with interest. Dependency reduction is worth doing. It is also a bet, with a price, competing with every other bet on the table.

And I'd defend project management here, or at least the useful version of it. Somebody has to go and find out that your work is fourteenth in that queue, then say so out loud in a room where the order can actually be changed. That isn't admin. Queue position is the most actionable bad news on the whole project, and bad news is data.
