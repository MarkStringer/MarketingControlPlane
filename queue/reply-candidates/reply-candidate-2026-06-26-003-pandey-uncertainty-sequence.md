Agreed that the job is reducing uncertainty. The trap is which uncertainty you reduce first.

Most teams burn down the comfortable uncertainty early, the work they already know how to do, because it produces tidy progress and a slide that nods along nicely. The thing that could actually kill the project, the integration nobody understands, the user who might hate it, gets left until late, when there's no budget and no nerve left to act on whatever you find.

Reducing uncertainty isn't free and it isn't uniform. You pay to find out, and you should pay first for the answer that decides whether the rest is worth building at all. The project is a bet, so run the part that could lose it while you can still walk away.
