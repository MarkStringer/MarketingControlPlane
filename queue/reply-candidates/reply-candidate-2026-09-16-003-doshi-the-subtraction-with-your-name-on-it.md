Agreed on the mechanism, subtraction beats addition every time. But there is a harder second step
past clarity: whose name is on the subtraction.

Anyone can produce a tidy summary that removes noise while staying vague enough that nobody can
later say it was wrong. That is clarity without a point of view. The rarer skill is subtracting down
to a specific claim, one that can be shown wrong in six months, and putting your name on it anyway.

Point of view is worth eighty IQ points precisely because it is the version of clarity that costs
something. The safe kind is cheap and everyone can produce it. The useful kind is the kind that
risks being wrong out loud.
