Walking with nothing lined up is the part most people will read straight past. It is the only bit that actually cost you anything, so I will take the rest of it seriously.

The three questions at the bottom are the weakest thing in the post. I say that as someone who would have written exactly the same three.

Who is in your planning meetings. When did engineering last say no to a feature. How did your previous CTO leave. Those get asked in an interview, which is a room where both sides are selling. An organisation that does not have this problem will answer them honestly. An organisation that does will answer them warmly, and mean every word, because nobody running a place like that recognises themselves in your post.

You cannot screen for this in advance. You find it out the first time you carry somebody a genuine piece of bad news and watch what they do with it in the following ten minutes. That usually happens somewhere around week six, it costs nothing to run, and it is unmistakable when it lands.

Which brings me to the line I think is doing the real work here, and you have put it in as an aside. I stayed too long. Kept hoping it would change.

You did not lack information. You tell us yourself it was not the first time, so you had the pattern well before you acted on it. What you did not have was a number you had agreed with yourself in advance about what would be enough. Without one, every single incident stays survivable, because each one on its own is smaller than the cost of leaving. That is not weakness. It is arithmetic, and it points the same way for years at a stretch.

Bad news is data even when the project is you. The difficulty is that this particular data arrives one incident at a time, and no individual instance is ever big enough to act on.

So the question worth asking before you take the job is not any of your three. It is what would have to happen, and how many times, before I go. Decide it while you still find the place slightly disappointing rather than intolerable, and write it down somewhere you will trip over it later. You will not be able to think clearly about it at the time, because by then you will be the one hoping it changes.
