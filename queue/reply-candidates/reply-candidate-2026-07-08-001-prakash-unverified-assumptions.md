post_summary: Ravi Prakash argues that "assumptions don't kill projects, unverified assumptions do." Assumptions are inevitable across scope, planning, budgeting and closure, but become dangerous when undocumented or unvalidated. His fix is to document assumptions, make them time-bound and measurable, back them with contingency plans, review them continuously, and convert them into identified risks. The distinction he draws is between assumptions themselves and their verification status.
---

"Unverified assumptions" makes it sound like a hygiene problem. Document them, time-bound them, review them, convert them into risks. Do all of that and you still get hurt.

The assumptions that sink a project are usually the ones nobody thought to write down, because at the start they weren't visible yet. A project is a bet placed on a stack of assumptions you can see and a bigger stack you can't. All projects are swamps. You don't drain one by keeping a tidy log. You drain it by getting in the water early and finding out which assumptions were wrong while being wrong is still cheap.

The verified assumptions rarely kill you. The one you were so sure of that you never wrote it down, that is the one to watch.
