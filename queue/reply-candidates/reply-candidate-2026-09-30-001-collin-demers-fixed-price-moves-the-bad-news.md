Fixed price doesn't transfer the risk. It transfers the bad news.

Once the price is fixed, every discovery about scope is a commercial event. The supplier learns something is bigger than assumed and the useful move is to hold it until it can be turned into a change order. The buyer learns the same and the useful move is to hold it until it can be argued as included. Both sides know the project is in a swamp. Neither says so, because saying so costs money.

So the failure you describe isn't really about picking the wrong contract type. It's that the contract decides who gets punished for telling the truth early.

T&M with a cap and cost-plus work better for fuzzy scope for the same reason. They make the surprise something you can both look at, rather than something you have to defend.

Whatever the contract type, the question worth asking is what it does to the first person who says the estimate was wrong.
