The numbers are real, but they don't explain the undervaluing. We undervalue competent management because we can only see events, and competent management is the quiet removal of events.

A manager who sets clear targets, develops people, and catches problems while they're still small produces a project where nothing dramatic happens. No firefight. No heroic weekend. Nothing to point at in the retro. The output is a non-event, and organisations are very bad at rewarding non-events.

Meanwhile the manager who lets it all drift and then pulls off a visible rescue gets the credit and often the bonus. The competent one looks like they did nothing, because the whole job was making sure nothing happened.

Bad news is data. So is the absence of bad news. Almost nobody is set up to read it.
