You said it yourself near the end. More art than science. That's the honest line, and I'd build on it rather than apologise for it.

A prioritization framework doesn't remove the judgment. It can't. What a good one does is make your judgment visible enough that someone can argue with it. That's the real value. Not minimizing bias, which no scoring column has ever actually managed, but putting your bias where people can point at it.

The danger is the other kind of framework. The one that turns a point of view into a number so nobody has to defend the point of view. "RICE said so" is how a team stops thinking and starts doing arithmetic. A score you're not allowed to challenge is worse than a gut call you can.

So keep the framework. Just make sure it's a way of showing your working, not a way of hiding whose working it was.
