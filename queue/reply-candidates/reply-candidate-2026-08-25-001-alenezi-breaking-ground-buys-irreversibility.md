The three mechanisms are right and I am not going to argue with any of the numbers. Rework at mobilisation really does cost multiples of rework at design review, and the contingency point is the one most people miss.

But you put the answer in your own second line and then walked straight past it.

Investors want to see movement.

Here's the thing. Nobody fast-tracks because they believe it saves time. Twenty years in, they have watched the same 15 percent land on every job. They do it anyway, which means it is buying something, and it is not schedule.

A design sitting in an office can be cancelled on a Tuesday afternoon by someone who has never visited the site. It costs them nothing. There is no photograph. A hole in the ground with piling rigs in it cannot be cancelled by that person, because cancelling it is now an event with a number attached and a name on the number.

So breaking ground early is not a scheduling decision at all. It is a commitment device. You are converting a proposal, which is easy to kill, into a thing that exists, which is embarrassing to kill. The 10x cost you describe is the premium, and the people paying it are not confused about the price.

Which is why the advice to wait six months is harder than it sounds. You are asking someone to leave the project in the one state where it is still cheap to cancel, for six months, in exchange for a saving that shows up long after the decision to wait has been forgotten. If the project gets shelved in month four, the person who counselled patience owns that. If it goes 15 percent over in year three, half a dozen people own that and the market took some of it.

The project is a bet, and the start date is a hedge on a different risk from the one you are measuring. You are measuring cost. They are managing the risk of it never happening.

So I would agree with the discipline and disagree about where it lives. Pre-construction discipline is not a habit a project team can adopt. It is a decision about who is allowed to cancel things and what happens to them when they do, and that decision is taken several floors above the project.

Find the last thing your organisation stopped at the design stage and look at what happened to the person who stopped it. That number tells you what your pre-construction phase is really worth, and everyone bidding your next job has already worked it out.
