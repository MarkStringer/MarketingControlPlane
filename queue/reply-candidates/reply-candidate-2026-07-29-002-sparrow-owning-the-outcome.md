Both barrels then, since you asked nicely.

I am with you on nearly all of it right up to "they own the outcome." That line gets applause every time and I think it has done more damage to the role than the note takers ever managed.

Look at what a project manager actually controls. Not the budget, someone approved that before you arrived. Not the scope, that came out of a sales conversation you were not in. Not the team, they were assigned. Not the date, the date was said out loud by a director in front of a board and has been load bearing ever since. You cannot hire, you usually cannot fire, and on a bad day you cannot get a decision out of the one person who is allowed to make it.

Then we hand that person the word ownership and go home.

Ownership without any of those levers is not accountability. It is a blame arrangement agreed in advance. Everyone gets to feel rigorous about it right up until the thing lands late, and then there is one obvious name on it, and the decisions that actually caused it were made in March by people who have since been promoted.

Here is what I think a PM can genuinely own, and it is not small. The accuracy of the picture. Whether the number in the report is the real number. Whether the sponsor finds out in week six or week twenty six. Whether the risk that everyone privately agrees is fatal has ever once been said in a room with the person who could fund the fix.

The project is a bet. It was placed by somebody else. Owning it is not your job and it was never on offer.

Telling the truth about the odds, weekly, when it is unwelcome, is the job, and almost nobody else in the building is willing to do it.
