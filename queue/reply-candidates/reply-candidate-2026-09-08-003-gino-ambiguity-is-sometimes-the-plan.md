Ambiguity fuels drama, but not by accident every time. Sometimes it's the plan.

A manager who names the priority also owns the tradeoff when it goes wrong. A manager who leaves it fuzzy gets to point at the fog afterwards and say nobody was clear. Your Clarity by Accountability matrix treats low clarity as something to be raised. Sometimes it's something to be maintained, by someone who has already worked out what naming the decision would cost them.

This matters because the fix, raise clarity and accountability together, only works on the teams where the fog is genuinely an accident. Against the deliberate kind it does nothing, because the person keeping things vague will read your clarity push as a threat and route around it rather than welcome it. You can't coach someone out of a position they're defending on purpose.

Point of view is worth 80 IQ points, which is exactly why some people spend real effort making sure nobody else's point of view is visible enough to be compared against theirs.
