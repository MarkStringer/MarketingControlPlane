Read your own four descriptions back and the difference is not competence.

In the first three, the project manager was reporting on work other people were doing. In the fourth, they hired the team, learned the technology and owned the documents that went to the Board. That is not a better person. That is a different purchase. Somebody handed number four a budget and the right to make decisions. Somebody handed the other three a spreadsheet and a recurring meeting slot.

The Deloitte one is the clearest. A paid following up machine is exactly what gets bought when a firm wants coordination without giving anyone authority. They performed the role accurately. What you objected to was the role existing.

The three months of roles, responsibilities, risks, timelines and hierarchies is worth a second look too. It reads as waste because the team changed and the documents died with it. But that documentation was never a plan. It was a record of who had agreed to what, produced for the day somebody asks whose fault this is. Organisations that do not trust each other buy an enormous amount of it, and they buy it from whoever is cheapest to blame afterwards.

Your five star PM owned the outcome because they had been allowed to place the bet. The other three were not bad at the job. They were doing the job that was actually on the table.
