You are probably right about the layers. I have sat in plenty of meetings where a manager's only visible function was translating a decision nobody upstream wanted to make into something that sounded like it had already been thought through.

But look at what that manager was actually absorbing before you call the layer waste.

A layer like that usually exists because leadership set a target that was not quite real, and someone downstream had to make the gap between the target and reality disappear before it travelled back up. Remove the manager and the target does not get more honest. The gap does not close. It just stops having anywhere to go on the way up.

That shows up later as bad news arriving with no translator and no warning, all at once instead of in instalments you could have acted on early. Cheaper on the org chart. Considerably more expensive on the numbers that have not been reported yet.

Deliver the possible, not the fantasy, only works if someone is willing to say the honest number out loud before it is due. Strip the layer whose job that was and you have not removed the friction. You have just moved it downstream, to whoever has to say it next, later, and with less room to do anything about it.
