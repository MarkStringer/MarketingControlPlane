The SBAR diagnosis is exactly right, and it isn't only a nursing problem.

Most project status reports have the same ancestry. Someone built a format to get one blocker in front of a sponsor fast: state the problem, name the decision needed. That format then became the weekly handover between a delivery team and everyone with a stake in the outcome, ten items at once, no single decision on the table, and the shape of the document never changed to fit the new job.

The receiving side notices something is wrong without being able to say what. A handover that reads like an escalation because it was built to be one, handed to someone who needs to live with the detail for a week, not act on it in the next five minutes.

Nobody decided to do this. The template was already in the building, so it got reused. Point of view is worth eighty IQ points, and most of it gets spent just noticing that the tool you inherited answers a different question to the one you're actually asking.
