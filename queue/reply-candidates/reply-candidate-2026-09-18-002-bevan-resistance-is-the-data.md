This is the more useful way to plot change response, and it matches what "bad news is data" means in project terms. The person who argues with you is handing you information. The person who nods and says nothing is handing you nothing, and you will not find out what they knew until the change fails and it becomes obvious afterwards.

Here is what I would add. Most change programmes are not neutral about which quadrant people end up in. Roadshows, FAQ decks, reassurance sessions, "any questions?" at the end of a town hall, these are largely built to move people from active resistance into passive acceptance. That gets treated as a win.

Your research says it is closer to the opposite of a win. It is converting a source of usable information into silence, and calling it success because silence is quieter to manage than dissent.

The uncomfortable version of your point: an organisation that prides itself on smooth change communication may just be an organisation that is very good at making its bad news stop talking before anyone above middle management hears it.
