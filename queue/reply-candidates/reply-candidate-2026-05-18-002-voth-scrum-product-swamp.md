The distinction is right. Scrum is optimised for delivery; it doesn't answer "what should we build?"

But Voth's product operating model has the same gap at a different level. The whole framework — diagnose, run experiments, validate, scale — assumes you can discover the answer before you fully commit to it.

The project condition is different. Government contract, regulated environment, capital project, board commitment: you're committed to delivering something before you have enough information to know whether it's possible. Experiments aren't on the menu. The decision has already been made.

In that situation you don't have a product to manage or a backlog to refine. You have a bet, placed in advance, with uncertain odds. Neither Scrum nor this model gives you tools for that — for acknowledging the uncertainty honestly, for updating your picture of what's possible as you learn, for managing the gap between what was promised and what can be delivered.

Both frameworks assume you can avoid that condition. Most projects can't.
