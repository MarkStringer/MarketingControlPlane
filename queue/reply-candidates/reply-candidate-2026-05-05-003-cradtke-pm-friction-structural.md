I'd push back slightly on framing it as character.

The friction a PM creates isn't usually a product of courage or willingness to say difficult things. It's a product of the gap between what was planned and what's happening. Any honest holder of that comparison generates friction automatically. The PM doesn't choose to be uncomfortable — they're structurally positioned at the point where the plan meets reality.

Which also means: the absence of friction isn't comfort. It's either a project going well, or a PM not looking hard enough at the gap.

The uncomfortable conversations aren't evidence that you're doing your job. They're evidence that the plan and the reality have diverged. The job is to find that divergence early and do something about it — not to build a reputation for saying unwelcome things.
