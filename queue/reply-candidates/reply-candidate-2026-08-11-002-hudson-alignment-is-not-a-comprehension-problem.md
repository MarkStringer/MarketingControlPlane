Your six are right and I think they are more than right, which is where I want to push.

Operations wants speed because Operations is measured on throughput. Finance wants lower cost because that is the number Finance carries. HSE wants zero compromise because an HSE function that compromises is an HSE function failing at its job. None of those six people is confused, and none of them is being difficult. Each one is reporting accurately from where they are standing.

Point of view is worth 80 IQ points. Every one of those six is seeing something real that the other five genuinely cannot see from where they sit.

Which is why I get stuck on one line. Communication creates alignment.

Communication fixes comprehension problems. It is very good at those. What is on your table is not a comprehension problem. Everyone in that room already understands the others perfectly well. Finance knows exactly what Engineering wants and why. Engineering knows precisely what it costs. They are not misunderstanding each other, they are wanting incompatible things for good reasons, and explaining does not touch it, because nothing anyone says in that meeting changes what any of them is measured on at the end of the year.

So the alignment gets found somewhere else, and here is the bit I would add.

One vision is cheap to agree to. That is why it works. Deliver a safe plant, on time, to budget, at quality. Everybody signs that, and everybody is sincere. It costs nothing because it does not say what happens when safe and on time point in opposite directions. The disagreement is not resolved in that meeting, it is preserved intact, and it comes back at the first hard choice with the added problem that everyone now believes it was settled.

Which is why alignment so often feels achieved right up until the week it obviously was not.

The version I would trust is smaller and much less pleasant. Put one real trade in front of them early. The safety review adds three weeks, so what do we actually do. Does the date move, does something come out of scope, does Finance release contingency, and who signs the answer. That is answerable now for the price of an uncomfortable hour, or later for the price of the thing itself.

You find out what people are aligned on by making them spend something. Alignment is discovered, not created, and it is usually smaller than the workshop suggested.

There is also a reason the word is popular that has nothing to do with you. Alignment quietly reassigns the work. A genuine conflict between two funded functions is a decision that a senior person owes, and it involves telling one of them they are getting less than they are measured on. Call it an alignment problem instead and it becomes a communication task, which lands on the project manager, who has no authority over any of the six and can be told to try harder at it indefinitely.

That is the trade I would resist. Deliver the possible, not the fantasy, and the fantasy on offer here is that all six can be satisfied by better facilitation.

When an alignment session goes smoothly, it is usually because nothing in it was decided.
