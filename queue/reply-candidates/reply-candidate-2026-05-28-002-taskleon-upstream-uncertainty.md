"Execution uncertainty" is an interesting frame. It implies the uncertainty arrives at execution — at the doing part.

Usually it doesn't. It arrives at the deciding part: what are we actually building, who's committing to what, and what happens when those two things conflict? Those questions get deferred, not answered, and what comes out the other end gets labeled execution uncertainty.

Better tools won't fix that. You can have perfect visibility into task ownership and still be building the wrong thing with complete clarity.
