This is the line most plans can't survive contact with. You write a scope at the start because the organisation demands one, and everyone in the room knows it's a guess wearing the costume of a commitment.

So the honest first task isn't defining scope, it's working out how you're going to find out what the scope actually is, and funding that finding out. Almost nobody funds it. They fund the build and then treat the discovery as failure when it arrives later, dressed as a change request and an awkward conversation.

Pretending you know the scope on day one is the fantasy. Deliver the possible, not the fantasy. The project is a bet, and the first thing you're betting on is whether you even understood what you were asked to do.
