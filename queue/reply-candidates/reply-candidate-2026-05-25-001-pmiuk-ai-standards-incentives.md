The assumption in this debate is that the bottleneck in project management is information processing. In most projects that fail, it isn't.

The data is usually there. The realistic forecast exists. What's missing is permission to surface it to the people who need to hear it.

AI will generate better predictions faster. That's genuinely useful. But a more accurate schedule doesn't help if the sponsor has already committed publicly to the original date and reads the new forecast as a threat rather than useful information.

The profession doesn't need AI to raise standards. It needs organisations that reward project managers for meeting those standards, which means treating accurate bad news as a contribution rather than a career risk.
