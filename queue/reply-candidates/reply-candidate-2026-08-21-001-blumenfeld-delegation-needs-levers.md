The task and outcome distinction is real, and "handle it" does beat reading someone their own email out loud.

But look again at the example. "We need the client to understand the delay. Handle it."

The person you just handed that to cannot move the date. They cannot add people. They cannot cut scope. So what you delegated was the conversation, not the outcome. They now own the client's reaction and none of the things causing it.

That is the bit that decides whether delegation sticks, and it is not on the list of seven. Not clarity, not trust, not feedback loops. Whether the person holding the outcome holds any of the levers that move it.

Delegate an outcome with no levers and you have not created a leader. You have created someone who has to be persuasive for a living. They will bring you good news for as long as they can, because good news is the only thing they are actually equipped to produce.

Give them one lever and it changes. Now they can come back and tell you the date has to move. That is not a failure of ownership. That is the ownership working, and bad news is data.

Every delegation is a bet on somebody. Worth checking they can play the hand you dealt them.
