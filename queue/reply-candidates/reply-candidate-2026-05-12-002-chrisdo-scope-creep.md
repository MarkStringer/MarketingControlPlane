The SOW advice is correct and still not enough.

The problem isn't always that the client didn't know the boundaries. It's that they couldn't know them yet. They find out what they want by watching it take shape.

No document captures requirements that don't exist at the time of signing.

"It's a you problem" works when the client has clear requirements and just lacks the discipline to state them. That's fixable with documentation.

But on most creative or complex projects, the scope expands because the understanding grows. The client adding three more features at month two isn't violating an agreement. They're updating their model of what they actually need. That's not a process failure. That's what commissioned work does.

The fix isn't a better scope-of-work document. It's a relationship structure that treats change as information rather than breach — and prices it accordingly from the start.
