You say it will inevitably happen. I think that sentence is doing more work than the rest of the post, and it deserves following all the way down.

If something happens on every project, at roughly the same point, for a reason rooted in how human beings actually work, then it is not a risk. It is a stage. And we have collectively agreed to keep a known, dated, near certain stage out of the plan and out of the budget, and then to act surprised when it turns up on schedule.

Which raises the awkward question of what the sign-off is for.

It is not for truth. Nobody in that workshop knows what they want. You know they do not know. They half know it themselves. Everyone signs anyway. So the signature is not establishing what the system should do, it is establishing whose fault it will be in eight months. It is a blame instrument wearing the costume of a requirements process.

Bad news is data. The first genuinely honest data on an ERP programme arrives in SIT, when somebody clicks a button and you watch their face change. Everything before that moment was people describing a system they had never used, which is not a requirement, it is a wish with a date on it.

So put the stage in the plan. A line in the schedule and a real number in the budget for the six weeks where they finally see it and change their minds. Tell the sponsor at kickoff that you are funding it, and tell them why, before it is anybody's fault.

Then the day someone says "this isn't what we need" stops being the day the project went wrong and becomes the day it hit a milestone. Same event, same cost, same people in the room. The only difference is whether you paid for it in advance or borrowed it at a very bad rate.
