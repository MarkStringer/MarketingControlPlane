The cognitive load doesn't come from noise. It comes from holding two realities simultaneously.

The plan says three weeks. The work is saying five. The stakeholder expects the original date. The team knows where things actually are. Your job is to hold all of those positions at once and keep things moving.

Simplifying decision pathways helps. But the deeper source of PM burnout is being responsible for a gap between what was promised and what's possible, a gap you didn't create and may not be able to close.

You can have perfect clarity about a project that's in serious trouble. The clarity just tells you exactly how bad it is.

Bad news from the project is data. Someone has to carry it upstream, and that person is usually the one burning out.
