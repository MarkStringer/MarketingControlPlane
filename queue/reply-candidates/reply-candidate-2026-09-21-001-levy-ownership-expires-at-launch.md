Every one of those five post-launch practices is right, and most projects never do a single one of them.

But "ownership stops once implementation ends" isn't a discipline failure. It's what the funding was for in the first place.

A project gets a budget, a team and a deadline to build the thing. Nobody gets a budget, a team or a deadline to prove the thing worked. The bet everyone signed up for was delivery, not adoption, and once delivery happens the people who were paid to care move on to the next bet.

That's not a people problem you fix by asking harder. It's a structural one. If you want a system that still gets enforced, audited and adjusted six months after go-live, someone's job has to depend on it six months after go-live. Otherwise you're hoping conscience does the work that a budget line was supposed to do.

Legal tech doesn't fade because teams get lazy after launch. It fades because the bet only ever paid out at launch.
