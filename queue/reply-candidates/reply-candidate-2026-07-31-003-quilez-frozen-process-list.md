The first half of this is straightforwardly correct and most people skip it, so it is worth saying.

The second half is how a six month system swap turns into an eighteen month transformation.

Does this process still make sense is a wonderful question with no natural stopping point. Every process you open belongs to somebody. Somebody designed it, somebody has been defending it since 2019, and somebody quietly benefits from the fact that it produces a report nobody reads. Opening it is not an analysis task. It is a negotiation, and negotiations do not appear on the plan as negotiations, they appear as a workshop with a two hour slot.

I have sat in the room where a like for like replacement acquired a review of the whole billing process because a reasonable person asked a reasonable question and nobody present had the standing to say we are not answering that this year.

All projects are swamps, and this is one of the ways you walk into one voluntarily, with a good motive and everyone nodding.

So I would add one artefact to the front of the work, which is the list of processes you are explicitly not touching. Carried over as they are, including the ones everybody knows are daft. Signed off by whoever is paying. That list is more useful than the improvement list, because the improvement list will defend itself and the frozen list will be under attack from week one.

You still get the rethink. You just get to say how much of it you bought.

Use the change as an opportunity, by all means. Decide in advance how big an opportunity you can afford, or the migration you were funded for quietly becomes a transformation nobody approved.
