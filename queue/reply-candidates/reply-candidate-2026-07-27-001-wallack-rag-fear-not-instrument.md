post_summary: Shawn Wallack argues that RAG (Red, Amber, Green) reporting is a classic project management tool that "kind of works" in Waterfall, where variance against a fixed plan is measurable, but fails in Agile because Agile is flow-driven rather than plan-driven. His specific criticisms are that RAG is "binary disguised as nuance", that "three options isn't nuance, it's faux clarity", that it rewards optimism because "no one wants to be the first team to say we're red", that green can mean "we don't know" as easily as "we're on track", that a team can be green for months without shipping, and that it invites "watermelon status" where everything looks green outside and insiders know it is red. His proposed fix is flow metrics (throughput trends, aging WIP, cumulative flow, cycle time stability, value delivered) and, if colours are kept, pairing each colour with concrete delivery context. He gives worked red/yellow/green example sentences. Closes on "Don't let color drive the conversation. Let delivery do it" and "Outcomes over optics." Watermelon status is the right diagnosis and I would push on the cure.

Swap RAG for throughput, aging WIP and cycle time and you have changed what gets measured, not what happens to the person who reports it. Flow metrics are just as gameable. Aging WIP goes down if you split the ticket. Throughput goes up if you count smaller things. Cycle time stabilises beautifully if nobody starts anything hard. Give me a month with any dashboard and I will make it look calm.

The reason nobody wants to be the first team to say red has very little to do with the colour only having three values. It is that in most places red is not a status, it is an admission. Someone senior turns up and asks what went wrong. There is a recovery plan and a weekly call. The team that flagged amber honestly in April gets more scrutiny than the team that stayed green and shipped nothing, right up until the quarter ends and everyone finds out at once.

So the honest team is punished twice. Once by the extra attention, and once by the comparison with the team that kept quiet.

Bad news is data. But it is only data in a place where the person carrying it does not get treated as the problem. Until that is true you are not measuring the project, you are measuring how frightened people are, and you will get a very stable reading.

Your worked examples are the actual fix, by the way, and not because they add context. It is because a sentence has an author. A colour does not.
