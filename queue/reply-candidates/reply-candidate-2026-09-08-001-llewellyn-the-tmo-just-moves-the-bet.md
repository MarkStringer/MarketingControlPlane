94% of milestones and a flat margin for two years is not a governance failure. It's a measurement success. The stage gates measured exactly what they were built to measure, and margin was never on that list.

A Transformation Management Office doesn't fix this by governing differently in the abstract. It fixes it, if it fixes it, because one sentence near the end of your post actually happens: funding gets re-cut next quarter when the evidence disappoints. Everything else, the chair, the structural capabilities, the label, is packaging around that one decision.

The project is a bet, and a stage-gated programme is a bet you're allowed to keep after you've lost it, because the metric that would tell you you've lost it was never wired up. Shortening the distance between evidence and funding is the actual fix. You didn't need a new office for that. You needed someone with the authority to stop paying for green dashboards, which is a much less comfortable line to put in a boardroom brief than "appoint a chair."

Deliver the possible, not the fantasy. The fantasy here was never the transformation. It was the dashboard.
