The critique lands, but it's aimed at the wrong target.

The problem with the "glorified note-taker" isn't that they're taking notes — it's that the notes are wrong. A PM who accurately records that a project is off track, over budget, and underdelivering is doing something genuinely difficult. Most organisations aren't set up to receive that information, let alone act on it. That's the skill: not the recording, but making the bad news land.

The competency gap described here — risk, contracts, finance, technology — is real. But there's one missing from the list: the willingness to say what's actually true about the state of the project when the answer is bad. That's harder to certify than a PMP, and harder to fake over time.
