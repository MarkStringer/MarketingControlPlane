"Decisions, not dates" is exactly right, and it's also where most teams quietly give up on it. A decision point is a bet, and the moment you express it as an expected value, someone upstairs hears the single most flattering number in the distribution and writes it down as the date.

The project is a bet. Sequential Decision Analytics doesn't remove the bet, it makes the odds legible. That's real progress, but legible odds are only useful in an organisation that can stand to be told them. Plenty can't. They ask for probabilities and then punish you for the part of the probability that wasn't 100%.

So the framework is sound and the hard part lives downstream of it. The work is getting the people reading the forecast to understand they are looking at a position in a game with incomplete information, not a promise. Until then "decisions not dates" just becomes decisions that everyone quietly converts back into dates.
