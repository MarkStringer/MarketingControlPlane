"How do we decide what to build?" That's still the wrong question.

Not because it's unimportant. Because "decide" implies a correct answer that analysis can surface. At the point you're committing resources to build something, there isn't one. What you have is a bet.

The five-step system Voth describes is good, but it isn't how you find out what to build. It's how you find out whether your bet is paying off. The experiments aren't inputs to the decision. They're early returns on the stake.

This distinction matters when the signal comes back wrong. A decision-maker defends the decision. A bettor adjusts the stake.

Scrum won't tell you what to build. Neither will strategic product management. What both offer, done well, is a structured way of running bets and knowing when to fold.
