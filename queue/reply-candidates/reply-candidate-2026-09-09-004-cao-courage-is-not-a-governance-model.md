Every specific complaint here is right: reports built to feed the process, stage gates as ritual, metrics chosen for what's easy rather than what's dangerous. That's a precise description of what a PMO looks like once it's been captured.

Where I'd push back is the fix. Conviction, foresight, and the courage to confront reality asks the same people who currently produce green dashboards to simply decide, one day, to start producing red ones. That's not a governance model, it's a personality requirement, and it's exactly the kind of thing that quietly disappears the first time someone gets punished for going first with bad news.

The PMO didn't become a reporting factory because nobody had courage. It became one because a green dashboard is safe to submit and an accurate one usually isn't, for whoever's name is on it. Bad news is data, but only in an organisation set up to reward the person who delivers it early. Build that, and you barely need the dashboards. Skip it, and the next model dies exactly the way this one did, just with a different colour scheme.
