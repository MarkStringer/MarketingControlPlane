The diagnosis is right and the cure is half a step short. You're correct that the baseline fuels disappointment because stakeholders treat an estimate as a promise. But switching from project to product doesn't remove the bet. It just stops printing the odds on the front page.

The project is a bet. So is the product. The difference is that a project at least has the decency to name a number you can be wrong about, which is the thing everyone hates and also the only thing keeping the conversation honest.

The real fix isn't a new mindset. It's getting stakeholders to understand that a baseline is a position in a game with incomplete information, not a contract. Deliver the possible, not the fantasy. The mindset that needs to change is the one that reads a forecast as a guarantee.
