The trait that wrecks projects isn't introversion. It's the inability to deliver bad news early.

I've worked with charming, fluent project managers who were a disaster, because charm is very good at keeping a status report green long after it should have gone red. And I've worked with blunt, linear engineers who said this won't work in week two and saved everyone a year.

Mistakes aren't expensive because they happen. They are expensive because of when you find out. Bad news is data. The person you actually want running a project is the one who can sit in a room and say the uncomfortable true thing while it is still cheap to act on it.

That is a temperament worth training for. It is not the same as being good at small talk.
