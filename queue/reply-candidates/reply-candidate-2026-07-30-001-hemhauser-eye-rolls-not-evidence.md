Agree with nearly all of this and then you lost me on the last line.

If eye-rolls are proof you are doing something right, you have just thrown away the only instrument you had.

Here is the problem with the badge. The PM who tells a sponsor that the date is fiction gets eye-rolls. The PM who has blocked a decision for three weeks because the RAID log is not up to date also gets eye-rolls. From the inside those two feel identical. Both of those people can read this post and take it as confirmation, and one of them is quietly wrecking the project.

I have been the second one more often than I would like, and I did not know it at the time. I thought I was being rigorous.

There is a bigger thing underneath it. Organisations are perfectly happy to have one designated unpopular person. It is much cheaper than dealing with whatever made the truth unsayable in the first place. The awkward thing gets raised, it gets raised by the usual person, everyone makes their face about it, and nothing has to change. That is not courage being rewarded. That is courage being absorbed.

Bad news is data, but only if somebody does something with it. If the only thing your bad news reliably produces is a badge, it was never data. It was a performance, and you were the one paying for it.

The test is not whether they rolled their eyes. The test is whether anything in the plan moved after you spoke.
