"If you make the same decision twice, it needs criteria, not memory" is a good test. I would use it.

But I do not think the reason it does not happen is that nobody has thought of it.

Consider what documenting judgment does to the person doing the documenting. Steps are safe. If you
follow a step and it goes wrong, the step was wrong. That is what a step is for.

Judgment written down stops being judgment and becomes a criterion. A criterion can be checked
against afterwards, in every case where it was applied, including all the ones you were nowhere near.
Write down "we take this on when the margin is above here and the client has already done that", and
you have handed the organisation a rule with your name on it that other people will now use badly.

Undocumented judgment can never be wrong on the record. That is not control. That is the last piece
of cover a senior person has.

So the empty folder is not an oversight. It is a price nobody has offered to pay.

If an organisation genuinely wants the judgment written down, it has to say first who owns the
outcome when the criterion was followed and the thing still failed. I have never once seen that
settled in advance.
