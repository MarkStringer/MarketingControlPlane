Zero missed deadlines across fifty projects is a real number and I believe you. I just think it is telling you something slightly different from what it looks like.

A perfect record is not mainly evidence about how you execute. It is evidence about which work you agree to take on, and about who gets to define where the finish line is.

Five days. Twenty one. Twenty five. Forty three. Those are bets sized so they can be won. That is a compliment, not a complaint. Most of this industry cannot do it.

The project is a bet, and a firm that has never lost one has either been very lucky fifty times running or has been extremely disciplined about the ones it refuses. I know which of those I would rather buy.

I have been on the other side of this. I once told a client we would be finished by a date I had no business promising, and we were, because I quietly let go of two things nobody had yet noticed were in scope. On time. Technically.

That is the risk with selling certainty. Certainty is easy to produce if you are also the person holding the definition of done, and the client usually cannot tell the difference until much later, at which point everyone has moved on.

So here is the thing I think would be worth more than the record. Publish the projects you turned down last quarter and why. That is checkable, it costs you something to say, and it is the actual skill you are selling.

On time is the right thing to sell. Deliver the possible, not the fantasy. The part that makes it believable is the list of jobs you said no to.
