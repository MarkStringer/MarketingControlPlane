The hedge often isn't cowardice. It's confusion.

Before you can say a hard thing simply and early, you have to understand it clearly enough to say it at all. A lot of soft, hedged messages aren't courage failures — they're comprehension failures. The manager hasn't sat with the bad news long enough to know what's actually true, so they describe the cloud rather than the lightning.

Bad news is data. But data has to be read before it can be communicated. The first step isn't finding the courage to say the difficult thing. It's looking at it directly enough that you know what you're actually saying.
