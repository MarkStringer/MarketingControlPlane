Quiet sprint planning is supposed to be the goal. I am not sure it is.

The noise in sprint planning — the "wait, what does this actually mean?" — is the team saying out loud what they already knew privately: that shared understanding has not caught up with the commitment they are about to make. That is data. Bad news is data.

If you fix it by pulling refinement earlier, excellent. But if the planning gets quiet because people have learned it is not worth raising what they do not know, you have moved the uncertainty to mid-sprint, where it becomes a blocker instead of a question.

Loud sprint planning is not a process failure. It is a team that has not yet learned to suppress its uncertainty. That is the version that can actually be fixed.
