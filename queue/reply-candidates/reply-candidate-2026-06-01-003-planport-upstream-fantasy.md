"High-performing teams eliminate friction before it creates delays" is a good description of one type of project.

The other type is where the friction doesn't exist yet at planning time. The regulatory change in month three. The integration that turns out to be harder than the vendor said. The dependency that nobody had visibility of because nobody knew to ask.

Everything on this list of upstream failures is real. Vague objectives, unprotected scope, hidden dependencies — all genuine causes of late delivery. But there's a category of missed deadline that isn't a leadership or discipline failure. It's a bet that didn't pay off. Deliver the possible, not the fantasy — and part of the possible is accepting that some uncertainty was genuine, not negligent.
