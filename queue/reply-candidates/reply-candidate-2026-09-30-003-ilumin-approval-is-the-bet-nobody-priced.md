Every approval is a bet that the capacity will be there. The trouble is that nobody writes down the odds when they place it.

So the schedule ends up carrying the loss. Leadership sees dates slipping, reads it as a delivery problem, and asks the PMO for a better plan. The bet was lost at the approval meeting, but the only place it gets reported is the Gantt chart.

That's why I'd push your point one step earlier. Making the cost of avoiding the choice impossible to ignore is good. Making it visible before the new work is approved is better. Put the capacity number next to the request and say what has to stop for this to start.

If nothing has to stop, the honest answer is that the odds are poor and we're approving it anyway.

Then the slippage is at least a known bet going badly, not a surprise.
