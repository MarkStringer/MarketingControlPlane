"No villains here" is generous, and probably true of the people involved. It's also doing a lot of work to protect the actual finding, which is sitting right there in what you wrote: everyone recognises the same systemic issues, even from different distances, and nothing changes anyway.

That's not a failure to understand each other. UN colleagues read INGOs as underestimating politics because from a UN seat, caution about political leverage is the rational, correct call, they answer to member states who'll still be in the room next year. INGO colleagues read the UN as too removed because from an INGO seat, speed is the rational, correct call, they're the ones standing in front of the person who needs something today. Neither side is confused. Point of view is worth 80 IQ points, and both sides have a good one for where they're standing.

The actual gap isn't comprehension. It's that nobody occupies a seat that has to answer for both time horizons in the same conversation, so the argument about who "gets it" just runs on a loop instead of resolving. Coordination becomes choreography because there's no seat where keeping up the choreography would actually cost anyone anything.
