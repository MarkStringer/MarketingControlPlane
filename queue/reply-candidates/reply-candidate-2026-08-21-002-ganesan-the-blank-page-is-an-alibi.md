The 800-row spreadsheet is worth a second look, because I do not think it is there because the vendor loves configuration.

Ask the customer to specify everything and you have moved the risk. If that configuration turns out wrong six months later, it was their spreadsheet, filled in by their people, signed off by their name. The blank page is not a design failure. It is an alibi, and it is a very good one.

Which is why a preset is a much bigger ask than it looks. "Based on companies like yours, here's what we recommend" means the vendor is now the one who is wrong when it does not fit. Every row you fill in on the customer's behalf is a row you own.

That is the real reason playbooks are rare. It is also the reason they work. The vendor is taking the bet back onto their own book, and customers can feel the difference between somebody who has decided and somebody who is covering themselves.

So I would put it slightly differently. Those 800 rows were never a decision fatigue problem. They were somebody else's insurance policy, and the customer was paying the premium in weeks.
