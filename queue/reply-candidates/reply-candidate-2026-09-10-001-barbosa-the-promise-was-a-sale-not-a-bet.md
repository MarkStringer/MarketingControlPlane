The guts question is the wrong frame. This isn't a courage problem, it's an exposure problem.

Nobody who signed a 25-year performance promise in 2014 is the one holding a dead inverter in
2026. The installer who cut corners on the wiring may not exist anymore. The EPC that promised the
panel spec has moved on to the next boom. The asset itself has probably changed hands at least once
since the handshake you mention.

A bet made by someone who won't be there when it resolves isn't really a bet. It's a sale. The
25-year promise wasn't reckless optimism, it was accurate pricing for a promise nobody expected to
have to keep personally.

If the next 100GW is going to survive to 2050, the fix isn't asking the industry to find courage it
never had. It's making sure whoever signs the performance number is still liable for it when the
number comes due.
