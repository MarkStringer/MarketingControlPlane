"Navigating ambiguity" covers two very different jobs.

One is managing ambiguity: tracking what is unclear, flagging it in status reports, facilitating workshops about it. AI is already reasonably good at this, and getting better.

The other is resolving ambiguity: making the decision that turns "we don't know yet" into "this is what we are doing." That requires authority, credibility, and enough of an opinion about the problem to be useful when the information runs out.

The project managers who are genuinely irreplaceable are the ones doing the second job. The ones who have been describing their value as "navigating" uncertainty have a more complicated case to make than they might think.
