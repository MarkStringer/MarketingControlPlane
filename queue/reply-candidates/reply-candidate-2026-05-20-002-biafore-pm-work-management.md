The dependency distinction is real. A tool that models the critical path knows what you told it. It doesn't know the dependency that doesn't exist yet because the requirement that creates it hasn't been discovered.

Modelling dependencies in a tool beats doing it in your head. But a fully-specified dependency model is a map of what was known at the time it was drawn. Most projects don't fail because the tool missed a dependency you entered. They fail because of the dependency on something nobody in the room knew existed.

True project management, as distinct from sophisticated record-keeping, is the practice of finding out what you don't know yet before it finds you.
