The formula is right. The problem is upstream of it.

EAC and TCPI produce reliable forecasts when the inputs are reliable. Actual cost needs to reflect what the project has actually spent, and earned value needs to accurately represent how much work is genuinely done, not what the team reported to avoid a difficult conversation in the status meeting.

In most projects that overrun badly, the arithmetic worked fine. The numbers fed into it were quietly optimistic. By the time the gap between the EAC and the real forecast became visible, there was no good time to say so.

Data-driven forecasting requires an organisation where surfacing accurate data is rewarded, not just an organisation that runs the formula.
