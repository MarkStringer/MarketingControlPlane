The PM you described as "a paid following-up machine" — that's not a character flaw. That's what happens when you have accountability for a project but no authority over the people doing the work.

If you can't direct, you have to ask. If you have to ask, you have to chase. If you have to chase, you become the person who keeps sending the same email. The role produced the behaviour.

The one PM you rated highly had learned the technology and took responsibility for outcomes. That works too — but it only works if the organisation lets you. Most don't. Most organisations want someone who can absorb the friction of a failing project without actually doing anything that might surface the real problem.

The question isn't really whether most project managers are good or bad. It's whether the job as usually set up is actually doable.
