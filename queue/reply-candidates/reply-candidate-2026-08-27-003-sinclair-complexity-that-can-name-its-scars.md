The heckler is wrong and also worth listening to, which is an annoying combination.

Wrong on the facts, obviously. But the answer given here is a list of the hard parts, and I have watched that answer lose the room more times than I can count.

Here's the thing about lists of hard parts. Every expensive undertaking on earth can produce one. I have sat in rooms where a team justified eighteen months and a great deal of money with a list exactly this shape, every line of it true, and the work underneath was still mostly waste. The list proves the thing is complicated. It does not prove that any particular item on it needs to be there.

And that is the actual question being asked, underneath the rudeness. How hard can it be really means which of these could you drop.

Most industries cannot answer that. Ask why a given sign off exists and you get precedent, and then you get the person who has been there longest, and they do not know either. The step is there because it was there last time. That is complexity nobody can account for, and the suspicion it attracts is completely earned.

Clinical research can answer it, and better than almost anyone.

The consent process, the safety reporting, the monitoring, the reason a site cannot simply be trusted to mark its own homework. None of that is process weight. It is scar tissue. Every serious piece of it went in after something went wrong, and it is possible to say what, and when, and to whom.

That is a different kind of complicated, and it is the whole answer. Not that the work is hard, but that the machinery has a reason attached to each part, and the reasons are on the record, and most of them are the names of people who were harmed before anybody thought to write the rule.

Say that to the heckler rather than the list. The list invites him to think you enjoy the difficulty. The reasons make him ask which of them he would like to volunteer for.

An orchestra with a changing score is a good line, but it undersells you. Nobody died to produce the second movement.
