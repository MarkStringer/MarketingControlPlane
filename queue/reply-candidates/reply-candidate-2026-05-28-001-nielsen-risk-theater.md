The phrase "risks logged, discussed, then parked" is doing a lot of work here.

The person who raises the flag owns the consequence. The delay, the renegotiation, the awkward sponsor conversation. The person who parks it stays on the plan, looks like a team player, and hopes someone else is standing nearest the fire when it goes up.

Risk registers become theater when the professional cost of acting is higher than the professional cost of silence. That's not a governance failure. It's a bad-news problem. Most organizations haven't built a place where bad news is actually welcome.
