There's something honest buried in this. Whether a change is "welcome" has nothing to do with your methodology and everything to do with who pays for it. On cost-plus, changes are delightful. On fixed price, the same request is a threat. Same change, same client, different contract.

Which tells you scope creep was never really a scope problem. The project is a bet, and the contract decides whose money is on the table when someone asks for one small thing at the end.

And the end is exactly when they ask, because that's when refusing costs you the most. You have a relationship to protect and a sign-off to collect. Paying for goodwill with free scope feels cheap in the moment. But you're teaching the client what asking costs, and the answer is nothing.
