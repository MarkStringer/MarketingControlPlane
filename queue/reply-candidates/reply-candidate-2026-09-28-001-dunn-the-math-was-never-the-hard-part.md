The ratios are necessary and everybody in project delivery already half knows they're not sufficient. Most of us just don't act on it.

Hit the budget. Hit the schedule. Hit the scope percentage. Sign off. That's the project, as far as the plan is concerned. Whether anyone can actually use what got built, whether it does the thing it was funded to do, was never one of the numbers anybody was tracking.

Your line about the pro forma is the whole argument in one sentence. The math tells you the building is compliant. It has nothing to say about whether it's a place.

Most failed projects fail the same way a badly designed mixed-income development fails. Not by missing the plan. By hitting the plan exactly and delivering something nobody actually wanted, because the plan was never measuring the goal, only a proxy for it.

Deliver the possible, not the fantasy. The fantasy is the spreadsheet that balances. The possible is whether anyone wants to live there once the keys are handed over.
