Most project teams I've watched weren't moving too slowly. They were moving too confidently in the wrong direction.

Tom's question — are we working quickly enough or building velocity? — is the right one. Activity isn't progress. Moving isn't the same as getting somewhere.

But the harder question is whether the destination was ever achievable.

In most projects that go seriously wrong, the team had reasonable velocity. The sprint reviews were fine. Progress was being made. The goal itself — the actual deliverable, under the actual constraints — was what was wrong. The brief was aspirational rather than real. Someone told a story to get funding. Nobody corrected it because correcting it would have stopped the project.

Velocity toward a realistic goal is useful. Velocity toward a fantasy just gets you to failure faster.
