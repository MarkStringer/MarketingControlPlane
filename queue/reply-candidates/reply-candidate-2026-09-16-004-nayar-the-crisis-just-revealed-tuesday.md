The three points read like character flaws: abandonment, dishonesty, hubris. Only the third one is
really a choice made in the crisis. The other two get decided months earlier.

You cannot manufacture honesty the day the cameras arrive if the organisation has spent the previous
two years training people to soften the weekly number before it reaches a leader. Crisis
communication is not a separate skill switched on for the occasion. It is whatever habit of handling
bad news was already running, now under a spotlight.

Bad news is data, and an organisation either practises treating it that way on an ordinary Tuesday
or it does not. The airline in the storm was not lacking a crisis blueprint. It was revealing what
its Tuesday reporting had been doing all along.
