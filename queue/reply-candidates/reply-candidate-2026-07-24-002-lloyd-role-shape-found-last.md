The split makes sense on paper. The bit that does not survive contact is that you have to make the call at the start, and which kind of work you have got is the thing you find out last.

Everything looks like a clean series of large deliverables from the outside. That is what business cases are for. You discover it was really a product problem, or that the deliverables were three unrelated projects in a trench coat, about six months in, by which point you have already hired for the shape you guessed at and put it on an org chart.

I would also say the badge matters less than what the week actually consists of. A Scrum Master inside an organisation that has already promised a date to a regulator spends their time negotiating with people outside the team about what done means and what gets dropped. That is project management. It does not stop being project management because the job title says otherwise, and it does not get any easier because the team is running two week sprints.

All projects are swamps. The useful question is not which delivery shape you have got, it is how much of the job is going to happen outside the team, because that is the part no framework staffs for and no certification covers.

Most projects should be products. Most of them will not be, because somebody has already told somebody else a date.
