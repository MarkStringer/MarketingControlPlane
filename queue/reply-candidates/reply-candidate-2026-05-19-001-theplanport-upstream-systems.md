The claim that root causes live upstream is right. But the reason they live there matters.

Most planning systems aren't weak in the sense of being badly designed. They're optimised for producing plans that look good. Everyone in the room usually knows the estimates are aggressive and the dependencies are fragile. The system's job is to convert those signals into something legible and non-threatening.

Bad news is data. The question is whether the upstream system is built to receive it. A project can have strong governance, good templates, and regular review gates and still fail to surface the thing that will sink it, because surfacing that thing has a cost.

Improving planning processes without changing the political context they operate in doesn't fix the root cause. It just produces better-looking bad plans.
