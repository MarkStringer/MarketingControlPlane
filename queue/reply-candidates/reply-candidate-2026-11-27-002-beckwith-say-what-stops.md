It's the third team I'd worry about, the one wondering whether months of work still matter.

They have spotted what isn't there. The decision said what is going to happen and didn't say what is going to stop. "What stays the same?" gets near it, but that's the comfortable half.

A new priority is pleasant to agree to. Saying which existing work now comes second means naming somebody's project, quite possibly belonging to somebody in the room. Leave it out and everyone can feel aligned. The argument then travels downstairs, to people with much less power to settle it.

So I'd add "what are we no longer doing" and put it at the top. A leadership team that can't answer that hasn't finished the meeting. They've only left the room.
