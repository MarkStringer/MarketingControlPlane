Sequencing as the order of decisions, not trades, is exactly right, and better put than most of what gets written about this project.

Here's the bit that tends to go missing from the lesson. Nobody starts building before the hard problem is solved because they believe complexity resolves itself on site. They start because somebody, somewhere, has already made a commitment that depends on visible progress by a date, and by the time that's true, undoing the commitment costs more than absorbing the eventual redesign. Clarity before commitment is good advice for the engineer holding the drawings. It's advice nobody upstream of the engineer particularly wants to hear, because for them the commitment was never really about the roof.

That's the part "the field absorbed the consequences" undersells. The field didn't just absorb bad sequencing. It absorbed a bet that had already been placed by someone who was never going to be the one pouring concrete twice.
