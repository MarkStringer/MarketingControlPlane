Her advice is to "earn the right to speak into their world by showing you truly understand their pain," and her thesis is that these objections are valid and represent opportunities rather than obstacles.

You've got the direction right, but I'd push it further. The pushback isn't a hurdle on the way to your initiative. It's the cheapest information you'll get on whether the initiative is any good.

The person objecting is usually closer to the actual work than the person who drew the plan, and often has less power. Which means they've weighed the cost of staying quiet and decided the objection was worth the risk anyway. That isn't difficulty. That's someone handing you data most people would have swallowed.

The trap sits inside your third point. "Earn the right to speak into their world" can quietly turn into better persuasion, and persuasion is how you talk someone out of the one honest read you had access to. Point of view is worth 80 IQ points. Theirs is the point of view you didn't have, which is the whole reason to hear it out before you've decided you're right.
