Scope creep is a symptom, not the disease.

An idea only becomes scope creep if it can walk straight into the project unchallenged. And it can do that when nobody agreed what the project was actually for in the first place. If you never said out loud what you were staking and what counts as winning, then every good idea is in scope by default, because there is nothing to measure it against.

Culture and rules help at the edges. But the real work is earlier and more uncomfortable than setting expectations. It's getting everyone to agree what the bet is, and to accept that agreeing to it means saying no to a pile of things that are genuinely good ideas. Deliver the possible, not the fantasy. A project that says yes to every great idea isn't ambitious. It's undefined.
