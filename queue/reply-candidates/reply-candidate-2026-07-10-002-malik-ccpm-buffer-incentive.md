The single buffer only works if people tell you the truth when they estimate. That's the whole mechanism, and it isn't a property of the schedule.

Ask someone for a best case and they don't hear "best case." They hear the question they have always been asked, which is "what number will you be held to?" Padding isn't ignorance about statistics. It's a rational answer to an asymmetry. Nobody has ever been punished for coming in under a padded estimate, and everybody remembers the person who blew an honest one. Move the padding out of the tasks and into one project buffer and the padding doesn't evaporate. It relocates, or it quietly stops being offered.

Then watch what happens the first time the buffer is half gone a quarter of the way in. The buffer burn chart is the most honest instrument on the project, which is precisely why there will be pressure to explain it away.

What CCPM really does is make the bet visible. The project is a bet, and the buffer is the size of the stake, stated out loud, which is more than most plans manage. It will compress your schedule where the trust it assumes already exists. Where it doesn't, you have built an excellent thermometer for an organisation that punishes people for taking the temperature.
