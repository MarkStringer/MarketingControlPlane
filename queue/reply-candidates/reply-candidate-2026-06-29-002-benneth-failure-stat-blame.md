"70% fail because of poor project management practices" is a very comforting number, because it quietly says the projects themselves were fine and only the running of them was bad.

Most of the failures I've sat in weren't good bets run badly. They were bad bets that no amount of communication or risk register was ever going to rescue. The business case had promised a benefit the world was never going to hand over. Calling that a practice problem keeps the bet itself above suspicion, which is exactly why people like the framing.

Bad news is data. A statistic that locates 70% of failure inside the project manager is the opposite of bad news. It protects the people who chose the project, and sends the person who ran it on another leadership course.

Before you fix the practices, check whether the thing was ever a fair bet. Most of the dead ones weren't.
