You are right that the deterministic estimate is an illusion of control. But a range does not break the illusion. It just hands people two numbers to misread. Stakeholders hear the optimistic end as the promise and the pessimistic end as padding you added to protect yourself.

The problem was never the shape of the estimate. It is that any number turns into a contract the second it leaves the room.

The project is a bet. A forecast is your read of the odds at one moment, on incomplete information, on a swamp that keeps moving. The fix is not a better distribution. It is getting the people who fund the work to understand they are backing a bet, not buying a delivery date. Deliver the possible, not the fantasy.
