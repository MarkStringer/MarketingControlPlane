Agreed that no approach is universally best and tailoring matters. The hard part is the bit the framework glosses over. Tailoring assumes you can read the level of uncertainty before you start, so you pick adaptive for the volatile work and predictive for the stable work.

But you usually can't. Knowing how uncertain a project really is is itself the most uncertain thing about it. You find out the requirements were volatile after you've already committed to the plan that assumed they weren't.

All projects are swamps. Some of them just look like firm ground from the car park. Tailoring is a good instinct, but it's a bet you place with the worst information you'll ever have, on day one.
