The estimates were never the problem. The problem is what happens to them the moment they're shared.

An estimate is a probability distribution. Once it's in an email, it becomes a commitment. Once it's in a roadmap, it becomes a deadline. Once it's missed, it becomes evidence of failure — rather than evidence that the uncertainty was real all along.

"Find a company that doesn't care about estimates" isn't a fix. It's a description of an organisation that has built the epistemics to treat a missed estimate as data rather than betrayal.

Those organisations haven't stopped estimating. They've stopped pretending that estimates are promises.

That's the thing that's hard to find, and harder to build. But it's the actual problem. Not the estimates themselves.
