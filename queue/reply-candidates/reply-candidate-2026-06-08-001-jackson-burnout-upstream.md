The mental state point is true. But it describes the symptom, not the cause.

Most PM burnout I've seen isn't sleep deprivation. It's what happens when you've spent three months trying to deliver something that cannot be delivered, and you know it, and you're running out of road to hide it on.

By the time you're exhausted, you're not managing a project. You're managing the gap between what was promised and what's possible. That is much harder work. And it doesn't get easier with rest.

Here's the thing. The burnout is data. It's telling you something structural is wrong — the estimate was wrong, or the scope was wrong, or the sponsor relationship has broken down, or all three. Treat it as a signal about the project, not a verdict on the person.

A rested PM asked to deliver a fantasy will burn out again. The problem is upstream.
