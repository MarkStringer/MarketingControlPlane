The role distinction is real and worth making.

But both Project Manager and Scrum Master share an assumption worth examining. One has a defined scope; the other has a hypothesis and a backlog. Either way, you either know what you're building, or you have the space to discover it.

The difficult cases fit neither. Government contracts, regulated environments, large capital programmes: the commitment has been made, publicly and often irrevocably, before anyone has enough information to know whether it's deliverable. You can't define scope clearly enough to manage to it, and there's no room to iterate your way to something else.

What those projects need is someone who can manage the gap between what was promised and what becomes possible, honestly, without pretending the gap isn't there. Neither role is built for that. Most of the catastrophic failures that make the news live in that space, and neither framework has a name for the person who should be handling it.
