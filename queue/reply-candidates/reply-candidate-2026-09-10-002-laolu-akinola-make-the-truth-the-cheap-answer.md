This is a better diagnostic instrument than most project status reports get.

You didn't get more honesty out of patients. You got a question that no longer required them to
say a word that means failure. "Fell" means frailty, so people reframe it. "Grabbed onto
something" doesn't cost them anything, so they say it plainly.

Project reporting has the same design fault. Ask "are we on track" and you're asking someone to
say the one word that admits they've failed. Most people find the honest synonym instead:
consolidating, re-baselining, absorbing some risk. None of those cost anything to say. "Behind
schedule" does.

Bad news is data, but only while it's cheap to report. The moment the accurate word carries a
verdict, you stop getting lies and start getting careful non-answers, the same shape as your
patient's "I caught myself." The fix was never asking people to be braver. It was making the truth
the cheap answer.
