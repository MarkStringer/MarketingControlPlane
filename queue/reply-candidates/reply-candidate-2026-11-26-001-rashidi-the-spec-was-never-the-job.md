The detail I'd hang on to is that you did the paperwork properly. Context, requirements, testing, validation. And the work still turned out to contain things nobody had written down.

I don't think that is peculiar to agents. Every requirements document has those holes. We rarely find out, because a person reaches one, shrugs, rings somebody, tries the other calendar, and carries on. The document gets the credit.

The agent doesn't shrug. It stops, or loops, or announces success. (Admittedly I have also met people who do the third one.)

So the two you fired did produce something. They showed you where the swamp is. A description of the work written by people who are good at the work will leave out the parts they are best at, because to them those don't feel like decisions at all.
