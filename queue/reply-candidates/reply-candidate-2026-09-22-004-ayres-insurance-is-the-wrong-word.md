143% of expected ROI, 93% of initiatives meeting objectives. Nobody produces numbers that clean by accident, and nobody produces them in the middle of a project either.

Those stats get measured on projects that finished. Call the ones that hit their numbers "excellent change management" and the causality points whichever way makes the number work. It's much harder to find the number for "we did all of this and it still failed," because that project doesn't get written up as a change management case study. It gets quietly closed.

Insurance is the wrong word for what you're describing. Insurance pays out on outcomes nobody can control. What you're describing is a bet, and bets get retitled as wisdom by whoever wins them.

None of that means adoption doesn't matter. It obviously does. It means the three questions at the end of your post are the right ones, and the two stats at the top are decoration.
