The iceberg is the right picture, and here is why it stays underwater. The software is the part you can put in a spec. The broken process, the data nobody owns, the department that has quietly run on its own spreadsheet for nine years, none of that goes in a document, because writing it down turns it into someone's problem and that someone is usually in the room.

So the organisation buys the thing it can describe and hopes the rest sorts itself out. It does not. All projects are swamps, and an ERP rollout is a swamp with a licence fee attached.

Your closing question is the only one that matters, and almost nobody asks it before signing, because the honest answer might stop the purchase. The decision to buy is a bet that the company can become the kind of company that runs this well. Most of the time that bet gets placed by people who will never have to wade in and find out.
