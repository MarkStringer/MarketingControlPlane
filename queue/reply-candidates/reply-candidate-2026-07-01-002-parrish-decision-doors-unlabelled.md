This works right up until you notice the doors don't come labelled.

The rule assumes you know the cost of the mistake before you make it. On a real project you mostly don't. That is what makes it a project and not a routine. The expensive, irreversible calls are usually the ones that looked cheap and reversible at the time. You find out which door you walked through after it has clicked shut behind you.

So the useful move isn't calibrating your speed to a number you don't have. It's spending a little to find out how big the number is. Buy information early, while it's cheap, precisely because you can't yet tell a two-way door from a one-way one.

Point of view is worth 80 IQ points. Knowing which kind of decision you are actually standing in is most of the game, and that knowledge almost never turns up on time.
