The diagnosis is right. The prescription is half-right.

"Think slow, act fast" addresses sequence: plan carefully, then execute quickly. That's better than the standard approach, which is optimism upfront and crisis management throughout.

But it doesn't fix where the bias actually lives.

The people doing the planning are the same people who want the project to succeed. Which means the plan encodes optimism from day one. More planning with the same bias produces a more detailed, more credible fantasy.

What projects actually need isn't a better plan. It's a plan that treats divergence from plan as information rather than failure. Mechanisms that force reality to intrude during execution, not just checkboxes to clear before it starts.

Bad news is data. If the plan becomes the only source of truth, you've guaranteed the data won't get through.
