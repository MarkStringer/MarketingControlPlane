Babysitting with a dashboard is a good phrase, and the line about decisions living in one person's memory is the expensive one on that list.

I would push back on why the reminders happen, though.

Almost nobody I have ever chased had forgotten. They had ranked. The thing I was asking for was fourth on a list I could not see, behind three things owned by the person who writes their appraisal, and that person is not me.

So the follow-up is not an information problem. It is a bid. Every polite chase is a project manager converting a shortage of authority into social pressure, one message at a time. Clear owners and visible deadlines make the debt easier to point at. They do not change who wins when two claims land on the same person on the same Tuesday.

Here's the thing. The chasing is the only instrument on the project that measures what people actually meant. The tracker records what they agreed to. The number of reminders records where you really sit.

Which means a project where you suddenly stop having to chase anyone has not necessarily got healthier. Something has usually just gone quiet.

Build the system anyway. Just don't expect it to retire the chasing, because the chasing is the bit telling you where you rank.
