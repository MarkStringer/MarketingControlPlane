The STO autopsy is the useful part. Most posts like this stop at "we hit a wall," and you're stuck guessing whether the wall was real or just a bad quarter. You named the exact place it broke: not enough engineers to give every business unit a full squad, and not enough leaders with the P&L range to run one.

Here's the thing though. It's tempting to read that as complexity catching up with you. I don't think it is.

Below a certain size, coordination is free. Someone walks over, asks a question, gets an answer, moves on. Nobody bills for it because nobody has to.

Above that size, every project has been quietly running on credit it never priced. The functional structure didn't get worse at 100 people. It just stopped being subsidised by proximity.

What looks like a scaling crisis is usually an invoice arriving late. The debt was always there. You just didn't have to service it yet.
