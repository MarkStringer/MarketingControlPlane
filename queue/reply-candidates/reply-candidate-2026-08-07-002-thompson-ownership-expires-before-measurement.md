The distinction is clean, and I think the PMOs are right to be nervous anyway.

Transparency and accountability are separable on paper. They come apart much less reliably in the room where the miss actually gets discussed.

Here is what happens in that room. A benefit was declared, tracked and missed. Somebody senior asks the only question anyone ever asks at that point, which is when did we know. And there is exactly one function present that can answer it, because it owns the measurement.

At that moment the PMO is not being asked whether it delivered the benefit. Nobody thinks it did. It is being asked to account for the gap between knowing and saying. That is an accountability question, and it has landed on the party that made the thing visible rather than the party that promised it.

Then there is the timing problem, which I think is the bigger one.

Benefits mature over eighteen months to three years. Sponsors change roles faster than that. So by the time actual value is measurable, the person who chose the initiative and approved the assumptions has been promoted, reorganised, or has gone somewhere else entirely. Your model says they own the outcome. They do, right up to the moment the outcome becomes known, at which point ownership is vacant and the PMO is the only party still standing next to the number.

Ownership that expires before measurement is not ownership. It is a signature.

So the fear you are describing is not a misreading of the operating model. It is a reasonably accurate reading of what happens in that particular organisation to whoever is nearest the bad number. Bad news is data, but it only behaves like data somewhere the person carrying it does not get charged for carrying it, and most PMOs can tell you from experience which kind of place they work in.

Which means the fix is not explaining the distinction more clearly. The distinction is already clear. What is missing is the thing that would make it hold, which is a named benefit owner who is still in post at the point of measurement, recorded at approval, with the handover written down when they move, and the measurement date in their objectives rather than in yours.

Escalating an at risk benefit is transparency if there is somebody left to escalate it to.

If there is not, it is a confession.
