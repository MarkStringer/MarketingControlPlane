The instinct to manage perception before communicating isn't dishonesty. It's processing.

When you're the first person to absorb a piece of bad news, you need to make sense of it before you can say anything useful. Is it real? Is it my fault? Can I fix it quietly before anyone needs to know? By the time the PM decides the answer is "no, this needs to go up," they've usually spent a week quietly trying to make it not true.

Early communication fails not because PMs don't value transparency but because transparency requires saying something clear before you understand it. And saying something unclear looks worse than saying nothing.

The fix isn't openness as a value. It's forcing the uncertain signal out early, while there's still time to respond to it — before the internal processing loop turns a fixable problem into a finished fact.
