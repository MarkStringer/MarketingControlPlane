The diagnosis is right. Leaders reach for the wrong message because they never stopped to ask which kind of bad news this actually is. That's worth the two questions you're giving it.

Here's what I'd add. Both of your questions, can we fix it, where did it come from, are questions about the news. Neither is a question about when you're asking them.

A "Fix It" message delivered the week the problem surfaces and a "Fix It" message delivered eight months after everyone downstream already knew something was wrong are not the same conversation, even with identical wording. The first is leadership. The second is confession dressed as leadership, and teams can usually tell the difference even when they can't name it.

Bad news is data. It's only useful as data while there's still time to act on it. Every one of your four categories can curdle into "Shut It Down" or "Move On" simply by arriving late, no matter how well it's delivered.

Diagnose the message. But diagnose the delay first. That's usually the part nobody wants to name.
