There is a third possibility, which is that it is neither. It is a position.

The list you put under job is not a lower calling that unambitious people settle for. It is what the organisation asked for and is currently paying for. Someone who decides to redefine their role as leadership and business alignment, inside an organisation that wanted the risk log kept up to date, does not become a trusted leader. They become the project manager who stopped keeping the risk log up to date.

I have watched people do exactly this. The reframe is completely sincere and it lands as unreliability.

The split you are describing is real, I just do not think attitude is what drives it. It is authority, and authority is given rather than adopted. Take two people with the same title, the same competence and the same self-image. One of them is in the room when the date gets set. The other is told the date afterwards. The second one is going to produce a great deal of status reporting, not because they see the work as a job, but because that is the only surface they have been handed.

I would also defend the admin. The status report is where bad news either travels or dies, and that is not clerical. It is the most consequential item on your whole list. Most of the projects I have seen go wrong did not go wrong because nobody was leading. They went wrong because something true was known on the ground in March and arrived upstairs in September.

Changing how you define your role is free. Changing what you are allowed to decide is the expensive part, and it is the part that actually moves a career.
