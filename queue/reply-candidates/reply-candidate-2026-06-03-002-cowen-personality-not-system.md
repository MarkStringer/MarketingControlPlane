The certification-versus-personality argument is seductive. It is also the same move organisations make when a project fails — find the person, not the system.

An engaging, enthusiastic PM dropped into a structure with political stakeholders, fragmented authority, fixed budgets, and reporting lines that punish bad news faces the same constraints as a less dynamic one. The personality does not change the information environment.

Certification programmes focus on process because process is teachable, assessable, and defensible in a hiring decision. Selecting for judgment and situational adaptability is harder to standardise. That is not a flaw in the certifications. It is a flaw in how organisations think about risk — in hiring, and in projects.
