Escalation as a feature rather than a bug is right, and the rate limit line is the useful part. Most places are slower than their plan for reasons that have nothing to do with how hard anyone is working.

I want to look at the definition though, because it is doing more work than it looks like it is doing.

Two peers who have both tried, who both agree they are stuck, and who both want it resolved, walking in together. That is a good escalation. It is also the cheap one. Nobody was ever really suppressing that. It costs almost nothing to raise a blockage that the other person will happily confirm is a blockage.

The escalations that get sat on are the ones your rule excludes.

The other team does not think there is a problem. They think you are behind and looking for cover. Going together is not available to you, because going together requires them to co-sign a description of the situation they dispute. So the only honest move left is the one you have just defined as tattling.

Or the blocker is the person you would be escalating to. Or it is that person's idea, which is worse, because now the tie-breaker is a party to the dispute and everyone in the room can see it except, apparently, them.

Or the problem is not that two teams are stuck against each other. It is that the plan is wrong and saying so out loud means somebody senior was wrong in public several months ago.

None of those pass the two-peers-together test. All of them are the ones that quietly cost you the quarter.

Which brings me to willingness.

I do not think companies are rate-limited by how willing people are. Willingness is downstream. People escalate roughly as fast as it is safe to, and the price is not set by the handbook or by anything said at an all-hands. It is set by what visibly happened to the last person who walked in on their own with something unwelcome, and whether they are still on the team.

Everyone knows that number. Nobody has written it down.

So if escalation is slow, asking for more courage is asking for a favour. Changing the price is designing a system. They are not the same intervention and only one of them survives your departure.

Bad news is data, but only where the person carrying it survives contact with the person receiving it. Otherwise it is just a personal risk somebody is being asked to take on the company's behalf, uncompensated, and people are quite good at pricing that.

The metric I would actually watch is not how often people escalate. It is how many things get raised by one person on their own, and what happens to them afterwards.

That second number tells you what the first one is going to be next quarter.
