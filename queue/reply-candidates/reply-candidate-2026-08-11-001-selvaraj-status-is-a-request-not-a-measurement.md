Green, green, green, then red overnight is exactly right, and I would go at it from a slightly different direction, because I do not think the status is a measurement at all.

It is a request.

When somebody writes amber, they are not describing the project. They are asking for something. A decision that has been sitting with someone for three weeks, two more people, a date moved, permission to drop something. Amber is the form you fill in when you want one of those.

Green is what you file when you have nothing to ask for, or when you already know the ask will be refused.

Which means the colour is tracking the expected response, not the health of the work. That is why it can stay green while everyone involved privately knows the date is gone. Nobody is lying. They are answering a different question from the one on the form.

That also explains something about your three leading indicators, which are the right three, and which is why they are never the ones on the report.

A decision still pending has a name on it. So does a dependency nobody confirmed. So does scope that grew without anyone approving it. Report any of those and you have not reported a project condition, you have reported that a specific senior person has not done a specific thing, and you have put a date on how long it has been true.

A slipped date has no name on it. It is weather. Everybody can be sad about it together.

So the lagging indicators are not a failure of measurement technique. They are the only findings that can be published without an accusation attached, and that is why status reports fill up with them.

Which brings me to making yellow safe, where I think the bar is higher than it sounds.

Safe usually gets read as nobody gets shouted at. That is necessary and it is not sufficient. Amber has to buy something. If reporting amber produces a meeting, a request for a recovery plan and a closer look at your numbers, and then produces no decision and no help, then amber cost a week of scrutiny and returned nothing. People stop paying for things that do not deliver. They are not being cowardly, they are being accurate about the price.

The test I would put to an organisation is not whether it says amber is welcome. It is whether anyone in the room can point to a case where somebody raised amber early, was wrong about the size of it, and was demonstrably better off for having raised it. If nobody can name one, the reports are not the problem. They are a correct reading of the incentive.

Bad news is data, but only where somebody is buying data. Where the market wants reassurance, that is what gets produced, and it will be produced skilfully.

The project is a bet, and a bet you cannot report as going badly is one you are going to keep funding until the money runs out and calls it for you.

Your leading indicators would work. That is the awkward part. They are not missing from the report because nobody thought of them.
