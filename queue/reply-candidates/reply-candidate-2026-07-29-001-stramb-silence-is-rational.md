You have named the right symptom and then let everyone off the hook about it.

Silence is the interesting word in this post. Everyone reads it as a communication problem, which is comfortable, because communication problems get solved with better one to ones and a bit more trust. But silence on a project is almost never a failure of communication. It is usually a very accurate piece of communication about something else.

People are quiet because they have watched what happens to the person who speaks.

I have sat in the meeting where a tester says the thing everybody knows, and I have watched the temperature of the room drop, and I have watched that person get quietly moved off the interesting work three weeks later. Nobody wrote that down. Nobody had to. Twenty other people were in the room and they all did the arithmetic.

So when you say read the room, I would go one step further. The room is already telling you the answer. The thing worth reading is who in the room has the least to lose by saying the true thing, because that is the only person you are going to hear it from, and they are usually not the person who knows the most.

Bad news is data. The problem is that in most organisations it is data with a price on it, and the person holding it is the one who pays.

That is not a soft skills gap. It is an incentive, and it sits several levels above whoever is running the standup.
