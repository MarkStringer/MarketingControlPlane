Clarity is necessary. It's not sufficient.

The PM who sees clearly that the project is in trouble but can't change the deadline, stop new scope arriving from three directions, or surface uncomfortable information to the right people — that clarity doesn't help the project. It just makes the failure more visible to the person most accountable for it.

The "clearest thinker in the room" framing assumes the room acts on clear thinking. Most project environments don't. They act on whoever has the authority to force a decision, the political cover to say the uncomfortable thing, or the budget to make a trade-off real.

Point of view is worth 80 IQ points. But only when you're in a position where your point of view changes something.
