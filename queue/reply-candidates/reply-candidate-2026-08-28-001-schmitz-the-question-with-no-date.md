You are right that the question does not get asked, and the line about finance tracking spend, delivery tracking milestones and strategy tracking announcements is the most useful sentence I have read about governance this month.

I would put the cause somewhere slightly different, though, because I think it changes what you do about it.

You say the question has no owner. I think the deeper problem is that it has no date.

Look at what actually triggers anything in a quarterly review. A milestone slips, and it slips on a particular Tuesday. Somebody owns that Tuesday. A budget line goes over, and it goes over in a month with a name. Every item on that agenda arrived there because something happened on a day.

Now try to say when a project stopped fitting the strategy. There is no such morning. Nothing was late. Nobody missed anything. The drift accumulates without ever producing a single event, and an organisation that responds to events will therefore never respond to it.

Which means I would defend the green status, in a way you will not like.

It is not wrong. The project is genuinely on track against the only thing anyone ever signed. And the thing they signed is a written description of what the company wanted last year. Green is an honest answer to a question nobody should still be asking.

Here is the harder bit. You finish by saying the PMO should be the one to ask, because the PMO is sitting in the only seat that can see both halves.

The seat is right. The incentive is not.

A PMO is funded in proportion to the portfolio it administers. Ask your question properly, get an honest answer, and the likely result is that some of the portfolio should not exist. Do that well enough and you have made a persuasive case for needing less PMO. That is a strange thing to volunteer for in the same meeting where next year's headcount gets decided.

So I do not think this is a PMO discipline problem. The person who asks cannot be the person whose budget is the answer.

What I would actually do is give the question the one thing it is missing, which is a date.

Not a standing agenda item, because standing agenda items become slides. A re-approval date. Every project of any size has a day in the calendar where it stops being funded and has to win the money again, against this year's strategy, and against whatever else the company would start today with the same money.

Nothing else changes. But now strategic fit is an event, on a day, with somebody's name on it, and it competes rather than continues.

A project is a bet, and you do not monitor a bet. You re-price it, or you find out much later what you were still holding.
