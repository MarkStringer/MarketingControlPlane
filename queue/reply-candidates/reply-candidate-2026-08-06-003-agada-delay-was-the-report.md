Agreed. The mechanism is the part worth naming, because it has nothing to do with that person's attitude.

Ask what tools they actually had.

They could not write "this design moves operational risk onto my team and nobody has given me the people to carry it" onto the plan. There is no field for that on the plan. There is no line on the RAID log called risk transferred to another department without compensation. There is no slide.

So they used the only instrument they controlled. The speed of their own signature.

The delay was the report. It was accurate, it was well calibrated, and it arrived through the only channel that was open to them. Then the organisation read it as a personality trait.

Point of view is worth 80 IQ points. On day one of that design, exactly one person in the building had priced it correctly, and it was them, because they were standing where the cost was going to land. Everybody with a better title had a worse view. That is not unusual. That is the normal condition, and it is why the most accurate assessment in the room showed up as an obstruction instead of as analysis.

Which brings me to the bit of your story I would sit with a while longer. You found out. Good. You found out weeks late, and only because the delay ran on long enough to get expensive enough to investigate.

So being difficult worked. That is the uncomfortable part. It was a functioning risk management process with a measurable output, and it was the only one available to the person using it.

I would take your closing question and give it a permanent home rather than leaving it as something you arrive at during an escalation. Who is being asked to absorb something in order for this to work, and what have they been given in exchange. Asked at design time, out loud, with names in it.

Until that question lives somewhere in the paperwork, the person everybody calls difficult is your risk register.
