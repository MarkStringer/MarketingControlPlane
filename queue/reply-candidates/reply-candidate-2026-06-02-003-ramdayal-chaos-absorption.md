Protecting the team from chaos is a reasonable short-term response. As a long-term strategy, it creates the next set of problems.

If the PM absorbs the uncertainty and delivers calm to the team, the team stops knowing how bad things are. The sponsor stops knowing. The person who could reallocate budget, adjust scope, or escalate to the executive level — they get managed rather than informed. The project keeps moving toward a wall.

The PM who carries the most weight isn't always the most effective one. Sometimes they're the last person standing between the organisation and information it urgently needs to act on.

Burnout in this model isn't just a personal cost. It's often the point where the truth finally arrives — too late and all at once.
