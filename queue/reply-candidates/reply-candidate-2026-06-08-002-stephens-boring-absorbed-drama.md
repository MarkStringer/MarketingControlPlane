Boring projects are not uneventful projects.

A project that looks boring from the outside — steady updates, no escalations, no late nights — often had enormous drama absorbed somewhere inside it. The risk that got identified early and quietly managed. The conversation with the sponsor that nobody wanted to have, that happened anyway. The estimate that got pushed back on before it went into the plan.

The boring is not the absence of drama. It's drama that got managed before it became visible.

What Stephens is really describing is a project that started with honest expectations. The exciting ones usually started with a fantasy. The drama was always in there — it just got deferred rather than absorbed, and eventually it surfaces all at once and calls itself a crisis.
