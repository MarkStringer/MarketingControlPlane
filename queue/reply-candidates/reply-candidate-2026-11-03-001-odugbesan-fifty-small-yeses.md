The trade-off sentence is the right fix, and I'd only add why it works. Each yes was a bet placed without anyone writing down what was being wagered, so nothing could look wrong until the sum arrived at week six.

Naming the price turns an agreeable reflex into a recorded decision, and it hands the choice to the person who owns the budget and the date. That is the part that matters more than the phrasing.

The fifty yeses were never the bad news. The bad news was that the timeline hadn't moved while the scope had, and that was visible from the second yes.
