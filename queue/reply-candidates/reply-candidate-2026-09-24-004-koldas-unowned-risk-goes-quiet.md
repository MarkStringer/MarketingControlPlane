Most safety writing stops at "the procedure wasn't followed," as if the procedure existing was ever going to be enough. Yours goes one step further, into why it wasn't followed, and that's the part worth sitting with.

The reboiler replacement wasn't unowned because nobody cared. It was owned by exactly one person, correctly, right up until the day he left. Nothing in the system converted "this engineer knows this is urgent" into "the organisation knows this is urgent." The knowledge was real. It just wasn't anywhere except in his head.

That's the failure. Not a missing form. A risk that was tracked as a fact about a person instead of a fact about the company.

And the detail about the other 78 projects getting funded while this one sat is the part people will skim past, but it's the sharpest line in the post. Unowned risk doesn't lose a vote. It doesn't come up for a vote at all. It just goes quiet, and quiet gets mistaken for fine.

If a project can only make noise while a specific person is still in the building, you don't have a project plan. You have a countdown nobody's watching.
