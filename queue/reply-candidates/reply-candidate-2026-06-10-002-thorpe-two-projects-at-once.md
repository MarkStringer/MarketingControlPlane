Running two projects simultaneously is exhausting. Most PMs don't realise that's what they're doing.

When you start managing how sponsors perceive a project — filtering, softening, timing the bad news — you're running a second project: the managed version. It exists alongside the real one, with its own narrative and its own version of where things stand.

The managed version consumes time and attention. It also means the people making decisions are working from the wrong data. By the time the gap between the two versions becomes impossible to hide, the options for fixing the real project are much worse.

The lesson isn't about trust. It's about decision windows. Bad news delivered late is expensive — not because it damages a relationship, but because it removes options.
