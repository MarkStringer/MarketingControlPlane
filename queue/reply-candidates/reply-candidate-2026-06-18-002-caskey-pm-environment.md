PM #1 being 'a paid following-up machine' is the most accurate description of bad project management I've seen. But I'd push back on where the blame lands.

Following up is what you do when you have accountability without authority. If the PM can't change the deadline, stop new scope arriving from above, or actually resource the work correctly, then following up — chasing, escalating, reporting — is the complete set of tools available to them.

The good PM in your fourth example hired the team, managed logistics, owned results. Those are signs of someone with real authority over the work. Most PMs don't have that. They have a title that implies it and a position that doesn't.

Excellent PMs aren't rare because the right individuals are rare. They're rare because the conditions that make good PM work possible are rare.
