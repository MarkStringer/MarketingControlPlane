You name the actual problem in one line and then walk past it. "The plan doesn't match reality, but no one has permission to say so out loud."

Permission. Not features, not intents. Permission.

Because here is the awkward thing about the feature list. It is a bad plan and a very good alarm. It fails out loud, on a date, in a way that anyone in the building can check. We said March. It is April. There is nothing to interpret.

A strategic intent can absorb almost any twelve months and still read as on track. Which is the point, and is also the cost. You have made the plan able to flex, and in the same move you have made it very hard to prove wrong.

The project is a bet. A bet you cannot lose isn't a bet, it's a mood.

So I don't think intents create permission. I think the quarterly check-in does, and that is the bit worth copying from Oscar Health. It is a date in the diary when saying "the assumption broke" is the expected contribution rather than an act of disloyalty.

Which is a low bar and still rare. If telling people in March that the plan is wrong costs someone their standing, they will wait until December, and it will not matter in the slightest whether the thing they were handed in January was a feature or an intent.
