His claim is that whether you are tight on time or planning a new project, you should "avoid falling into the trap of treating all scope the same", and that using an effort versus value scale to understand what scope to work on and what to park or move out of scope is "a great way to get early alignment on what matters." He describes a quadrant mapping scope items on two axes, value from low to high and effort from low to high, and recommends doing high value low effort items first, then high value items regardless of effort, and removing or descoping items that take significant effort for minimal value. The grid is fine. The trouble is that value is not a property of the scope item.

It is a property of whoever is asking for it. Take any item you have put in the low value corner and go and find the person whose name is on it. To them it is not low value. It is the reason they agreed to the project in the first place. You have not discovered that their thing is worth less, you have decided that their claim on the project is weaker than somebody else's, and that may well be the right call, but it is a political judgement wearing a lab coat.

I am not against the lab coat. Making a political decision look like a measurement is often the only way to get it made at all.

It just helps to know that is what you are doing, because it changes who you need to talk to before the workshop rather than after it.

The other axis has a problem too. Effort estimates are least reliable exactly where the work is least understood. So the high value low effort corner, the one everybody gets excited about, is partly a list of things nobody has looked at closely yet. On a time-crunched project the cheap items have a habit of turning out not to be cheap, and they do it late, which is the expensive way round.

What actually holds when the pressure arrives is not the quadrant. It is whether the people who lost scope were in the room while they were losing it. Alignment is not agreement about a diagram. It is a specific person saying out loud, in front of witnesses, that they can live without their thing.

Everything else gets relitigated in week nine.
