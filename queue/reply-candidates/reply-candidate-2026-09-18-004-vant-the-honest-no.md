The "willing but unaware" pattern is real, and worth naming, because it does not look like resistance and gets treated like it anyway.

But there is a third group this framing skips, and it is the one that matters more. People who tried the tool, weighed the switching cost against what it actually saved them, and correctly decided it was not worth it for that particular task. That is not resistance and it is not ignorance. It is an accurate verdict on a bad bet.

Enablement will always produce a number that looks like progress, because showing someone a capability and getting them to try it once is easy. Whether it was worth doing is a separate question, and it might be the one low adoption is already answering honestly.

Before running the "show me the part of your week you dread" session, it is worth asking a harder question first: for this specific task, is the bet actually good? If nobody in the room can answer that, the low adoption number is not a training gap. It is the only honest data point anyone has produced.
