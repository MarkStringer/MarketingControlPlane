One PM who can prototype over ten who can run a workshop. Agreed. But be careful what you think the prototype buys you.

A working prototype in the scoping meeting doesn't remove the bet. It moves it earlier. That's genuinely valuable. You find out the model can't do the thing in week one instead of month six. But the project is still a bet, and now the person placing it is whoever built the fastest demo.

The reason most AI projects fail isn't the backlog grooming. It's that nobody in the room can tell when the prototype is lying to them. Something that works on three cherry-picked inputs is a fantasy with better production values than a spec.

So yes, hire the PM who can build. Just make sure they can also read what the build is not telling them. Deliver the possible, not the demo.
