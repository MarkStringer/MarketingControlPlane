Agreed that a project can land on time and on budget and still be worthless. That part is right.

But I don't think projects solve the wrong problem because nobody did the analysis. In my experience somebody nearly always asked what problem we were solving. They asked it properly, they wrote it down, and it went into the business case.

That is where it stops being a question and becomes a commitment.

Here's the thing. Once the answer is funded, the analysis turns into the thing everyone is measured against. So when the problem turns out to be different six months in, and it usually is, the requirements document is not the remedy. It is the obstacle. Changing it means telling someone the basis on which they released the money has moved.

All projects are swamps. The problem moves while you are standing in it.

What projects are short of is not a better answer at the start. It is somebody with the standing to say the answer changed, after the money is already out the door.
