You are right that the acronym is now a coin flip, and right that plenty of PMOs do slide down the value scale. I would just be careful which way the causation runs.

The name is not doing the damage. It is reporting it.

A PMO turns into an administrative burden the moment it is given the duty to collect status and no power to act on what it collects. After that, everything it produces is a report, and a report that cannot change a decision is overhead. Nobody in that building is reacting to three letters. They are reacting to three years of being asked for updates by a function that never once stopped anything.

Rename it and you reset the clock. The slide starts again at the same gradient, because the missing thing was never the label.

Here's what I like about Investment Delivery Office, though. The word investment implies you are allowed to decide not to invest. Take that name honestly and the first job of the function is to fold a hand where everyone can see it. Kill something real, early, and say out loud why.

Do that and nobody much cares what it is called. Skip it and the new name gets decoded inside two quarters, and you are shopping for a third one.

The test was never the title. It is what happens to bad news on the way up. If it gets softened before it reaches the sponsor, all you have bought is an administrative burden with better branding.
