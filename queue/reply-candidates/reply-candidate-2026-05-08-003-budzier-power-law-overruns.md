The power-law finding makes sense if you think of projects as swamps rather than ponds.

A pond has visible edges. Normal distribution assumes a pond: most overruns are small, the rare large one is the outlier. But complex IT projects are swamps. The edges are not visible. What looks like solid ground can drop you several feet deeper without warning.

The cascade effect — one dependency failing and pulling down others — is not an exceptional failure mode. It is swamp physics. The interconnections were always there. You just could not see them from the bank.

This is why the question is not "will we overrun?" It is "how deep can this go before anyone decides to stop?"
