This is every steering committee I've ever sat in, just with a sales label on it.

We add people to a decision thinking we're spreading the risk. We're actually removing the owner. A decision needs one person for whom being wrong costs something. Put fifteen people in the room and that person quietly disappears, because the cost of being wrong gets shared down to nothing while the cost of being the one who called it stays whole.

So the room does the thing that feels like progress and carries no personal risk. Alignment. Next steps. Follow-ups. All of it produces artefacts and none of it produces a decision, because a decision is the one output with a name attached.

Inaction wins because inaction has no author. Nobody gets fired for the call that was never made.

If you want a committee to decide, shrink the number of people who can say no and name the single one who has to say yes. All projects are swamps, and a big enough room is just how a swamp votes to stay wet.
