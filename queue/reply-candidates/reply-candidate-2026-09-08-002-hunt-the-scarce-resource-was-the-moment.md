You didn't gain credibility by listening. You gained it by asking before the design was signed off, when being wrong cost three days instead of your reputation.

The same operator raising the same objection after your firm had issued the drawings isn't field experience any more, it's a claim. Someone has to own the delay, and once concrete is poured, "let's collaborate" turns into "whose fault was this" fast. The information in his head didn't change between those two moments. What changed was the cost of being told.

This is the part construction management doesn't put on a slide. The ground knowledge was always there, sitting in the cab of a D6, available for free. Point of view is worth 80 IQ points, but only if you go looking for it while it's still cheap to be wrong.

Every time you were wrong in this business, you say, it was because you ignored someone who knew better. I'd bet every time you were right, you asked before anyone had to be blamed for the asking.
