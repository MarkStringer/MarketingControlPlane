Your third sign is the one that explains the other four.

Information travels toward whoever can act on it. Reports go up to the donor because the donor can stop the money. Communities get the survey and none of the findings because nothing they say can stop anything. The channel follows the consequence.

That's why "participatory" turns into data collection. If the answer can't change a decision, it isn't feedback, it's noise with a nice cover page.

Bad news is data, but only where someone is allowed to act on it. So the test I'd run is simple. Name one decision that changed last quarter because a community said something unwelcome. If nobody can, you have a reporting system, not a listening one.
