When the certification well dries up, that is usually a signal that the product was solving the wrong problem.

Scrum certification sold the idea that project failure is primarily a process problem. Learn the framework correctly, avoid the anti-patterns, and things go better. That argument is reasonable when the environment is stable and the dominant failure mode is poor execution.

But when environments become more uncertain — and AI is making them much more uncertain — teams stop asking "are we doing the process right?" and start asking "is this the right process at all?" The Scrum Masters are correct that most implementations are broken. But even a perfectly executed Scrum is still a bet that iterative delivery into a knowable product space is the right approach.

The sentiment that Scrum doesn't work in the age of AI may not be as flimsy as it looks. It might be the market correctly updating on what the framework was always unable to promise.
