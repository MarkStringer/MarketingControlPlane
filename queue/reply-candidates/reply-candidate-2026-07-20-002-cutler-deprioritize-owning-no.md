Right. And the reason most teams never actually deprioritize isn't that they're missing a framework for it. It's that saying no out loud is a bet with your name on it.

A backlog is where organisations go to avoid saying no. "It's on the list" feels like a decision without being one. Nobody has to stand up and say we are choosing not to do this, which conveniently means nobody can be blamed later when it turns out we should have.

Real prioritization is deciding who to disappoint, and when. That's a visible, owned, uncomfortable call. The framework doesn't make it for you. At best it tells you the bill has come due.

The project is a bet. So is every no you keep refusing to say.
