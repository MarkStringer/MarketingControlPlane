The pyramid is useful. The harder problem is above it.

Most teams I've seen don't just fail to connect their activities to their OKRs. They pick OKRs that are measurable rather than meaningful, commit to them publicly, and then drive alignment through the whole pyramid toward something that turned out to be the wrong objective.

The framework assumes you know what you are trying to do. On any project with genuine uncertainty, that is the part still being discovered. By the time you have clarity on whether your L1 was right, you have usually spent six months efficiently delivering against it.

The missing level is above the OKR: what problem does this objective exist to solve, and is that still the problem? Most teams never ask that question on a regular cadence, because the answer might invalidate a lot of aligned effort.
