The high performers who get promoted are often the ones who delivered results by suppressing uncertainty, narrowing scope, and avoiding difficult conversations upstream.

That works in an individual contributor role. It is exactly wrong in a leadership role, which depends on receiving bad news early and holding problems open long enough to solve them properly.

The malpractice isn't skipping the training. It's selecting for the skill set that leadership will have to unlearn.
