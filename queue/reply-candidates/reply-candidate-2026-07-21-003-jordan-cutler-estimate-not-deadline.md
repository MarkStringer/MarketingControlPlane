The move that does the damage is silent, and it only goes one way. An estimate is a probability. A deadline is a promise. Nobody ever converts a promise back into a probability, but everyone quietly converts the estimate into the deadline the moment it leaves your mouth.

Being explicit up front helps. Being just as explicit later matters more. The estimate was true given what you knew that morning. Three weeks in you know more, and almost nobody goes back to update the number, because reopening it feels like admitting failure instead of reporting new data.

So the fantasy hardens. The date set on the day of least knowledge becomes the one everyone is held to.

Deliver the possible, not the fantasy. Which means saying, out loud and more than once, when the possible has moved.
