"We're three members down, so the project is screwed" is the most useful sentence anyone will say on that project all week.

So of course it never makes the report.

The translation isn't a communication failure. It's a rational one. People soften "screwed" into "amber, some resourcing pressure" because on most projects the person who says the blunt version out loud gets a talking-to before the person who caused the shortfall does. The honesty gets punished at exactly the moment it would still have been cheap to act on it.

Bad news is data. A status report that filters out the word "screwed" has thrown away the one data point that could have changed the outcome. You don't fix that by asking people to be braver. You fix it by making the blunt sentence safe to say, so it arrives while three-down is still recoverable and not after it's terminal.
