"Most project managers confuse stubbornness with leadership." I'd put it the other way round. Most project managers aren't allowed to kill anything.

Your post reads like someone who could. Either the authority came with the job or you had enough standing that nobody fancied stopping you. That isn't the position most people reading it are in, so what you're describing as a judgment problem lands on them as a permission problem, and no amount of recognising dead ends early fixes that.

The persistence lecture isn't hustle culture either. It comes from whoever signed the business case. Their name is on the bet. Cancelling doesn't cost the company money it has already spent, it costs that specific person an admission, in public, with a date on it. "See it through" is what that sounds like when it comes out of their mouth.

So if you can't cancel it yourself, the move isn't announcing the project is dead. It's making it undeniable. Get the evidence in front of enough people at once that carrying on requires somebody to say out loud that they are ignoring it. Bad news is data, and data is what you use when you haven't got authority.

You rarely get to kill a project. You get to make it impossible to keep pretending it's alive.
