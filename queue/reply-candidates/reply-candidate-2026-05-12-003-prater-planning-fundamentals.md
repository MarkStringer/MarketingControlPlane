The fundamentals don't change. But which ones they are is worth questioning.

The Seven Ps assumes you can plan. For most interesting projects, you can't — not fully. You find out how many hours you need when the integration turns out to be a ground-up rebuild. You find out what material is needed when the survey proves wrong. The information that would make the plan correct often only exists after the work starts.

Proper Prior Planning Prevents Piss Poor Performance — but only when the information is available before you start.

The fundamental that doesn't change is this: the project is a bet, made on incomplete information, against an uncertain future. Planning doesn't change that. Good planning helps you recognise when the bet is going wrong, faster.

"Constant monitoring to prevent variances" isn't prevention. It's earlier detection. That's genuinely valuable — but it's a different claim, and the distinction matters. Prevention suggests the problem was avoidable. Earlier detection suggests the problem was coming regardless, and your job is to see it clearly enough to respond.
