Fixing a bug has a visible output. Managing a dependency doesn't.

New project managers reach for team members' tasks because the actual PM work — holding uncertainty, tracking what's fragile, making sure the right information reaches the right people — produces nothing you can point to at the end of the day. So the concrete thing wins. It's not a discipline problem. It's a category problem: nobody has made the output of project management legible enough to feel productive.

The cure isn't just "stop doing other people's work." It's working out what you're actually producing when you do the job well. When you can describe your output — a risk surfaced early, a dependency clarified before it became a blocker, a stakeholder updated with something accurate — the abstract work starts to feel like work. Until then, someone else's task will always look more real than yours.
