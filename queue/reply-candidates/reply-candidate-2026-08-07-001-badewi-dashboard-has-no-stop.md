The four views are right, and I would still expect the dashboard to be decorative, for a reason that sits upstream of anything you can fix in the design.

Look at where the target number came from.

It was not an estimate. It was the number that got the money released. Somebody worked out what the investment committee needed to see and then worked backwards to it. Eighteen per cent because twelve per cent does not clear the hurdle rate. Two point one million because two million sounds rounded and made up.

That is not dishonesty, and I am not accusing anyone. It is what a competitive funding round asks people to produce. Every business case in that room is doing the same thing, so the one that does not do it is simply the one that does not get funded.

Which means when the Benefits Realisation Manager sits down two years later to put actual value next to forecast value, they are not measuring the programme. They are measuring the bid. A good part of the gap they find was already there on day one, before anybody wrote a line of code, and it is the size of the exaggeration it took to win.

Everybody senior knows this. That is the actual reason the dashboard stays decorative. Not laziness about reporting. Making it non decorative would require someone to say out loud that the approved figure was never reachable, and that reopens the approval, and reopening the approval means naming who approved it.

Then there is the thing I cannot find on any of your four views.

A state that says stop.

The SRO gets "are we still on course to deliver the promised value". The Programme Manager gets whether outputs are genuinely enabling benefits. The Benefits Realisation Manager gets forecast, actual, disbenefits, risks and corrective actions. Corrective action assumes the benefit is recoverable. None of the four has a place to record the finding that this benefit is not going to arrive, no correction is going to get it, and the remaining budget would do more good somewhere else.

The project is a bet. An instrument that can report the bet going well and the bet going slowly, but has no state for the bet being lost, is not a measurement instrument. It is a progress bar.

So I would add one row to the Benefits Realisation Manager's view. Value still achievable if we spend the rest of the money here, sitting next to value achievable if we stop now and spend it elsewhere. That number is calculable. Almost nobody calculates it, because it occasionally comes out as stop, and no dashboard has ever been commissioned by a person who wanted that answer.

Bad news is data, but only if the instrument has somewhere to put it.

Get that row onto the page and the conversation changes on its own. Leave it off and four role specific views are just four different ways of not asking.
