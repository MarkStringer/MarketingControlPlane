I would keep every one of the four, and the second one especially. Assertiveness without empathy is aggression is the best one-line description of a certain kind of site manager I have read.

But I would quarrel with the definition, because I think it is doing less work than it could.

You say emotional intelligence determines whether you can lead people who don't have to follow you. From the projects end, I would put it differently. It determines whether people tell you things.

Think about the manager you describe. High assertiveness, low empathy, six points of gap or more. He does not get less information because the crew dislikes him. He gets less because the crew has done the arithmetic. Bringing him bad news on Tuesday has a known cost, and it is paid by whoever brings it. Letting him find out for himself on Thursday also has a cost, but it is paid later and by the project. Thursday wins. Every time.

The information does not go away. The pour is still going to slip. It just travels around him instead of through him, and he is the last person on site to know.

That is what I think your ranking experiment is measuring. Not that empathetic managers perform better, exactly. That managers who are told the truth early perform better, and empathy is what being told costs. Profitability, client retention and project outcomes are all downstream of how many weeks of warning you got.

Which is why I would add one thing to "empathy is not agreement". It is not comfort either. You can hear that the steel is late, hold the line on the schedule, and still be the person who was told first. The hearing is the whole trick. Holding the line is easy once you know what the line is.

Bad news is data. Empathy is the cost of collecting it.

So I would go further than "not soft". The aggressive manager is not the problem on that job. He is not even stupid. He is just working with less information than everyone else on the site, and he is the one who arranged that.
