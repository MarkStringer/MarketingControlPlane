# Replies drafted

- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-07-001-badewi-dashboard-has-no-stop.md` — grants the four role specific views and attacks what sits upstream of them. The target number was not an estimate, it was what got the money released, so comparing actual against forecast two years later measures the bid rather than the programme, and a good part of the gap was present on day one. The dashboard stays decorative not through laziness but because making it honest reopens the approval and names who approved it. Then the missing state: none of his four views has anywhere to record that a benefit is not going to arrive and the remaining budget is better spent elsewhere, because corrective action presumes recoverability. Proposes one added row, value still achievable if we continue against value achievable if we stop now. Uses "the project is a bet" and "bad news is data". Ends on the point that four role specific views are otherwise four ways of not asking.
- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-07-002-thompson-ownership-expires-before-measurement.md` — concedes the model is clean on paper and argues transparency and accountability do not separate in the room where the miss is discussed. When a benefit is missed the only question asked is when did we know, and the only function that can answer owns the measurement, so the accountability lands on whoever made it visible. Second and larger mechanism: benefits mature over eighteen months to three years, sponsors move faster, so ownership is vacant at exactly the moment the outcome becomes known. Ownership that expires before measurement is a signature. The PMO's fear is therefore well calibrated rather than a misunderstanding. Proposes a named benefit owner still in post at measurement, recorded at approval, with the measurement date in their objectives. Uses "bad news is data". Ends on the point that escalation without anyone to escalate to is a confession.
- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-07-003-kelly-integers-you-cannot-defend.md` — accepts the pairing advice and argues with the strict no duplicates rule. Nobody can defend why item seven beats item eight, so a strict ordering manufactures precision the group does not have, and unearned precision is trivially attacked when someone senior wants their item moved, after which the list records who has been in the building recently. Honest ties are harder to move because an attacker has to come at the defensible top six directly. Adds that what drops off the end drops off silently, since the list never says not happening, it says twenty three, and it returns ten months later with a sponsor attached. Uses "the project is a bet" and "point of view is worth 80 IQ points". Ends on the point that the accurate amount of pain is the amount worth sharing.
