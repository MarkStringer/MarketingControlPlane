# Replies drafted

- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-10-002-dunsmuir-report-written-to-be-unreadable.md` — concedes all three of his reasons and argues they are symptoms rather than causes. Starts from the register the report is written in, then the mechanism: once a lesson is in the file it is available, and the next project that hits the same wall is not unlucky, it is negligent with a document to prove it. So the document is drafted so as not to convict anyone, and it is unread because everybody already knows what was allowed into it. Then the lesson that never appears at all, since reviews are scoped to how well we played the hand and never to whether we should have been at the table, and the people who put us there commissioned the review. Argues his prescription of reflecting throughout raises the cost rather than lowering it, because mid-project the same finding threatens a live business case with a sponsor who has said the words in public. Answers his closing question directly: yes twice, and both times the document was ammunition for someone who already wanted to fire. Uses "bad news is data" and "the project is a bet". Ends on the point that an organisation which cannot make the true sentence survivable does not have a lessons learned problem, it has a working system for not finding out.
- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-10-003-knight-the-only-remedy-nobody-has-to-sign.md` — accepts all six causes and reorders them by who has to sign something. Each of the six is the residue of a decision taken above the engineers, so fixing any of them means a named senior person unmaking a decision they announced and defended, while "work harder" is the only remedy that leaves every existing decision standing. That is why it survives being wrong. Second mechanism, the effort theory cannot be tested: ship and effort worked, miss and they did not try hard enough, so no result counts against it. Then the reframe, swapping "are they working hard enough" for "how many things is this team allowed to be doing at once and who added the last one", which turns the answer into a number with a person attached, which is exactly why the swap does not get made. Uses "point of view is worth 80 IQ points". Ends on the point that arguing the engineers do work hard concedes that effort was ever the subject.

# Notes

The thing that found it was not a new engine, it was a different query shape: quoting an argumentative fragment ("nobody reads") instead of a topic noun ("lessons learned"). Topic nouns return explainers because explainers are written to rank for topic nouns. Opinion fragments return opinions.
