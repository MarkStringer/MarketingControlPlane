# Replies drafted

- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-13-001-graham-the-escalations-that-do-not-qualify.md` — concedes escalation as a feature and the rate-limit framing, then argues the definition attached to it is doing more work than it looks. Two peers who both agree they are stuck, both want it resolved, and walk in together describes the cheap escalation, and that one was never being suppressed. The expensive ones fail her test structurally: the other party disputes that there is a problem at all, so going together is unavailable because it requires them to co-sign a description they reject, which leaves only the move she has defined as tattling; or the blocker is the tie-breaker, or the tie-breaker's own idea; or the disagreement is that the plan is wrong, which means somebody senior was wrong in public months ago. Second half rejects willingness as an input. People escalate as fast as it is safe to, the price is set by what visibly happened to the last person who went in alone and whether they are still on the team, everyone knows that number and nobody has written it down. Asking for courage is asking for a favour, changing the price is designing a system, and only one of those survives your departure. Uses "bad news is data" with the survives-contact qualifier. Ends on the metric to watch, which is not how often people escalate but how many things get raised by one person alone and what happens to them, because the second number sets the first one next quarter.
- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-13-002-attieh-the-ambiguity-was-the-settlement.md` — concedes "between whom" as the right question and the copy-everyone mechanism as real, then pushes on why the ownership is unclear. In her post it reads as an oversight fixable with a better document. The addition is that the vagueness is usually a negotiated outcome: two directors wanted different things, neither could win outright, and the form of words got signed precisely because it did not say who decides. The ambiguity is not a hole in the governance, it is what let the work start, which is why clarifying it means reopening the argument the vagueness settled with a loser this time, and why the RACI gets signed and not used. Smaller point that governance is a communication initiative with a firmer noun and an org chart attached, which is why it gets approved when the workshop does not. Then a cheap falsifiable test: count the recipients on the same class of email three months later, and if the list has not shortened, the document described an authority nobody transferred and the people copying six extra names have read the room correctly. Ends on the CC list as a free, continuously updated, unusually honest map of the decisions the organisation has not made, with "bad news is data, and so is an email header".
- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-13-003-gupta-price-the-default.md` — concedes the opening five ticks as the best thing in the post and then uses them against the remedy. They establish that every clarity input was present and nothing moved, so the missing ingredient was not clarity, and four of her five prescribed questions are about making the problem more legible. The fifth, what happens if no decision is made, is doing all the work. Names the mechanism: deciding costs somebody something, not deciding costs nothing, nobody minutes the meeting where a decision did not get made, and a crisper ask makes a refusal legible, which is a positive reason for the room to prefer the vague version. The fix is removing the free option, replacing "we need a decision on A or B" with a dated default that happens by itself, which usually produces an answer inside a day and often not A. Then the reason PMs avoid it, which is not laziness: a dated default puts the author on the hook, and if the date passes and nothing bad happens then the consequence was not real and they were wrong in front of the audience they were trying to impress. Uses "the project is a bet" on that point, that the bet exists either way and the only question is whether it is written where it can be checked. Ends on "bad news is data, but only when it is dated", an undated risk being a conversation and a dated one a decision that happens whether or not anyone turns up.

# Notes

- **Method finding, and the useful one from this run: LinkedIn activity IDs are timestamps and can be decoded offline.** The numeric part of `activity-7487776791453974528` is a Snowflake-style ID whose upper bits are milliseconds since the Unix epoch. `datetime.fromtimestamp((activity_id >> 22) / 1000)` returns the post date to within a day. This was checked against LinkedIn's own relative timestamp on all five posts fetched today and matched every time: 2 weeks against 2026-07-28, 3 months against 2026-05-12, 8 months against 2025-11-20, 1 year against 2025-04-24, 9 months against 2025-10. This matters because it makes freshness filtering free. Search results can be sorted by true date from the URL alone, before spending a fetch, and every run so far has been fetching posts blind and discovering the age afterwards. Recommend this goes into the search-routing memory and gets used as the first filter on every result set from now on.
- **Brave gave six clean queries and 429'd on the seventh**, which sits between the five-query rule of thumb and the unlimited run on 2026-08-12. Treat six as the working budget and spend the queries on fragments, not topic nouns.
- **The negated-premise fragment held up as the lead technique**, which is now two runs in a row. All three selections came from fragment queries on Brave. But it pulls hard from outside project management: the communication-problem query returned a remote-work post and a dialogue post before it returned anything about delivery, and the plan query returned an HBR video reshared eight times.
