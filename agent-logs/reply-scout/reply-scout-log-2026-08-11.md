**WebSearch is not uniformly stale, and the query shape is the reason.** Every previous log records WebSearch returning the same four stale results for the bare `site:linkedin.com/posts "project management"` query, and that is still true. But WebSearch indexes a LinkedIn post under its opening line, because that is what LinkedIn puts in the page title. Querying `site:linkedin.com/posts` plus a quoted **opening line fragment** returns live posts, including posts from 2026. `"The hardest part of project management isn't"` returned the Hudson post with its first two sentences in the result title. `"Most projects don't fail because"` and `"scope creep isn't"` both returned real posts. This is an extension of the 2026-08-10 finding about argumentative fragments: the fragment needs to be positioned where a post *opens*, not merely somewhere in the body. Two of today's three selections came this way, after Brave and DuckDuckGo were both dead.

Ages below are LinkedIn's own relative timestamps, confirmed by direct fetch of each post. No post from inside the 24 hour window was reachable today. Freshest was 1mo.

# Posts considered

## Selected

- **Jon Selvaraj** — "The most dangerous status a project can have is green." (**1mo**, 7 reactions, 3 comments) — SELECTED. Freshest post reached this run and the strongest argument of the day. Real mechanism rather than sentiment: green is the safe answer, so people hold it until the evidence is impossible to ignore, which is exactly when the fix is expensive. He names three leading indicators and prescribes making yellow safe to say. Low engagement, selected on argument quality. The non-obvious addition is that a status colour is not a measurement at all, it is a request, so it tracks the expected response rather than the state of the work, which is why it can stay green while everyone privately knows the date is gone. Second addition explains why his three leading indicators never appear on reports: each names a person above the PM who has not done something, and puts a date on how long that has been true, whereas a slipped date is an event with nobody's name on it. Raises the bar on his own prescription, since safe is usually read as nobody gets shouted at, when what amber actually needs is to buy something.
- **Keston Hudson** — "The Hardest Part of Project Management Isn't Planning… It's Alignment." (2mo, 61 reactions, 19 comments) — SELECTED, borderline on format because the six competing priorities are emoji bullets. Selected because the post rests on one load bearing causal claim, "communication creates alignment", and that claim is wrong in an interesting way. The reply concedes all six priorities as accurately observed and rational from where each function stands, then argues communication only fixes comprehension problems and this is not one: nobody in that room misunderstands anybody, they want incompatible things for good reasons and nothing said in the meeting changes what any of them is measured on. Second half is the part worth having, that an outcome statement is cheap to agree precisely because it does not say who loses when the outcomes collide, so the disagreement is preserved rather than resolved and returns at the first hard choice with everyone believing it was settled. Closes on why the word alignment is popular: it converts a decision a senior person owes into a communication task that lands on the PM, who has authority over none of the six.
- **Justin J. MacBale** — "Scope creep isn't a red flag. It's a decision point." (8mo, 60 reactions, 89 comments) — SELECTED. Highest comment to reaction ratio surfaced in weeks, and a genuine taxonomy of good and bad scope creep rather than a complaint. The addition is the asymmetry underneath the taxonomy: original scope had to compete in a funding round and beat rivals for the same money, while a change request arrives after the money is committed and competes against nothing, so the test quietly degrades from is this better than what we are already doing to is this worth doing. Almost everything passes the second test. Proposes a different axis, what came out and who agreed to lose it by name, and notes good and bad are mostly assigned retrospectively depending on whether the project landed.

# Notes

- **Method finding, and the most useful thing in this log.** WebSearch works for LinkedIn post discovery if the query quotes an **opening line** fragment rather than a body fragment or a topic noun. LinkedIn titles each post page with its first line, so opening line fragments hit the title field. `"The hardest part of project management isn't"` and `"scope creep isn't"` both returned live 2026 posts with usable snippets, from the same tool that returns a six week old stale set for the bare query. This matters because it does not rate limit and does not CAPTCHA, unlike both search engines this agent has depended on. Recommend the next run leads with WebSearch and a list of opening line stems: "Most projects don't fail because", "The hardest part of X isn't", "Nobody tells you that", "X isn't a Y, it's a Z", "Stop treating X like", "The real cost of X isn't". The `X isn't a Y, it's a Z` construction is the highest yield of these, since it guarantees the post contains an argument rather than a definition.
- Generic openers cut the other way. `"nobody wants to say"` is a LinkedIn opener across every topic on the platform, so it returned designers, recruiters, crypto and Singapore hiring, and cost two Brave queries and two post fetches to clear. Pair opener stems with a domain word, or use stems that only a PM would write.
- Brave gave five clean queries then a hard 429 that did not recover across three attempts spread over the run. That is the second consecutive run with exactly this pattern, so five is a reliable budget and the rolling window is longer than the ten minutes previously guessed. DuckDuckGo was worse than last run, CAPTCHA on all four attempts across both hosts, so the 2026-08-10 advice to spend the first DuckDuckGo query on the highest value search could not even be executed. Neither engine can be relied on for a specific query landing.
