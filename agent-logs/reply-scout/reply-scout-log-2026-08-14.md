# Replies drafted

- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-14-001-rachitsky-correctness-is-not-the-currency.md` — concedes that nobody reporting to you is the accurate part, then points out that every success factor the post lists, know the market, know the competition, be clear with engineering, integrate it all, is a claim about being right. Correctness is not what transfers in a room where you cannot fire anyone. What transfers is whether people think you will still be here and still saying this when the bill arrives. Then turns on the closing line, that what matters is that the product wins: the result arrives a year and a half after the decisions that caused it, by which time the room has rewritten who said what, so a win means everyone was aligned from the start and a loss means you were the one who pushed for it. That asymmetry falls hardest on the person with no formal power. Uses "the project is a bet" to argue the move is not becoming more persuasive but writing the bet down while it is still a bet, with a date and an agreed test for being wrong, because that survives people going off you and influence does not. Ends on the observation that the advice comes from someone who could fire people, and that the hard part of having no authority was never knowing what to do.
- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-14-002-igg-bau-is-the-shock-absorber.md` — puts two items from the post's own list next to each other, protecting BAU is essential and open and honest conversations early, and shows they cannot both hold. Ring fencing BAU is not a risk statement, it is an allocation decision already taken, so when resource runs short the project timeline absorbs everything and the honest conversation is settled before it starts. What is left to discuss is not whether the project slips but who says so and how early. Second half turns the gatekeeper line back on itself: a gate is a filter, and one person cannot both decide what gets through and guarantee that things are raised early, because people learn what a gate passes faster than anyone writes a policy about it, and the price of raising an issue ends up set by the people who most need it raised. Uses "bad news is data" with the qualifier that data only moves when the holder is not also the person who pays for handing it over. Closes on the duller remedy, which is to say out loud that the project timeline is the shock absorber and replan against it, accepting a later date that can still be defended in twelve months. Ends on "deliver the possible, not the fantasy", where the fantasy is not the deadline but the belief that candour can close a gap that policy created.
