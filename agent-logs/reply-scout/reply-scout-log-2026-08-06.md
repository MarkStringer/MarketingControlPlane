## Selected

- **Patrick Giwa, PhD** — "70% of software projects fail because of bad requirements. Not bad developers. Not bad tools" (~11 months, 176 reactions, 57 comments) — SELECTED. Best post of the run by a distance, and the only one with real engagement. It carries an explicit, falsifiable causal claim and rules out two rival causes by name, which makes it argue-able rather than agree-able. Partly a list post (ten questions in five categories) but the list is downstream of the claim, and the reply engages the claim, not the list. The non-obvious addition is that every one of his ten questions has an honest answer that already exists in the room and cannot safely be said out loud, so the failure is not in the gathering technique but in what the organisation permits on the record.
- **Aurora Hemming** — "Unpopular Opinion Alert: It's NOT a lack of strategy alignment that's causing projects to fail, it's a lack of accountability" (~12 months, 11 reactions, 6 comments) — SELECTED. Low engagement, selected on the strength of the argument. She makes a comparative causal claim, names the rival explanation, rejects it, and reaches a conclusion. The reply runs a test she has not run: ask who has to change if each diagnosis is true. Alignment sends the bill upstairs, accountability sends it downwards and sideways, so accountability is the popular diagnosis rather than the unpopular one. Her own examples (sponsors missing decisions, priorities shifting, late feedback) are all exercises of power by people senior to the project manager, which cuts against her conclusion.
- **Queen Agada, CBAP, CMC** — "There's always one stakeholder everyone calls 'difficult'" (~5 months, 12 reactions, 1 comment) — SELECTED, borderline, and flagged as such in the candidate's `notes:` field. She already arrives at "resistance is often rational", which is close to ground the queue has covered in `zepernick-resistance-is-data`, `stramb-silence-is-rational` and `worsley-silence-is-calculation`, and a reply that restated it would be agreement. It survives because there is a step past her conclusion: the delay was the only reporting channel the person had, the accurate pricing of the design existed on day one, and "difficult" is the label an organisation uses on a report it does not want to cost. That is a mechanism argument about missing channels rather than another restatement that resistance carries information.

# Replies drafted

- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-06-001-giwa-requirements-nobody-allowed-to-say.md` — bad requirements is the diagnosis that is always available after the fact, because you can only learn which requirement was wrong by failing, and it exonerates everyone present. Walks three of his ten questions and shows the honest answer to each is unsayable in front of a director: the date and why it is fixed, that nobody actually wants this and it is an audit response, that the last two attempts failed and two people in the room were on them. The answers were in the room and did not reach the page, and not because nobody asked. Proposes an eleventh question asked of the room rather than the project: what does the person who answers honestly have to lose. Uses "bad news is data". Ends on the point that the missing answer is the project.
- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-06-002-hemming-who-gets-the-bill.md` — applies a test to competing failure diagnoses: who has to change if this is true. Alignment sends the bill upstairs and requires someone senior to admit two priorities were incompatible; accountability sends it downwards and sideways and can be adopted without anyone powerful doing anything, which is why it is the popular diagnosis, not the unpopular one. Her own three examples are all exercises of superior power, not character defects. The ownership script she wants works sideways and downwards and is a career event upwards. Accountability is a bet on the organisation, and blame-shifting is an accurate weather report. Uses "bad news is data" and "the project is a bet". Ends on the point that what happened to the last person who owned a serious miss in public is the real accountability policy and everything else is a poster.
- `queue/reply-candidates/reply-candidate-2026-08-06-003-agada-delay-was-the-report.md` — names the mechanism she describes but does not examine. The stakeholder had no field on the plan, no RAID line and no slide for "this transfers risk to my team uncompensated", so they used the one instrument they controlled, the speed of their own signature. The delay was the report, accurate and well calibrated, delivered through the only open channel, and then read as a personality trait. The one person who priced the design correctly on day one was the one standing where the cost would land, and everyone with a better title had a worse view. Notes the uncomfortable part: being difficult worked, and it was the only functioning risk process available. Uses "point of view is worth 80 IQ points". Ends on the point that until the question has a home in the paperwork, the difficult stakeholder is your risk register.
