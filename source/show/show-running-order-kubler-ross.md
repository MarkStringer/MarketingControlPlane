# Running Order — You Can Write a Book
*Developed in conversation, 2026-05-24. Structured around the five stages of Kübler-Ross.*

---

## Opening — Death
*On Death and Dying* — Elisabeth Kübler-Ross

Your boss. Cornwall. The ambulance too far away. You don't get a countdown.
Uncle Jo. Room 51. The scraping sound. Dead in a pool of his own piss, bags packed.
*"I have been in many rooms, but I do not think I am yet on the lavatory floor."*

---

## 1. Denial
*Passages* — Gail Sheehy

I'm busy. I'm having a career / getting married / having children / just been diagnosed.
There's always another passage. The quiet moment never arrives.

---

## 2. Anger
*Bluebeard* — Kurt Vonnegut

The frustrated artist who only gets out sideways — drunk, dancing on the table, buried again the next morning.
Jeremy Clarkson and the pencils. The professional footballer's book. My book ranks 60,000th on Amazon. Which means it exists.
The spectrum: Rabo Karabekian kept it inside and made a secret masterpiece. Hitler kept it inside and made the twentieth century.
Jung: the unlived life of the parent — and the unlived life of the failed artist.

---

## 3. Bargaining
*The Hound of Heaven* — Francis Thompson

Buying guitars. Expensive notebooks. Writers retreats. Creative writing courses.
The diary as a sealed room — Stephen King's closed door. You can fool yourself for 50 years.
All of it is running. The hound catches you anyway.

---

## 4. Depression
*The Hound of Heaven* (continued) — Francis Thompson

You've run out of excuses. Money — neither do most writers. Time — 20 minutes a day is enough. Knowledge — read, or make it up.
The moment you stop running and still don't start.
Isherwood showed Auden the draft. Never recovered from the look on his face. Wrote *Goodbye to Berlin* anyway.

---

## 5. Acceptance
*Zen in the Art of Archery* — Eugen Herrigel / *Bird by Bird* — Anne Lamott

Carry water, chop wood. Write something. Read it. Change it. Show it to someone. Change it. Keep writing.
Remain comfortable in the state of highest tension.
The closed door and the open door are the same thing — showing up.
The arrow releases itself. But only if you keep drawing the bow.

---

## Close — The Dangerous Thing
*The War of Art* — Steven Pressfield
*Travels with My Aunt* — Graham Greene

Scarborough. The big outdoor pool. Three and a half years old, terrified. Playing it safe in the footbath.
Your dad picked you up and put you in the big pool. But your dad can't do that. No one can do that.
You have to pick yourself up and put yourself in.

Opening the door is dangerous. The safe life ends there.
T.S. Eliot — no longer at ease with the old dispensation.
Henry Pulling goes home changed. Bilbo goes home changed. You can't unsee the 52 rooms.
A writing life is a decision not to have an easy life. Easy in at least one way.
*You write the book because you're not on the lavatory floor yet.*
