# Clip candidates — closing night, 29 August 2026

Self-contained 45 to 120 second passages from the last night of the Fringe 2026 run, when
nobody came and Mark performed the whole show to an empty room and recorded it.

Two source files, neither in the repo:

- **A** = `~/Downloads/20260829_182537.mp4` — the show, 48m 37s
- **B** = `~/Downloads/20260829_191832.mp4` — piece to camera afterwards, 1m 43s

Both are portrait phone video, 1080x1920 after rotation, shot close. The show one is lit
magenta by the stage lights in a dark room; the piece to camera is outdoors in daylight.
It is a face filling the frame rather than a wide of a man on a stage, which suits a phone
feed and means 9:16 needs no crop at all.

Timecodes are the in and out points actually used, and they live in `CLIPS` in
`scripts/closing-night/make_clips.py`. Quoted wording is from the corrected transcript.

---

## Top picks

### 1. It finally happened
**B — 00:00 to 01:43 (103s), or 00:00 to 00:52 (52s)**

The whole of the piece to camera. "So it finally happened. I got no people in for a show,
which I think is the first time this has happened in three years of doing the Fringe... The
thing that I did was, I did the entire show anyway. I didn't stop for the writing breaks,
but I just talked about all the things beginning with S, and then I heckle myself at the
end, which is demented."

This is already shot as a short and he says so at the end of it. It needs no edit beyond a
trim. If it goes out at 52 seconds, end on the beer or the fish and chips.

The 103 second version carries the better ending: talking about a book is part of how he
gets it made.

### 2. The empty room is the point
**A — 21:33 to 23:09 (96s)**

Emotional labour, then the turn onto tonight. "When you do something new, when you do
something different, you experience a bunch of emotions, and they may not all be positive...
if you avoid the thing that generated the negative emotions, then you'll just give up. And
you know, that's fitting for tonight, really, isn't it? I've got nobody. And I could pretend
I'm not upset about it, but I am a little bit upset about it. But if I just give up because
this happened, then I've given up."

The strongest thing in the recording. It is the show's actual argument being tested live on
the man making it, and he says the quiet part out loud. Ends clean on "you've given yourself
some emotional work, then you have to do it."

### 3. I'm stuck again
**A — 08:25 to 09:14 (49s)**

Holds up the STUCK card. "This is the whole point of the show, that I wrote the bulk of a
book and then I got stuck. And then last week, when I was walking around after the show, I
realised, oh my God, I'm stuck again, because I'm trying to write a mystery story at the
moment and I'm stuck. And so I realised that part of the reason I'm doing this show and part
of the reason I'm talking about being stuck is because I am stuck, and I'm using this as a
way to get unstuck."

Short, complete, and it undercuts any suggestion the show is being delivered from a position
of having solved it.

### 4. Stay in the pool
**A — 42:53 to 44:24 (91s)**

Colm Toibin on swimming, via the Facebook algorithm. "The whole business of writing is
staying in the pool, staying in the pool, doing the swimming. And that's obviously what I
completely failed to do when I wrote my 20,000 words. I've now got a 35,000 word mystery
story. And I need to stay in the pool. I need to not just paddle around in the shallow end."

Opens on "maybe hiring a venue and having nobody in it is a bit of a weird thing to do",
which earns the rest of it. New material, not in the earlier recordings.

### 5. Why should I listen to you?
**A — 47:19 to 48:37 (78s)**

The last self-heckle and the end of the show. "We're very well designed to learn fastest
from somebody who's about 18 months advanced from us... And I've only written one book, so I
don't know lots of things about it. I know I've got a lot to learn. I am determined to write
this second book. So if you haven't written a book, maybe it's worth you listening to me...
Nobody has done here tonight in Edinburgh, but I'm going to post this online and I'm going
to keep going."

Ends on "thank you very much, good night" to an empty room. If you post a series, this is
the closer. Tack the polar bear joke on the front (46:53) if you want the laugh first.

## Also strong

### 6. To use a technical term
**A — 02:14 to 03:26 (72s)**

"There is this idea that nobody needs to write books any more, now that we've got AI, AI
will write the books for us. And I think that is, to use a technical term here, bullshit.
And when I say technical term, I mean a technical term. Bullshit is something where the
people who are telling you don't care whether it's true or not." Then who benefits: the AI
companies, the bosses, and why they want writers scared.

The most quotable minute in the recording and the most likely to travel. Says bullshit four
times, so it is a judgement call whether that is the register you want.

### 7. A tenth of an arse
**A — 10:25 to 12:14 (109s)**

The slush pile reader, then the good bit. "They weren't half-arsed, they were like a tenth
of an arse." Ends somewhere better than the August 22 version did: "in a first draft, none
of that is right. But it's important to understand that in a first draft, there is
something. You've created something. You've done something. And that is a good thing.
That's a thing that is not shitty."

### 8. I sulked for six years
**A — 13:31 to 15:28 (117s)**

The Metropolitan Line, 20,000 words in a month, and the Hollywood montage with the seasons
changing behind the typewriter. "I sulked for six years. I didn't essentially touch this
book for six years." This version goes further than the earlier ones: "if I hadn't sulked,
if I'd carried on and persevered, I could have been through that book and maybe on my next
book, or maybe on my third book by now."

Long. Trim the commute-length digression at the front if you need it under 100.

### 9. You don't have to start at the beginning
**A — 16:11 to 17:56 (105s)**

How to Read a Book, the war bits of War and Peace, and then the forehead slap: the same is
true of writing. "You don't have to start at the beginning and go through to the end. You
can start in the middle and then tunnel out to both ends."

The most straightforwardly useful clip here. It is advice someone can act on this evening.

### 10. You are in the club
**A — 29:17 to 30:16 (59s)**

Split infinitives, but the version without the swearing. "Split infinitive comes from Latin,
because in Latin infinitives are one word so you can't split them. But English isn't Latin.
It seems fine to me to say to boldly go... What this is doing is telling you that you're not
in the club and you're not allowed to write. And you are in the club. If you can speak the
language you can write the language, and other people shouldn't be telling you that you
can't."

Better than the August 22 split infinitive clip and cleaner. Ends on the line that matters.

### 11. Skull, not crossbones
**A — 33:24 to 34:34 (70s)**

The pirate ship chapter, the icon he asked for and the icon he got, then the point: "once
you've got to a certain point when you're publishing a book, you lose control. You don't
have complete control over it. And you could throw a complete paddy about that, but that's
probably not a smart idea. And you certainly shouldn't get stuck on it."

Lands on the contract punchline: a month of arguing, and the only thing he changed was the
date format under the signature from American to British, "which is not that big a victory
if truth be told."

### 12. You're not Paul McCartney
**A — 34:43 to 36:00 (77s)**

The guitar lessons story again, and this telling has the better ending. "Paul McCartney is a
genius, you're not a genius... And this sounds ridiculous, but this is what happened to me
when I wrote my 20,000 words. I discovered that I wasn't a genius. I should have known
earlier than that. And then when I discovered that I wasn't a genius, I sulked."

Contains a small aside where he panics about quicker versus more quickly, which is charming
and worth keeping.

### 13. How do you improve your scene?
**A — 36:29 to 38:14 (105s)**

Brian Eno, Liverpool in the sixties, and then the domestic version: four hours before work
because of the time difference with Greece, and a stranger to swap chapters with. Ends on
Virginia Woolf at Girton telling a room of clever women they need money and a room of their
own. "Brutal but true, right? So how do you improve your scene so you can actually do some
writing?"

### 14. Talk your way out of it
**A — 38:44 to 40:33 (109s)**

Fringe 2023, no venue, three half-hour shows a day on the Meadows for a week, then the
friend with COVID hands over his slot and an audience expecting topical comedy gets an hour
on project management. "I said, look, I'm going to do a show about project management, it's
not going to be topical comedy, if you don't want to stay then that's fine, I completely
understand. But everybody stayed."

### 15. Come in the bathroom
**A — 41:26 to 42:53 (87s)**

The bankrupt polar explorer who blames John Mills for it, gets asked round for tea, and
leaves with an after-dinner career. "John Mills said, no, come in the bathroom." Cleanest
telling of the three recordings and there is no audience noise over it.

### 16. Put it all in one place
**A — 26:59 to 28:26 (87s)**

The simplest advice in the show and he knows it. "I got stuck for six years, but I still
managed to pick up what I'd written, because I was reasonably good at putting everything
that I've written in one place." Then the diaries, which were not all in one place, and
which gave him the insight about the six-year sulk once they were.

### 17. A body under the patio
**A — 24:01 to 25:14 (73s)**

The novelist's answer to a complicated question about heightening language: tell me a story.
Then the list, ending "if the police find a body under your patio, then you have to figure
out a story, right? You have to keep a story straight."

Same beat as the August 22 version. Use whichever reads better on the night, but this one
has no audience laugh to cut around.

## Handle with care

**Content for AI to steal.** A — 44:28 to 45:52 (84s). Explains the heckle cards, then
answers the AI heckle via Cory Doctorow: "they want you to be scared, they want you to
despair. Don't be scared, don't despair. Or be scared and do it anyway." Good material, but
the transcript shows a stretch of hesitation around 45:00 to 45:18 that will need listening
to before you commit to it.

**Boo.** A — 45:52 to 46:35 (43s). "Not everybody's going to like what you do, but you
should do it anyway." He then gives the Scottish version and says on tape that he shouldn't,
because it will upset the algorithms. Either cut before it or don't use the clip.

**Grammar for people who aren't twats.** A — 30:49 to 31:56 (67s). Announces next year's show
idea, plus Muphry's Law. Contains the word in the title, repeatedly, which is the whole joke.

**Fur coat, no knickers.** A — 46:53 to 47:18 (25s). Too short to stand alone. Put it on the
front of clip 5.

## What the footage looks like

The 4:5 versions are not cropped. The phone was held close and his face fills the frame, so a
crop would lose either the top of his head or his chin. They sit the whole portrait frame
inside the 4:5 canvas over a blurred copy of itself instead.

The stage lighting is strongly magenta and has been left as it was. Grading it out is a
judgement call, not a fix, and it is what the room looked like. Say the word and the pipeline
can do it in one pass.
