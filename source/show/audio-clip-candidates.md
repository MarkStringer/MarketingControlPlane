# Audio clip candidates — You Can Write a Book, Fringe 2026

Self-contained 45–90 second passages from the live transcripts, suitable for posting as an
audio clip over a still image.

Timecodes are the start times of the Whisper segments at each boundary, so the out point is
approximate — add three or four seconds to catch the end of the final sentence. Verify against
the audio before cutting. Transcripts are uncorrected machine output; the wording quoted below
is indicative, not exact.

Source audio lives in `~/Downloads/`, not in the repo.

---

## Top picks

### 1. You're not Paul McCartney
**`Voice 260822_181740.m4a` — 44:00 to 45:34 (~90s)**

The friend with a PhD in chemistry who won't take guitar lessons because Paul McCartney can't
read music. Ends on "you're not Paul McCartney, you're not a genius... that is just a recipe
for misery," then turns it onto literary heroes.

Best clip in the run. It is a complete story with a setup, a turn and a punchline, it needs no
context, and the point lands on writing without having to say so. Trim the vinyl-collection
aside around 44:14 if you need to get under 90 seconds.

*Image:* a guitar, or the book cover. Something plain enough that the story does the work.

---

### 2. The Hollywood montage and the six-year sulk
**`Voice 260822_181740.m4a` — 11:01 to 12:00 (~60s)**

Twenty thousand words on the Metropolitan Line in May 2016, then nothing to do with them.
"I'd fallen for a kind of Hollywood idea of what writing a book is... there's a montage and
the seasons change around them and the manuscript gets higher and higher. And so what I did
was I sulked for six years."

This is the spine of the whole show in one minute. If only one clip goes out, it is this or
the McCartney one.

*Image:* a Tube carriage, or the Metropolitan Line roundel.

---

### 3. Books left on the Victoria line
**`Voice 260824_181409.m4a` — 42:26 to 43:34 (~68s)**

The earlier version of the show involved a suitcase of books. He left the suitcase on the
Underground. "Some part of me decided that that wasn't the way to do this show and left those
books on the Victoria line... were they destroyed in the controlled explosion? I don't know."

Funny, self-deprecating, and it is doing real work about the subconscious rather than just
being an anecdote. Strong standalone.

*Image:* an empty Tube seat, or a suitcase.

---

### 4. The slush pile
**`Voice 260822_181740.m4a` — 08:00 to 09:03 (~63s)**

The man who volunteered to read a publisher's unsolicited manuscripts, a hundred of them in a
year. The difference between the ones worth publishing and the ones that weren't was not the
prose. They were the right length but they weren't finished. "They were half-assed, or they
were like seven-eighths-assed, but they weren't finished."

Useful to anyone who has a draft sitting somewhere, which is the audience.

*Image:* a stack of paper, or the notebook and pen from the show.

## Also strong

### 5. Come in the bathroom
**`Voice 260824_181409.m4a` — 28:14 to 29:41 (~87s)**

The bankrupt polar explorer who writes an angry letter to John Mills blaming him for it, gets
invited round for tea, and comes away with an after-dinner speaking career built in an
afternoon in the bathroom. "You're going to have to talk your way out of it."

The 25th's version (29:32 onward) is warmer but has the audience member in it.

*Image:* something Antarctic, or a bathroom door.

### 6. Talking about it in a field
**`Voice 260824_181409.m4a` — 30:17 to 31:19 (~62s)**

Fringe 2023, no venue and no show, so he gave the talk on the Meadows two or three times an
afternoon for a week. Then a friend got COVID and handed over his slot, and an audience
expecting topical comedy got an hour on project management instead. Nobody left. Then he got
COVID and spent the next week in bed.

Pairs naturally with clip 5 and ends on a laugh.

*Image:* the Meadows, or the 2023 flyer if there is one.

### 7. Tell me a story
**`Voice 260822_181740.m4a` — 33:46 to 34:53 (~67s)**

The published author's answer to a convoluted question about heightened language: tell me a
story. Then the list — stories down the pub, stories to your partner about work, stories to
your parents when you were a teenager, "if the police find a body under our patio, we have to
tell some kind of story."

The patio line is the hook. Consider leading the post with it.

*Image:* a patio. Genuinely.

### 8. Emotional labour is work
**`Voice 260822_181740.m4a` — 40:31 to 41:51 (~80s)**

Arlie Russell Hochschild, then air crew being pleasant to obnoxious passengers, doctors being
cheerful with terrible news, lawyers not laughing at their clients. "Responding to emotions is
work... when you start to write a book you send yourself on this emotional journey."

The one clip here that speaks straight to the project management audience as well as the
writing one.

*Image:* an aeroplane cabin, or keep it typographic.

### 9. Skull, not crossbones
**`Voice 260822_181740.m4a` — 49:07 to 50:06 (~59s)**

The martini principle, then the cover fight: he sent the publisher a skull and crossbones for
the chapter about pirate ships and they used just the skull. "I can't figure out whether
that's more badass or just annoying. But the point I'm making is that you get to a point where
you lose control."

*Image:* the book cover, cropped tight on the skull icon.

### 10. We Englished our way through it
**`Voice 260824_181409.m4a` — 51:05 to 51:51 (~46s)**

Four years of swapping chapters every Saturday with someone he had met once. "His wife died
and then he had a stroke and this was all the way through the pandemic. So we kind of Englished
our way through it. Hello, how are you? Oh, terrible. Okay, well then shall we look at the
draft?"

The shortest of these and the one with the most in it. Two hours a week changed everything.

*Image:* two coffee cups, or nothing — a plain card with the last line.

### 11. Don't ask your nearest and dearest
**`Voice 260824_181409.m4a` — 51:51 to 52:39 (~48s)**

Why the people who love you are the wrong readers for an unfinished draft. "They're used to
reading finished stuff. They don't have any kind of handle on reading stuff that's not
finished."

Follows directly on from clip 10 in the recording, so the two can be cut as one 95-second piece
if you don't mind going slightly long.

### 12. The smile opening the box
**`Voice 260824_181409.m4a` — 59:27 to 60:20 (~53s)**

Answering the "writing is just content for AI to steal" heckle. Ends with someone from
university liking a video of him opening the box of author copies: "the smile on my face when
I'm opening a box with books in it, I haven't felt that good in a long time."

The natural closer if you post a series.

*Image:* a frame from the box-opening video, if you still have it.

## Handle with care

**The one-person show, opening.** `Voice 260825_182031.m4a` — 00:00 to 01:07 (~67s). "Did you
want me to go away and you can go home and have a night off?" The audience member's reply is
what makes it. Pairs with the existing post candidate, but it is their voice as well as yours,
so ask first.

**Split infinitive.** `Voice 260822_181740.m4a` — 20:53 to 22:04 (~71s). Ends on "and those
people are dicks, mainly." Good clip, mild swearing.

**The tech rehearsal.** `Voice 260824_181409.m4a` — 56:32 to 57:26 (~54s). The night before the
first Fringe show, no idea what a tech rehearsal was, briefly considered not doing the show at
all. Contains one f-word at 56:44 that would need cutting or bleeping.

## Cutting them

```
ffmpeg -ss 44:00 -to 45:34 -i "~/Downloads/Voice 260822_181740.m4a" -c copy clip.m4a
```

For audio over a still, LinkedIn wants video, so:

```
ffmpeg -loop 1 -i image.jpg -ss 44:00 -to 45:34 -i "~/Downloads/Voice 260822_181740.m4a" \
  -c:v libx264 -tune stillimage -c:a aac -b:a 192k -pix_fmt yuv420p -shortest clip.mp4
```

Square (1080x1080) or 4:5 crops read better in the feed than 16:9.
