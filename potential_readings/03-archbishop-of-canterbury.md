# The Archbishop of Canterbury Is Alive and Well

*Source: Chapter Bet, lines 5355–5430*
*Estimated reading time: ~2 minutes*

---

Here's a joke. Bob is on holiday in Rome. He visits the Vatican. Through a series of unlikely accidents, he discovers that the pope is dead. But it's Easter weekend and the church is covering up the big news until after the weekend's celebrations. Bob tells his friend Dave to put a big bet on at the bookies for the pope to die. It's a sure thing. Bob and Dave agree that they'll split the winnings! The Tuesday after Easter, the Vatican makes the announcement, which comes as no surprise to Bob and Dave. The pope is dead.

Bob goes round to Dave's to get his share of the winnings. Dave looks miserable.

"Sorry Bob. We lost the bet."

"What?" says Bob. "How could we have lost the bet?"

"I got carried away," says Dave. "I put the money on an accumulator for the Archbishop of Canterbury to kick the bucket as well."

What's an "accumulator?" An accumulator is a bet on multiple horses. The winnings from one horse — if it wins — are bet on the next horse. A tiny bet accumulates into a large payoff. So, betting on the pope/archbishop double offered a payoff that Dave couldn't resist. Although of course the increase in the odds of it happening turned a dream bet into a certain loss.

For a long time, I'd noticed that some projects get foiled, their chances of success flattened by the addition of extra unnecessary risk. But I didn't have a way of explaining it to other people. Now, using betting as a way of seeing, I do. Some projects are bets. Some projects are risky bets. And then some projects — notionally they are bets — but they're like a bet that the pope, the poor old Archbishop of Canterbury AND the Dalai Lama will all shuffle off this mortal coil on the same Easter weekend. They aren't really bets, they are *nonsense accumulators.*
