# Surprise!

*Source: Chapter Escape, lines 2517–2565*
*Estimated reading time: ~2.5 minutes*

---

When we try to push software as far as we can out into the real world, we will be surprised. And that is because software is treated like magic.

Arthur C. Clarke, the science fiction author, said that sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic. Advanced technology isn't magic. But in many situations, it's indistinguishable from magic. And that's absolutely the case with software. People will react to your software in ways that you never imagined. They will do things with it that you didn't expect them to do.

They will react to software in a way that is completely different from the way they would react to a written specification.

You will find yourself thinking: What was that? What did she just do? Why did she do that? Users will put things together in ways that you didn't expect. They'll totally ignore or be unimpressed by parts of the software that your team struggled with and worked hard to get right. But at the same time, they'll get enthusiastic about parts of the software that you didn't think were a big deal.

Part of the point of pushing software as far as you can — pushing it out into a live environment — is to be surprised.

But of course, some of those surprises won't be pleasant. As you try to get the software out past the fences, you'll discover more obstacles. Often these obstacles will take the form of people who appear from nowhere. They'll tell you that you need to comply with a certain regulation.

Other people will tell you that you must pass an assessment of some sort, or that you must answer a questionnaire. Questionnaire, you say? That's no big deal. Oh, no? One of these questionnaires that I encountered was 500 pages long.

All those kinds of things will happen, and that is part of why you're trying to get the software as near live as you possibly can. By trying to promote software, you're aiming to be surprised. But that doesn't mean that the experience won't be unnerving. And it doesn't mean that a lot of people connected with the project won't get upset when the surprises arrive.
